# Phang vs. Blanche

2026-11-21 

## Core Legal Conflict

### Administrative Procedure Act vs. Statutory Compliance

- The litigation centers on whether the Department of Justice violated the Epstein Files Transparency Act by using automated discovery filters to redact vast amounts of public records

- Judge Emmet G. Sullivan previously ruled that Katie Phang has standing to sue under the Administrative Procedure Act because journalists suffer concrete informational and economic injuries when denied access to statutorily mandated disclosures

- The central statutory tension involves balancing the Act's mandate for maximum public disclosure against narrow exceptions for victim privacy and ongoing investigations

### Algorithmic Over-Redaction and the Privacy Act

- The Department of Justice relies on the Everlaw platform and a "First Level Review Protocol" to automatically strip personally identifiable information across millions of pages

- Applying blanket Privacy Act filters to Jeffrey Epstein's own email address highlights the flaw of uncalibrated automation, as deceased individuals do not hold standard Privacy Act protections and Epstein is the central subject of the statute

- Phang's legal team contends that human administrators configured the machine-learning parameters, meaning the government bears direct responsibility for "superficial compliance" and missed foreign-language translations

### Procedural Posture and Dismissal Strategy

- Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche is pursuing a motion for judgment on the pleadings to terminate the case before further administrative records or unredacted files must be compiled

- This defensive strategy aims to establish that good-faith deployment of industry-standard e-discovery tools satisfies the Department's legal obligations under tight congressional deadlines

## Next Steps Speculation

### Judicial Scrutiny of the Administrative Record

- Given Judge Sullivan's prior orders requiring in camera review of disputed documents and warnings regarding potential contempt proceedings, the court is unlikely to grant a complete dismissal on the pleadings

- The court will likely deny the Department's motion in whole or in part, forcing the government to produce a detailed administrative index justifying specific redaction categories

### Potential Appointment of a Special Master

- Because manual review of millions of pages exceeds standard judicial bandwidth, Judge Sullivan may grant Phang's earlier request to appoint an independent special master

- A special master would audit the Everlaw search parameters, recalibrate the machine-learning filters to exclude non-protected figures, and oversee targeted re-processing of foreign-language files

### Appellate and Legislative Escalation

- If ordered to unredact high-profile associates or overhaul its review protocol, the Department of Justice will likely seek an emergency stay and interlocutory appeal to the D.C. Circuit

- Concurrently, members of Congress who have already reviewed unredacted files in secure DOJ facilities may leverage these court admissions to hold oversight hearings or issue subpoenas

## Answers

### Redaction of Epstein's Email

- The Department of Justice programmed the Everlaw platform under its First Level Review Protocol to automatically redact all email addresses and personally identifiable information

- Government lawyers claimed this blanket filter was necessary to comply with the Privacy Act while meeting tight statutory deadlines

### Statutory Basis for Lawsuit

- Katie Phang is suing under the Administrative Procedure Act to enforce compliance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act

- Judge Sullivan ruled that the Administrative Procedure Act provides a valid cause of action because the transparency statute itself lacks an explicit private right of action

### Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings

- Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche filed the motion to dismiss Phang's claims based purely on the initial legal filings

- The government argues its automated review protocols are legally sufficient and seeks to avoid compiling further manual administrative records

### Potential Judicial Resolution

- Judge Sullivan may deny the dismissal motion and order the government to recalibrate its Everlaw search parameters

- The court could also appoint an independent special master to audit the redactions and oversee foreign-language file translations

### Non-Party Survivor Intervention

- A survivor identified as Jane Doe moved to intervene for the limited purpose of ensuring victim identities remain properly redacted

- Previous court filings noted that improper disclosures in earlier releases caused severe personal safety concerns for victims
