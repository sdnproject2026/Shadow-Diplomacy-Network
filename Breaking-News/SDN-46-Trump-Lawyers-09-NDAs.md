# Non-Prosecution Agreement

(NPA)

Jeffrey Epstein's 2007

## Status

## Executive Synopsis

- Jeffrey Epstein's September 24, 2007 federal Non-Prosecution Agreement remains one of the most controversial negotiated prosecutorial arrangements in modern American criminal justice.

- Negotiated by Epstein's defense team, including Alan Dershowitz, Jay Lefkowitz, Kenneth Starr, Roy Black, Gerald Lefcourt, and Jack Goldberger, the agreement provided Epstein with extraordinary protection from federal prosecution in the Southern District of Florida.

- The agreement also purported to protect four specifically identified individuals and any potential co-conspirators from federal prosecution.

- Epstein subsequently pleaded guilty to two Florida state felony offenses on June 30, 2008.

- The NPA was never judicially rescinded through the victims' litigation. However, its legal protection was not universally applicable to other federal prosecutors, and its attempted protection of Ghislaine Maxwell was rejected by the Second Circuit.

- Epstein's death in August 2019 eliminated the possibility of prosecuting him personally under the agreement.

- The agreement remains historically significant because its confidentiality provisions, co-conspirator protections, and treatment of victims became central issues in subsequent litigation and congressional investigations.

### Important Distinction

- The NPA was a federal non-prosecution agreement, not a judicially approved blanket immunity statute. Its contractual protections must be distinguished from immunity granted by a court, statutory immunity, and private civil settlement releases.

## 1. The 2007 NPA

### Negotiating Parties

- Jeffrey Epstein

- Alan Dershowitz

- Jay Lefkowitz

- Kenneth Starr

- Roy Black

- Gerald Lefcourt

- Jack Goldberger

- US  Attorney Alexander Acosta and the Southern District of Florida prosecution team

### Principal Terms

- Epstein agreed to plead guilty to Florida state offenses

- Epstein agreed to register as a sex offender

- Epstein agreed to an incarceration term of at least 18 months under the negotiated agreement

- Epstein agreed to a mechanism facilitating financial recovery by victims

- Federal prosecutors agreed not to prosecute Epstein in the Southern District of Florida for the investigated conduct

- Federal prosecutors agreed not to prosecute four named individuals and any potential co-conspirators of Epstein

### Extraordinary Co-Conspirator Provision

The agreement contained language stating that the United States would not institute criminal charges against potential co-conspirators of Epstein.

This provision became particularly significant when federal prosecutors subsequently charged Ghislaine Maxwell.

The provision did not establish that every federal prosecutor throughout the country was bound by the agreement.

## 2. Legal Status

### 2019 - Southern District of Florida

- On February 21, 2019, the district court determined that federal prosecutors violated victims' rights under the Crime Victims' Rights Act (CVRA) by failing to confer with victims before entering the NPA.

- The court found that victims had also been unfairly treated and denied appropriate notice.

- Epstein died on August 10, 2019, before the litigation concerning remedies could be completed.

- The district court subsequently dismissed the remaining litigation.

### 2020 - Eleventh Circuit

- On April 14, 2020, a divided Eleventh Circuit panel rejected the victims' petition.

- The majority held that CVRA rights did not attach before formal criminal proceedings were initiated against Epstein.

- The court acknowledged the disturbing circumstances surrounding the agreement and prosecutors' treatment of victims.

### 2021 - Eleventh Circuit En Banc

- On April 15, 2021, the Eleventh Circuit ordered rehearing en banc.

- On April 15, 2021, the en banc court held that the CVRA did not authorize a freestanding judicial action to enforce victims' rights outside an existing criminal proceeding.

- Consequently, the victims did not obtain judicial rescission of the NPA.

### 2024 - Second Circuit

- On September 17, 2024, the Second Circuit affirmed Ghislaine Maxwell's conviction.

- The court specifically rejected Maxwell's argument that Epstein's NPA immunized her from prosecution.

- The court held that the Southern District of Florida agreement did not bind the Southern District of New York.

### Current Legal Position

- Epstein's NPA was not judicially rescinded.

- Epstein's death rendered personal prosecution under the agreement moot.

- The agreement did not prevent Maxwell's federal prosecution.

- The agreement did not establish blanket immunity for every alleged Epstein associate.

- The Second Circuit's ruling provides particularly important precedent against expansive interpretations of the co-conspirator provision.

## 3. Related NDAs

An important distinction is necessary: not every confidential settlement is technically an NDA. The following are identifiable legal agreements or documented confidentiality provisions.

### A. September 24, 2007 

NPA Confidentiality Provision

- Epstein negotiated confidentiality provisions designed to prevent public disclosure of the NPA.

- The agreement contemplated that the document would not become part of the public record.

- It required advance notice to Epstein if prosecutors received compulsory process requiring disclosure.

- The confidentiality arrangement contributed to victims remaining unaware of the federal agreement for more than nine months.

Legal significance:

- This was governmental confidentiality incorporated into a federal prosecutorial agreement.

- It was not simply a private NDA between Epstein and a victim.

- The confidentiality provision became part of the controversy over prosecutors' concealment of the agreement.

### B. 2009

Jeffrey Epstein and Virginia Giuffre Settlement

- Major civil confidentiality agreement

- Epstein entered into a confidential civil settlement with Virginia Giuffre in 2009.

- The agreement included a general release, payment provisions, confidentiality requirements, and restrictions concerning disclosure of the agreement.

- The agreement also contained language restricting its use in subsequent proceedings involving Epstein.

- Prince Andrew later attempted to invoke the release provisions as a defense against Giuffre's civil claims.

Legal significance:

- In January 2022, Judge Lewis Kaplan rejected Prince Andrew's motion to dismiss based on the 2009 agreement.

- The court found that Andrew was not a party to the agreement and was not entitled to invoke its protections in the manner he proposed.

- The decision exposed the legal significance of drafting broad releases involving unnamed third parties.

- This is particularly important because it demonstrates how an Epstein-negotiated confidentiality and release arrangement was subsequently invoked by another individual accused of participating in the underlying abuse.

### C. 2017

Virginia Giuffre and Ghislaine Maxwell Settlement

- Giuffre and Maxwell settled their defamation litigation in May 2017.

- The settlement terms remained confidential.

- A judicial protective order governed confidential discovery materials.

- The protective order restricted disclosure of designated materials to third parties.

Legal significance:

- The confidentiality provisions did not prevent subsequent federal investigators from obtaining relevant materials through grand jury process and court-authorized modifications.

- The litigation became a significant source of evidence concerning Epstein's associates.

- Subsequent unsealing litigation helped make previously confidential material available to the public.

### D. 2021-2022

Virginia Giuffre and Prince Andrew

- The parties negotiated settlement-related confidentiality provisions.

- Their 2021 tolling agreement included confidentiality protections for settlement discussions.

- On February 15, 2022, they announced an out-of-court settlement.

- The financial settlement amount was not publicly disclosed.

- The public settlement announcement acknowledged Giuffre's suffering and included Andrew's intention to make a substantial charitable contribution.

Legal significance:

- The settlement resolved Giuffre's civil action against Andrew.

- It did not constitute a criminal acquittal or adjudication of the underlying allegations.

- The confidentiality protections did not create immunity from criminal investigation.

### E. 2022

Dershowitz-Related Civil Settlement

- Alan Dershowitz was subsequently involved in civil defamation litigation arising from Giuffre's allegations.

- Dershowitz denied the allegations.

- In November 2022, the parties publicly announced resolution of their litigation.

- The public statement acknowledged that Giuffre recognized she might have made a mistake in identifying Dershowitz.

- The litigation settlement should not be confused with Epstein's 2007 NPA or the 2009 Epstein-Giuffre settlement.

The confidential aspects of the litigation settlement should not be treated as proof that a separate criminal immunity or trafficking-related NDA existed.

## 4. Adjudicated Predicate Crimes

This is the most consequential distinction for subsequent financial, civil, and criminal investigations.

### Jeffrey Epstein

- June 30, 2008: Pleaded guilty in Florida state court to felony solicitation of prostitution and procurement of minors for prostitution.

- These were adjudicated criminal convictions.

- His 2019 federal sex-trafficking indictment was never adjudicated because he died before trial.

Significance:

- The 2008 convictions establish that Epstein's criminal conduct was not merely an unproven allegation.

- Subsequent financial investigations can examine transactions and assistance connected to the underlying criminal enterprise.

- The 2007 NPA does not erase the state convictions.

### Ghislaine Maxwell

- December 29, 2021: Federal jury convicted Maxwell on five counts.

- The convictions included sex trafficking of a minor, transportation of a minor for illegal sexual activity, and conspiracy-related offenses.

- She received a 20-year federal sentence.

- The Second Circuit affirmed her conviction on September 17, 2024.

Significance:

- Maxwell's convictions establish adjudicated federal sex-trafficking offenses.

- The convictions provide a legally established foundation for examining financial transactions, facilitators, and proceeds connected to the trafficking enterprise.

- The NPA did not protect Maxwell from prosecution.

### Donald Trump

Separate Predicate Case

- May 30, 2024: A New York jury convicted Trump on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records.

- The prosecution concerned the concealment of reimbursement payments to Michael Cohen associated with the Stormy Daniels payment.

- This was a separate criminal case unrelated to Epstein's NPA.

Significance:

- The conviction establishes that Trump was found guilty of falsifying business records in connection with a concealed payment arrangement.

- It does not establish participation in Epstein's trafficking enterprise.

- It is relevant only as a separate adjudicated example involving legal intermediaries and concealed financial transactions.

## Implications 

Related NDAs and Financial Facilitators

### What the Convictions Establish

The Epstein and Maxwell convictions establish adjudicated criminal conduct.

They provide a foundation for investigating:

- Financial transactions connected to the trafficking enterprise

- Payments to facilitators

- Concealment of criminal proceeds

- Financial institutions' knowledge and compliance obligations

- Potential conspiracy and money-laundering offenses

- Whether particular legal services furthered criminal activity

### What the Convictions Do Not Establish

- That every person who signed an Epstein-related NDA participated in a crime

- That every settlement payment constituted trafficking proceeds

- That every financial institution knowingly facilitated criminal activity

- That every attorney representing Epstein knew about or participated in criminal conduct

- That every confidentiality agreement is unenforceable

- That every person named in an NPA provision was guilty of a crime

### Crime-Fraud Exception

- Attorney-client privilege and work-product protections do not automatically protect communications used to further a crime or fraud.

However:

- A court must find an adequate factual basis for applying the crime-fraud exception.

- The underlying attorney-client communication must have been made in furtherance of the alleged crime or fraud.

- Merely representing a convicted criminal does not establish that an attorney participated in criminal conduct.

- An NDA cannot prevent lawful government investigation, compulsory production, or admissible evidence from being obtained.

## Overall Assessment

The 2007 NPA remains historically and legally consequential, but its protections were considerably narrower than the sweeping immunity sometimes attributed to it.

Three developments are particularly important:

- The 2019 district court findings established serious violations of victims' statutory rights by federal prosecutors.

- The 2021 Eleventh Circuit decision prevented victims from obtaining the requested freestanding CVRA enforcement remedy.

- The 2024 Second Circuit decision conclusively rejected Maxwell's argument that the NPA protected her from prosecution in New York.

### Related Civil Agreements 

Independently significant.

The 2009 Epstein-Giuffre agreement, the 2017 Giuffre-Maxwell settlement, and the 2021-2022 Giuffre-Andrew arrangements demonstrate how confidentiality, releases, and restrictions on disclosure were used in the surrounding civil litigation.

However, the existence of these agreements does not establish a coordinated criminal conspiracy among their signatories.

The strongest investigative direction is to compare the actual contractual language, payment records, communications concerning the agreements, and subsequent financial transactions against the conduct established in Epstein's and Maxwell's criminal cases.

# Questions

What additional named or unnamed individuals were potentially protected by the original NPA?

Which civil settlement agreements contained confidentiality or non-disclosure provisions that could affect future discovery?

What legal mechanisms could permit investigators to obtain records protected by those agreements?

How could Epstein's and Maxwell's adjudicated convictions support financial investigations into individuals who facilitated their activities?

What evidence would be necessary to establish that an attorney, financial institution, or NDA signatory knowingly participated in concealing proceeds of the trafficking enterprise?

## Answers

### Potentially Protected Individuals

- The NPA expressly included four named individuals and a broader potential-co-conspirator provision.

- The identity and legal status of each person must be assessed individually.

- Maxwell's prosecution demonstrates that the provision did not provide universal protection.

### Civil Confidentiality

- The 2009 Epstein-Giuffre agreement and 2017 Giuffre-Maxwell settlement contained significant confidentiality protections.

- The Andrew settlement-related documents also included confidentiality provisions.

- These agreements did not create criminal immunity.

### Obtaining Protected Records

- Grand jury subpoenas

- Court orders modifying protective orders

- Compulsory government process

- Applicable statutory exceptions

- Crime-fraud exception where established

- Independent banking and financial records

### Financial Investigations

- Epstein's and Maxwell's convictions establish adjudicated underlying offenses.

- Investigators can trace potentially connected payments, financial intermediaries, and assets.

- Additional evidence is necessary to establish individual liability for money laundering or conspiracy.

### Establishing Knowing Participation

- Actual knowledge of the underlying criminal activity

- Communications demonstrating intent

- Financial transactions and payment instructions

- Evidence of concealment or destruction of records

- Proof connecting the individual to the relevant unlawful conduct
