# Predicate Crimes

Adjudicated criminal cases resulting in guilty verdicts or guilty pleas establish legally proven baseline offenses - known as predicate crimes - that carry severe statutory, evidentiary, and strategic consequences in subsequent civil, regulatory, and criminal proceedings.

Under federal statutes such as the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act (RICO, 18 U.S.C. 1961) and federal money laundering statutes (18 U.S.C. 1956), a proven predicate offense removes the threshold burden of proving that underlying unlawful conduct occurred.

Instead, investigators and litigants can focus on tracing financial proceeds, proving a "pattern of racketeering activity," piercing attorney-client privilege under the crime-fraud exception, and establishing conspiracy or obstruction liability among facilitators, financial institutions, and legal representatives.

## Donald Trump

- The People of the State of New York vs. Donald J. Trump (Indictment No. 71543-23, Supreme Court of the State of New York, New York County)

- On May 30, 2024, a Manhattan jury found Donald Trump guilty on 34 felony counts of Falsifying Business Records in the First Degree (New York Penal Law 175.10).

- Prosecutors proved that Trump falsified internal corporate ledger entries, invoices, and checks to conceal a 130,000 dollar hush-money reimbursement payment made through former personal attorney Michael Cohen to adult film performer Stormy Daniels to unlawfully influence the 2016 presidential election.

- Relevancy Regarding Focus of Responses: This adjudicated felony conviction establishes a legally proven predicate pattern of using private attorneys, shell corporate structures, and disguised legal retainers to conceal hush-money disbursements.

- In the context of congressional probes into ten major law firms performing roughly 940 million dollars in unwritten pro bono settlements - and demands for records involving "any public or private associate of the President" - the 2024 conviction provides investigators and courts with an established evidentiary baseline when evaluating third-party privilege waivers, Antideficiency Act violations, or potential crime-fraud exceptions involving private legal intermediaries.

## Ghislaine Maxwell

- United States vs. Ghislaine Maxwell (Case No. 1:20-cr-00330, US - District Court for the Southern District of New York)

- On December 29, 2021, a federal jury found Ghislaine Maxwell guilty on five federal counts, including sex trafficking of a minor, transporting a minor with intent to engage in criminal sexual activity, and conspiracy, resulting in a 20-year federal prison sentence.

- The trial established that Maxwell recruited, groomed, and facilitated the abuse of underage girls for Jeffrey Epstein over a decade, supported by multi-million-dollar financial transfers from Epstein.

- Relevancy Regarding Focus of Responses: Maxwell's conviction serves as the foundational federal predicate offense establishing the existence of an ongoing, transnational sex-trafficking enterprise funded through major financial institutions.

- Her adjudicated guilt anchors Senator Ron Wyden's August 2026 Senate Finance Committee report documenting Bank Secrecy Act compliance failures at JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, and Bank of New York Mellon, as well as the November 2026 Deutsche Bank leak showing nearly 600 million dollars in suspicious transfers.

- Under federal money laundering laws, Maxwell's proven sex-trafficking convictions supply the "specified unlawful activity" required to prosecute financial facilitators or co-conspirators who laundered or obscured proceeds linked to the enterprise.

## Jeffrey Epstein

- State of Florida vs. Jeffrey E. Epstein (Case Nos. 2006-CF-009454AXX and 2008-CF-009381AXX, 15th Judicial Circuit Court, Palm Beach County, Florida)

- On June 30, 2008, Jeffrey Epstein pleaded guilty in Florida state court to felony charges of solicitation of prostitution and procurement of minors to engage in prostitution, serving 13 months in county jail with work release under a controversial federal Non-Prosecution Agreement (NPA) negotiated by his legal defense team, which included Alan Dershowitz. (His subsequent 2019 federal sex-trafficking indictment in the Southern District of New York was abated upon his death prior to trial.)

- Relevancy Regarding Focus of Responses: Although Epstein's death abated his 2019 federal indictment, his 2008 Florida felony guilty pleas remain adjudicated predicate convictions for sexual exploitation offenses.

- Those convictions directly underpin subsequent civil discovery, Bank Secrecy Act enforcement referrals, and Senator Wyden's investigation into Leon Black's 170 million dollars in post-conviction payments (2012-2017) and DEA Operation "Chain Reaction" records detailing "illegitimate wire transfers" involving 14 unnamed co-conspirators.

- They also frame ethical and privilege scrutiny surrounding post-2008 legal representation, including former Paul, Weiss chairman Brad Karp's coordination with Epstein on witness surveillance and Mitchell Webber's legal research emails under Alan Dershowitz.

## Analysis

### Impacts of Predicate Crimes on Persons and Entities

- Elimination of Threshold Proof in Money Laundering and RICO Claims: Because Maxwell's federal sex-trafficking convictions and Epstein's 2008 Florida convictions are adjudicated facts, civil plaintiffs and regulatory investigators do not need to re-litigate whether underlying criminal activity occurred when pursuing Bank Secrecy Act penalties, civil RICO claims, or asset forfeitures against financial facilitators and estates.

- Heightened Vulnerability to the Crime-Fraud Exception: Trump's 34 felony convictions for disguising hush-money payments as legal fees establish a judicial precedent that legal retainers and attorney-client channels can be misused to conceal unlawful conduct.

- When congressional committees or grand juries subpoena records from settling law firms (such as Paul, Weiss, Kirkland and Ellis, or Sullivan and Cromwell) regarding work performed for private presidential associates, courts evaluating privilege claims can weigh adjudicated history alongside allegations of bribery, extortion, or tax evasion.

- Collateral Estoppel and Civil Discovery Leverage: Adjudicated convictions create estoppel effects and establish relevance thresholds under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 26 and Rule 45, enabling Epstein survivors and oversight committees to compel unredacted banking records, law firm billing narratives, and DEA Operation "Chain Reaction" files that would otherwise remain shielded as speculative "fishing expeditions."

- Regulatory and Bar Disciplinary Exposure: Proven predicate offenses trigger mandatory compliance reviews across regulated professions.

- Law firms implicated in either administration pro bono deals or Epstein-related witness surveillance face state bar disciplinary exposure, while banks identified in Senate Finance reports face Treasury/FinCEN civil money penalties for failing to file Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) on convicted offenders.
