# 1970s Shadow Diplomacy

## International Era

In the context of the 2026 unsealed records and other sources, the 1970s was a foundational decade for the network of figures discussed.

This period saw the transition of several individuals from military or academic roles into the high-stakes worlds of international finance, defense, and retail.

## Jeffrey Epstein

### Teaching at Dalton

1974–1976

- Hired as a math and science teacher by Headmaster Donald Barr despite lacking a college degree.

- He was noted for his flamboyant style and for tutoring the children of prominent Wall Street executives.

### Entry into Bear Stearns 

1976

- Recruited as a junior assistant to a floor trader, reportedly after meeting Alan "Ace" Greenberg through his Dalton connections.

### Rise to Limited Partner

1970s

- Within four years, he rose rapidly through the firm, specializing in the "Special Products" division and tax mitigation for ultra-wealthy clients.

## Donald Barr

### Resignation from Dalton

1974

- Stepped down as Headmaster of the Dalton School in early 1974, just months before Epstein was hired.

### Space Relations

1973

- Published the science fiction novel Space Relations, which oddly featured themes of **oligarchic control** and **child exploitation**, a work that later drew significant retrospective scrutiny.

### Post-Dalton Career

1970s

- He continued as an educator and reviewer, maintaining deep ties to the New York intellectual and political elite.

## Robert Maxwell

### Regulatory Scrutiny

1971

- The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) issued a damning report declaring him unfit to lead a public company due to his artificial inflation of share prices at Pergamon Press.

### Recovery & Expansion

1970s

- Despite the DTI report, he rebuilt his influence, leveraging private family companies to regain control of his publishing interests.

### Foundations of the Empire

1980

- He acquired of the British Printing Corporation by cultivating relationships with international financiers and political leaders.

## Leslie Wexner

### Retail Explosion

1976

- Opened his 100th "The Limited" store.

- The company moved its headquarters to Columbus, Ohio, in 1977.

### Vertical Integration

1978

- Acquired Mast Industries, an apparel importer.

- This allowed him to control his supply chain, a "specialized" business strategy that became his hallmark.

### Wealth Accumulation

1970s

- Transitioned from a regional retailer to a national mogul, seeking high-level financial counsel for his growing personal fortune.

## Alan "Ace" Greenberg

### Bear Stearns

1978

- Assumed the role of CEO.

- Under his "meritocracy" philosophy, he fostered an environment where unconventional talent - like Epstein - could thrive.

### Market Resilience

1973

- Successfully navigated the 1973–1974 market crash, cementing Bear Stearns' reputation as a aggressive, risk-tolerant investment bank.

## Adnan Khashoggi

### Arms Dealer

1970s

- Acted as the primary "fixer" for US defense contractors (Lockheed, Northrop, Raytheon) in Saudi Arabia.

- Between 1970 and 1975 alone, he received over $106 million in commissions from Lockheed.

### Political Influence

1972

- Allegedly donated $200 million to Richard Nixon’s 1972 campaign via offshore channels, an act that partially inspired the 1977 Foreign Corrupt Practices Act.

### Social Orbit

1970s

- Established his reputation as "The Great Gatsby of the Middle East," using his yacht and private planes to host world leaders and business tycoons.

## Ari Ben-Menashe

### Signals Intelligence

1974–1977

- Served in the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) focusing on signal gathering and encryption.

### Military Intelligence Directorate

1977

- Transitioned into the Military Intelligence Directorate, where his background in Farsi and Arabic made him a key analyst during the lead-up to the Iranian Revolution.

## Edgar Bronfman, Sr.

### Seagram

1970s

- Edgar Sr. worked to modernize the Seagram liquor empire in the mid-1970s amidst rising international competition.

### Creative Ventures

1972

- Edgar Jr. began a brief career in the 1970s as a film and Broadway producer (e.g., The Blockhouse, 1972) before eventually returned to the family’s core beverage and entertainment businesses.

## Jean-Luc Brunel

### Modeling Industry Entry

1970s

- Began his career in the late 1970s as a scout for Karin Models in Paris.

### Karin Models

1978

- Became the head of Karin Models, establishing the scouting network that he would later merge with Jeffrey Epstein’s financial backing.

## Peter Branch

### Dalton Transition

1974

- Served as the acting or interim head of the Dalton School following Donald Barr’s departure, overseeing the faculty during Jeffrey Epstein's initial teaching tenure.
