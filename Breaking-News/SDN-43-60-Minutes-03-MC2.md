# MC2 Model Management

The 1988 60 Minutes Expose

## Corporate Biography

MC2 Model Management operated as a prominent modeling and talent agency with primary United States operations established in Miami and New York during the 2000s.

Founded in 2005 (evolving from earlier operations tied to Jean-Luc Brunel's Karin Models framework), the agency positioned itself within the international fashion market.

Because MC2 Model Management operated as a privately held corporate entity rather than a publicly traded corporation, comprehensive balance sheets, detailed annual reports, and formal public board of directors rosters were maintained privately and are not fully accessible via public corporate registries.

### Corporate Structure And Leadership

- Jean-Luc Brunel served as a primary founder and backer, leveraging his decades-long career in international talent scouting and agency management to establish the firm's market presence.

- Jeffrey Fuller acted as president and co-owner, directing day-to-day administrative operations, business management, and financial coordination for the United States branches.

- Administrative staff, bookers, and scouting coordinators managed regional offices across Miami Beach and New York City, handling cross-border model placements and visa sponsorships.

### Financial Operations And Transparency

- Financial records and funding structures for MC2 Model Management involved private capital arrangements, including significant credit line facilities and financial backing linked through external networks.

- Investigative reporting and legal records revealed that the agency generated operational revenue through standard commercial booking commissions, placement fees, and retail client contracts across North America.

- Due to the private corporate status of the agency, statutory public financial audits and annual profit disclosures were not legally required, leaving exact annual turnover figures restricted from public records.

## Epstein vs. Brunel

Detailed Legal Analysis of Jean-Luc Brunel and MC2 Model & Talent Miami, LLC vs. Jeffrey Epstein.

The civil litigation spanning from 2015 to 2019 between Jean-Luc Brunel, his modeling agency MC2 Model Management, and financier Jeffrey Epstein - culminating in the appellate decision Epstein v.

Brunel, 271 So. 3d 1173 (Fla. 3d DCA 2019) - uncovered critical dimensions of the internal operational disputes, financial dependencies, and procedural hurdles governing private talent networks.

### Nature of the Claims and Legal Theory

In January 2015, Jean-Luc Brunel and MC2 Model & Talent Miami, LLC filed a multi-count civil lawsuit against Jeffrey Epstein in the Circuit Court for Miami-Dade County (Lower Tribunal Case No. 14-21348).

### Core Allegations

- The plaintiffs sought damages exceeding $15,000 (later scaling into claims of millions of dollars in lost business value and revenue), asserting claims that included defamation, tortious interference, and intentional infliction of emotional distress.

### The Underlying Argument

- Brunel and MC2 argued that Epstein’s highly publicized criminal investigations, sexual misconduct charges involving minors, and subsequent conviction in Florida (starting in 2006) created toxic public notoriety.

- Because Brunel and MC2 were historically and financially intertwined with Epstein - who provided foundational capital backing, including a $1 million investment/letter of credit arrangement initiated around 2005–2006 at a 5% interest rate - they alleged that commercial clients, photographers, and other modeling agencies (such as Elite Model Management) cut ties with MC2, crippling its operations and brand value.

### Procedural Hurdles

The core of the appellate review by the Third District Court of Appeal of Florida did not center on the merits of the defamation claims, but rather on a fundamental threshold issue:

- personal jurisdiction via service of process.

### The Lower Court Action

- On October 5, 2016, the trial court ordered Brunel and MC2 to properly serve Epstein within 120 days, failing which the action would be dismissed without prejudice.

### The Defective Service Execution

- Rather than attempting service at Epstein's known residential abode or utilizing statutory substitute service provisions for hard-to-reach defendants, the plaintiffs' process server delivered the papers on November 17, 2016, to Jeanne Brenna - identified as an office supervisor at an Epstein business address in the US Virgin Islands.

- Furthermore, the plaintiffs asserted that serving Epstein directly at his residence on Little St.

- James (a private island in the US Virgin Islands) was "impossible" because it was private property.

### The Appellate Court Ruling

- On April 24, 2019, the Third DCA (in an opinion by the panel featuring lower tribunal rulings by Judge Rodney Smith) sharply rejected the plaintiffs' logic.

- The appellate court noted the assertion that a private island is immune to service of process was "absurd on its face," emphasizing that private cays are routinely accessible by boat and process servers can approach front doors just like any other private residence.

### Outcome of the Appeal

- Because Brunel and MC2 failed to strictly comply with Florida’s statutory requirements for service of process (such as Florida Statutes governing service on individuals or sole proprietors at places of business) and failed to exercise due diligence to utilize proper substitute service mechanisms (e.g., concealing whereabouts or service by publication under sections 48.181 or 49.021), the Third DCA reversed the trial court’s denial of Epstein's motion to dismiss.

### Final Mandate

- The appellate court remanded the case with explicit instructions to enter a dismissal without prejudice in favor of Epstein.

### Exposure of Private Financial Conduits

- Although the 2019 appellate ruling dismantled the active lawsuit on procedural grounds, the extensive court filings, exhibits, and related investigative records publicly exposed the hidden financial plumbing connecting Epstein's network to boutique talent agencies.

- Documents entered into the record validated that Epstein had functioned as a primary financial backer, supplying credit facilities, startup capital, and financial coordination lines that sustained MC2's transatlantic expansion from Paris (Karin Models framework) to Miami and New York.

### Dissolution of Corporate Entities

- The mounting legal pressures, public exposure of historical recruitment pipelines, and fallout from these proceedings coincided with the final collapse of the agency’s corporate footprint.

- On September 27, 2019 - just months after the Third DCA's mandate was handed down - MC2 Model & Talent Miami, LLC officially filed for voluntary dissolution in the Florida corporate registries, leading to the dispersal of its remaining assets into independent successor entities (such as The Identity Models and MC2 Tel Aviv).
