# Interlochen Center for the Arts

Michigan Youth At Risk - Epstein Lodge

## Traverse City, MI

Interlochen Center for the Arts is a non-profit arts education institution located in Grand Traverse County, Michigan.

### Founded

Founded in 1928 as the National High School Orchestra Camp, it operates the Interlochen Arts Camp and Interlochen Arts Academy. 

### 1967 Student

During the 1990s, financier Jeffrey Epstein - who attended Interlochen Arts Camp as a student in 1967 - became a major donor to the institution.

### Epstein Lodge

In 1995, Epstein provided a financial gift to fund the construction of an on-campus facility designated as the Jeffrey E. Epstein Scholarship Lodge (commonly known as the Epstein Lodge).

### Severed Ties

Interlochen officials severed formal philanthropic ties with Epstein in 2007 following his initial criminal charges in Florida, removing his donor signage and renaming the facility the Green Lake Lodge.

### Demolished

The building was demolished by the institution in June 2026.

## Institutional Governance 

### Board of Trustees (1980-2000)

- Interlochen's Board of Trustees during this period included prominent arts patrons, corporate executives, and civic leaders.

- Notable trustees from that era included figureheads such as Charles McWhorter (trustee from 1970 to 1999).

- Specific public corporate records connecting Interlochen's board leadership directly to Jeffrey Epstein's financial operations or ultra-high-net-worth wealth management networks remain undocumented in formal institutional disclosures.

### Financial Institutions

- Independent investigations and regulatory filings have detailed Jeffrey Epstein's extensive banking relationships with financial institutions - primarily JPMorgan Chase (where Epstein maintained accounts from 1998 to 2013) and Deutsche Bank (which handled his accounts from 2013 to 2019).

- However, public corporate audit reports and institutional filings for Interlochen Center for the Arts do not indicate that these banking relationships were established through Interlochen's governance board, nor is there documentation linking Interlochen's institutional endowments directly to joint financial operations with Bank of America, JPMorgan Chase, or Deutsche Bank.

- Standardized protocols established by non-profit educational institutions to screen high-net-worth philanthropists, evaluate reputational risk, and govern naming rights agreements.

- Interlochen Center for the Arts established enhanced donor background checks, criminal record reviews, and ethics-based naming rights criteria following the 2007 severance of ties with Jeffrey Epstein.

### Private Banking Compliance

- Regulatory frameworks and monitoring systems employed by financial institutions to track large transfers, detect suspicious financial activity, and maintain due diligence on high-risk clients.

- Private banking compliance teams monitored high-net-worth donations through Know Your Customer protocols, Anti-Money Laundering transaction monitoring, and source-of-funds verification for large institutional wire transfers.

### Institutional Abuse Reports

- Formal third-party legal reviews examining historical misconduct, administrative response failures, and board governance accountability across multiple decades.

- The July 2026 Sanghavi Law Office report detailed structural oversight gaps across seven decades, including unaddressed student complaints and insufficient historical vetting by board leadership.

### Endowment Fund Reallocation

- Legal procedures and judicial remedies that govern how charitable organizations handle, return, or redirect funds derived from unlawful activity or compromised donors.

- Educational institutions utilize legal mechanisms such as court-approved cy-près applications, donor agreement clawback clauses, or voluntary restitution transfers to surrender or reallocate illicit gifts.
