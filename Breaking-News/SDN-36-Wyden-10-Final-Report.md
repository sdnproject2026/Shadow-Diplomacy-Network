# Proposed Reforms and Recommended Federal Action

## Proposed Reforms to Federal Anti‑Money‑Laundering Laws

The investigation identified systemic failures across multiple major banks that enabled Jeffrey Epstein’s criminal activity for nearly two decades. To prevent similar failures in the future, the report proposes several reforms to strengthen federal anti‑money‑laundering (AML) laws and improve oversight of ultra‑wealthy clients.

Key reforms include:

- **Strengthening SAR filing requirements**  

  Banks should be required to file Suspicious Activity Reports in real time, with stricter penalties for late or retroactive filings.

- **Enhanced oversight for ultra‑high‑net‑worth clients**  

  Banks should implement specialized due diligence requirements for clients whose wealth or transaction volume poses elevated AML risks.

- **Mandatory documentation for large payments**  

  Financial institutions should be required to obtain and retain business records substantiating the purpose of large wire transfers.

- **Improved inter‑bank information sharing**  

  Section 314(b) of the Patriot Act should be expanded to encourage confidential information sharing between banks when suspicious activity is detected.

- **Stronger penalties for compliance failures**  

  Civil and criminal penalties should be increased for banks and individuals who fail to comply with AML laws.

- **Executive accountability requirements**  

  Senior executives responsible for compliance failures should face personal consequences, including fines, suspensions or criminal referrals.

- **Modernization of AML technology**  

  Banks should be required to adopt updated transaction monitoring systems capable of detecting complex patterns associated with human trafficking and other crimes.

## Recommended Federal Action

The report calls on federal agencies to take immediate action to address the compliance failures uncovered during the investigation. Recommended actions include:

- **Department of Justice (DOJ)**  

  Conduct thorough investigations into the conduct of individual bankers who handled Epstein’s accounts and failed to report suspicious activity.

- **U.S. Treasury Department**  

  Review SAR filing practices at JPMorgan Chase, Deutsche Bank and Bank of America to determine whether violations of the Bank Secrecy Act occurred.

- **Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN)**  

  Strengthen guidance on AML compliance for accounts held by ultra‑wealthy individuals and require enhanced monitoring of high‑risk clients.

- **Federal Reserve and Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC)**  

  Examine internal compliance failures at major banks and impose penalties where appropriate.

- **Congressional Oversight**  

  Congress should expand oversight of Wall Street banks and ensure that federal regulators have the authority and resources needed to enforce AML laws effectively.

## Summary

The investigation revealed widespread failures across multiple major financial institutions. These failures allowed Jeffrey Epstein to operate his trafficking network with financial support from banks that ignored red flags, delayed reporting and prioritized profit over legal obligations.

The proposed reforms and recommended federal actions are intended to:

- strengthen AML laws  

- improve oversight of ultra‑wealthy clients  

- ensure timely reporting of suspicious activity  

- hold banks and executives accountable  

- prevent future compliance failures that enable dangerous criminal activity  

These steps are necessary to protect public safety and restore integrity to the U.S. financial system.
