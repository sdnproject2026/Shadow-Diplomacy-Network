# Deutsche Bank Leak 

## November 17, 2026

The provided text summarizes a major international journalism investigation published on November 17, 2026, titled _Inside Deutsche Bank_.

## The Claims

### Accidental Trump Tax Leak

- According to the Financial Times, a Deutsche Bank staffer mistakenly emailed year-end tax statements for 1,586 private banking accounts - including then-President Donald Trump's - to another client's accountant in March 2017.

- The accountant deleted the file and alerted the bank.

- While executives notified regulators, they decided not to inform affected clients or the White House.

- Current CEO Christian Sewing, who led the private banking unit at the time, called the error an "awful mistake" in internal emails.

- Speaking to reporters in Manila on November 17, 2026, Donald Trump called the disclosure "illegal".

### Whistleblower Leak

- Two confidential whistleblowers leaked more than 15 million internal files - including emails, board minutes, and compliance reports - to investigative journalists.

- A consortium of over 200 journalists across more than 50 news outlets, including the Financial Times, Der Spiegel, Le Monde, and The New York Times, reviewed the documents.

### Jeffrey Epstein

21 suspicious activity reports (SARs)

- As reported by Forbes, internal records show Deutsche Bank filed 21 suspicious activity reports in 2019 flagging approximately $582 million across roughly 3,200 transactions tied to Jeffrey Epstein.

$582 million

- This amount is more than double what US Senate investigators had previously uncovered.

### Russian Ties

- Coverage by CNBC and other consortium outlets details how the leaked files reveal relationships with Russian oligarchs and suspected money-laundering networks.

- The files also reveal internal efforts to expand Russian business shortly before the February 2022 invasion of Ukraine despite compliance warnings.

### Deutsche Bank's Response

- In statements responding to the reports, Deutsche Bank noted that many of the cases date back up to two decades and were reviewed with regulators at the time.

- The lender stated it invested €7 billion ($7.7 billion) into strengthening its internal controls between 2019 and 2025, emphasizing that it is a different institution today.
