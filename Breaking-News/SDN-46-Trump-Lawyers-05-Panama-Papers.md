# Panama Papers Encyclopedia

## Executive Summary

- The Panama Papers should be treated as an international financial-investigation leads encyclopedia, not simply a historical tax-evasion scandal.

- The 2016 leak of approximately 11.5 million documents from Panamanian law firm Mossack Fonseca exposed offshore companies, beneficial-ownership concealment, financial intermediaries, politically connected clients, and cross-border asset structures.

- Its continuing investigative value lies in identifying relationships among offshore corporations, banks, law firms, accountants, fiduciaries, trusts, foundations, and politically exposed persons.

- The most significant connection to the present investigation is the possibility of using historical offshore records to identify financial intermediaries, corporate structures, counterparties, and transactions that overlap with later Epstein-related financial disclosures and Trump-associated business networks.

- An offshore company, undisclosed beneficial owner, or international transfer is not independently evidence of criminal conduct. The investigative significance comes from corroborating the underlying transactions, identifying their beneficial owners, establishing the source and destination of funds, and determining whether financial institutions or professional intermediaries knowingly facilitated unlawful activity.

## Status of the Panama Papers

### 1. Original Leak and Financial Records

Published April 3, 2016, through the International Consortium of Investigative Journalists (ICIJ), Süddeutsche Zeitung, and more than 100 media partners.

Approximately 11.5 million leaked records, representing 2.6 terabytes of information.

Nearly 40 years of records, principally 1977-2015.

More than 214,000 offshore entities connected to individuals and organizations in over 200 countries and territories.

Records included:

- Bank and financial spreadsheets

- Internal emails and correspondence

- Corporate formation documents

- Shareholder and director records

- Passport and identification documents

- Beneficial ownership information

- Trusts and foundation arrangements

- Financial intermediaries and corporate service providers

ICIJ identified more than 14,000 banks, law firms, company incorporators, and other intermediaries that worked with Mossack Fonseca.

The importance of this database is that it documents relationships, not merely individual offshore accounts. It can help reconstruct who established companies, who controlled them, which intermediaries participated, and how corporate structures connected across jurisdictions.

### 2. Panama Criminal Proceedings

Important legal development

2024-06-28

Panama's Second Criminal Liquidation Court acquitted all 28 defendants in the Panama Papers money-laundering proceedings.

The court found that electronic evidence obtained from Mossack Fonseca's servers had not satisfied chain-of-custody requirements.

The evidence presented in printed form lacked adequate digital authentication and integrity verification, including appropriate hash values.

The remaining evidence was considered insufficient to establish criminal responsibility.

The court also acquitted 31 defendants in the separately originating Lava Jato proceedings, which had been consolidated into the case.

2026-08-10

- Panama's Superior Court affirmed the acquittals on appeal.

2026-08-13

- Panama's Judicial Branch publicly confirmed the appellate decision.

The prosecution's appeal did not overturn the acquittals.

Contemporary reporting indicated that a further cassation proceeding before the Supreme Court remained a possible avenue.

Legal significance

The acquittals do not establish that the Panama Papers were fabricated.

They do not establish that every offshore transaction was legitimate.

They establish that the prosecution did not produce sufficiently authenticated and persuasive evidence to convict those defendants in that particular criminal proceeding.

The chain-of-custody failure is particularly important for future investigations relying on digital financial records.

Sources: Panama Judicial Branch and ICIJ.

### 3. International Financial Consequences

ICIJ reported that governments had recovered more than $1.36 billion in unpaid taxes, fines, and penalties from investigations inspired by the Panama Papers.

Germany, Australia, Belgium, Denmark, Italy, and other countries pursued tax and financial investigations.

The leak prompted investigations into beneficial ownership, tax evasion, professional intermediaries, offshore banking, and politically exposed persons.

The collapse of Mossack Fonseca did not eliminate the underlying offshore corporate structures or the intermediaries who established them.

### 4. Significant Subsequent Criminal Development

2026-04-24 - German Conviction

A German court convicted Christoph Zollinger, a former co-owner of Mossack Fonseca, of aiding and abetting tax evasion.

He received a sentence of one year and nine months' probation.

The case concerned offshore companies established in Panama and other tax havens.

German prosecutors alleged that the structures were established on a large scale in exchange for fees.

This is an important distinction: the acquittals in Panama did not prevent a separate foreign prosecution based on evidence developed under another country's jurisdiction.

## Panama Papers as a Leads Encyclopedia

The most useful investigative application is to treat the Panama Papers as a historical relationship database.

### Financial Relationships

Identify offshore companies associated with individuals already appearing in other investigations.

Identify shareholders, directors, beneficial owners, nominees, and corporate agents.

Identify banks that maintained accounts for offshore entities.

Identify law firms and accountants who established or administered those entities.

Identify intermediaries appearing repeatedly across unrelated transactions.

Identify companies that were dissolved, struck off, transferred, or reactivated.

Identify transfers involving trusts, foundations, holding companies, and private investment vehicles.

### Cross-Reference Opportunities

The Panama Papers should be compared with:

The objective is to find independently corroborated connections, not simply matching names.

## Implications for Trump 

The Administration's Free Legal Services Arrangements

### The Central Investigative Question

Could law firms that accepted administration-directed free legal work have performed other legal, financial, corporate, or intermediary services for private presidential associates whose financial relationships overlap with entities or intermediaries documented in the Panama Papers?

This is a legitimate investigative hypothesis.

There is currently no established evidence that the ten settling law firms performed Epstein-related work through their administration agreements.

However, the Panama Papers create a potential avenue for investigating overlapping professional relationships.

### 1. Identifying Undisclosed Relationships

Suppose congressional investigators obtain records from a law firm showing that it:

- Represented a private presidential associate.

- Structured an international holding company.

- Provided advice concerning offshore trusts.

- Reviewed international wire transfers.

- Advised on beneficial ownership disclosures.

- Represented a financial intermediary connected to a previously identified offshore entity.

Investigators could compare those records against historical Panama Papers corporate records.

A match involving the same intermediary, corporate agent, bank, beneficial owner, or offshore entity could establish a concrete investigative lead.

A matching name alone would not establish wrongdoing.

### 2. The Free Legal Work Issue

The approximately $940 million in reported legal-service commitments creates a separate accounting and oversight issue.

Investigators should distinguish:

- Legal services actually delivered.

- The identity of the actual beneficiary.

- The agency or private party requesting the work.

- The source of the firm's uncompensated labor.

- The financial value assigned to each service.

- Whether the work displaced ordinary appropriated government spending.

- Whether the work benefited private individuals or commercial entities.

- Whether the work involved international financial structures or sensitive financial investigations.

The most consequential question is whether ostensibly charitable legal services were used to provide economic benefits to private parties or circumvent restrictions on government spending.

### 3. Potential International Financial Overlap

The Panama Papers could help investigators identify:

- Offshore entities used by private presidential associates.

- Corporate service providers that also serviced Epstein-associated entities.

- Banks involved in both historical offshore structures and later suspicious financial transactions.

- Attorneys who represented multiple parties in interconnected financial arrangements.

- Trust administrators and nominees who concealed beneficial ownership.

- International payment channels that appear in more than one investigation.

This would be particularly relevant if subpoenaed law firm billing records identify work involving the same entities or intermediaries.

## Potential Legal Exposure

### A. Money Laundering

Federal money laundering statutes, including 18 US C. § 1956 and § 1957, require proof of their respective statutory elements.

The investigation would need to establish:

- A qualifying financial transaction.

- Criminal proceeds or specified unlawful activity.

- The required knowledge.

- The relevant intent or statutory conditions.

- A connection between the transaction and the alleged underlying offense.

The existence of an offshore corporation does not establish money laundering.

### B. Bank Secrecy Act Violations

The Bank Secrecy Act and its implementing regulations impose obligations on covered financial institutions.

Investigators could examine:

- Customer identification and beneficial ownership information.

- Know Your Customer records.

- Transaction monitoring.

- Suspicious Activity Report decisions.

- Internal compliance escalations.

- Correspondence between banks and corporate service providers.

An important distinction: failure to file a SAR is not automatically proof of participation in the underlying criminal activity. Liability depends on the applicable reporting obligation and evidence establishing the violation.

### C. Attorney-Client Privilege

This is potentially one of the most important areas of investigation.

Historical Panama Papers records could establish that a firm or attorney had an existing relationship with an offshore entity or financial intermediary.

Subsequent congressional subpoenas could seek:

- Engagement letters.

- Client identification records.

- Conflict-check records.

- Billing narratives.

- Corporate formation documents.

- Communications concerning financial transactions.

- Records identifying the actual client and beneficial owner.

However, ordinary legal advice remains potentially privileged.

The crime-fraud exception requires a sufficient evidentiary showing that the communications or legal services were intended to further crime or fraud. Merely representing a person later accused of misconduct is insufficient.

### D. Civil Discovery 

Collateral Estoppel

This requires an important qualification to the earlier investigative theory.

Adjudicated criminal convictions can establish certain facts in subsequent proceedings, but they do not automatically establish the liability of banks, law firms, or unrelated third parties.

Collateral estoppel requires the relevant issue to have been actually litigated and necessarily decided, along with the applicable requirements concerning parties or their legal relationship.

Likewise, Panama Papers disclosures do not automatically entitle plaintiffs to unrestricted Rule 45 subpoenas.

Nevertheless, independently authenticated historical records can provide a concrete factual basis for seeking narrowly tailored discovery.

## Revelations

### 1. Evidence Opportunity

The 2024 Panama proceedings and 2026 appellate affirmation expose a significant weakness in the prosecution's handling of digital evidence.

The problem was not simply the absence of documents. It was the inability to establish the authenticity and integrity of the digital evidence.

For future litigation, this makes original electronic records, forensic imaging, cryptographic hashes, metadata preservation, and documented chain of custody especially important.

This lesson is directly relevant when evaluating leaked Deutsche Bank records and historical offshore databases.

### 2. Foreign Prosecutions 

Succeed Independently

The April 2026 German conviction of a former Mossack Fonseca co-owner demonstrates that the Panama acquittals did not resolve every potential international criminal investigation involving the offshore industry.

Different jurisdictions can prosecute distinct conduct using independently obtained evidence.

### 3. The Intermediary

The most important investigative discovery might not be a matching name between Epstein and a Trump associate.

It could be the same:

- Offshore company formation agent.

- Private banking intermediary.

- Trust administrator.

- Corporate nominee.

- Financial adviser.

- Law firm.

- Bank account administrator.

Those relationships can establish investigative connections that warrant further examination without prematurely alleging that the parties participated in the same criminal enterprise.

### 4. Coerced Free Legal Services 

Investigation Has a Potential International Dimension

If law firm records reveal work involving offshore corporate structures, international trusts, or private financial intermediaries, the Panama Papers could provide historical corroboration.

That could transform a congressional inquiry into uncompensated legal services into a broader investigation of financial relationships, conflicts of interest, disclosure obligations, and potential private benefits.

It remains a hypothesis until supported by actual engagement records and independently authenticated financial evidence.

### 5. Biggest Legal Opportunity

The strongest strategy is to combine three evidentiary categories:

- Adjudicated predicate offenses.

- Authenticated historical offshore and financial records.

- Newly compelled law firm and banking records.

Together, these could help establish financial relationships, knowledge, intent, and transaction histories.

Importantly, an adjudicated predicate offense does not automatically prove that every associated financial transaction was criminal or that every professional intermediary knew about the underlying offense.
