# Deutsche Bank Turned a Blind Eye to Epstein’s Suspicious Transactions

After JPMorgan Chase forced Jeffrey Epstein out in 2013, he moved his banking relationship to Deutsche Bank. From 2013 until his death in 2019, Epstein became a major client of Deutsche Bank’s private wealth division. During this period, Deutsche Bank repeatedly ignored massive cash withdrawals, suspicious wire transfers, and other red flags that should have triggered immediate scrutiny under federal anti‑money‑laundering laws.

## Epstein’s Relationship with Deutsche Bank

Epstein’s accounts at Deutsche Bank were used to:

- withdraw large amounts of cash  

- send payments to women and girls  

- move money through high‑risk foreign jurisdictions  

- fund operations in the U.S. Virgin Islands  

- pay accomplices and employees who facilitated his criminal activity  

Despite Epstein’s status as a registered sex offender, Deutsche Bank treated him as a lucrative client and continued providing services without proper oversight.

## Massive Cash Withdrawals

One of the most alarming patterns involved Epstein’s frequent and unusually large cash withdrawals. These withdrawals:

- lacked clear business purpose  

- were inconsistent with typical client behavior  

- matched known indicators of human trafficking activity  

Deutsche Bank employees raised concerns internally, but senior management repeatedly overrode or ignored these warnings.

## High‑Risk Wire Transfers

Epstein also executed numerous wire transfers that should have triggered enhanced due diligence. These included:

- payments to women in foreign jurisdictions  

- transfers routed through correspondent banks in high‑risk regions  

- wires lacking any legitimate business explanation  

In 2019, Deutsche Bank retroactively flagged 1,140 wire transfers totaling \$147 million — years after the transactions occurred and only after Epstein’s arrest.

## Compliance Failures

Deutsche Bank’s failures included:

- inadequate know‑your‑customer procedures  

- failure to verify the purpose of large transactions  

- ignoring internal compliance alerts  

- prioritizing revenue over legal obligations  

- failing to file timely Suspicious Activity Reports  

These failures allowed Epstein to continue operating his trafficking network with financial support from the bank.

## Protecting a Lucrative Client

Internal documents show that Deutsche Bank executives viewed Epstein as a valuable source of business. The bank earned significant fees from Epstein’s accounts and related financial activities. This financial incentive contributed to a culture where compliance concerns were minimized or dismissed.

## Summary

Deutsche Bank’s oversight failures were severe and systemic. By ignoring clear indicators of criminal activity, the bank enabled Epstein to move millions of dollars through its systems without scrutiny. These failures represent a major breakdown in federal anti‑money‑laundering compliance and contributed directly to Epstein’s ability to sustain his sex trafficking operations.
