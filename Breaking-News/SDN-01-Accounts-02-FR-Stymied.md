### Broader Network Inquiry

Labyrinthine

The investigation is being treated as a broader network inquiry rather than simply a review of Epstein's conduct in France.

Beccuau characterized the network as "labyrinthine", with investigators following leads extending from Paris to New York and other locations.

### Jean-Luc Brunel

A key piece of context is the earlier French investigation involving Jean-Luc Brunel, the modeling agent associated with Epstein.

The newly released US files prompted French authorities to revisit that material and examine whether additional crimes or participants can be identified.

### Daniel Siad

The case also intersects with the investigation of Daniel Siad, a modeling scout whose communications with Epstein have attracted renewed scrutiny.

Available reporting describes Siad as someone who supplied Epstein with profiles and information about potential models, while French authorities have been examining whether people involved in recruitment facilitated Epstein's access to victims.

## Why it matters

- new witnesses

- new recruiters

- new intermediaries

The most important development is therefore not simply the number of victims.

It is the emergence of potentially new witnesses, recruiters and intermediaries that could allow French investigators to reconstruct how Epstein's network operated in France and internationally. The investigation remains ongoing, and being identified as a potential recruiter or associate should not by itself be interpreted as proof of criminal conduct.

## New York Times Article

- https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/11/world/europe/france-paris-epstein-prosecutor.html?unlocked_article_code=1.AVE.z7b9.OVP4ISxvqvac&smid=url-share


The New York Times article reports a significant expansion of the French investigation into Jeffrey Epstein's activities in France. Paris Prosecutor Laure Beccuau said investigators now count **26 potential victims**, including **13 who had not appeared in earlier Epstein-related case files**. Authorities have also identified **additional potential recruiters**, although prosecutors have not publicly named them.

The investigation was reopened in February 2026 after the US Department of Justice released millions of Epstein-related records. The DOJ says its January 30 release added more than 3 million pages, bringing the total production to nearly 3.5 million pages.

A central development is the death of **Daniel Siad**, a French modeling scout whom investigators were examining as a suspected recruiter for Epstein. Siad died at his home near Paris in July before French investigators had questioned him. His death ended the investigation specifically against him, but **not the broader investigation into the suspected trafficking network**. Siad denied the allegations while alive.

Beccuau's explanation is that the investigation is unusually broad and international: investigators have followed leads involving **Paris, New York, Cannes and Saint-Tropez**. She characterized the Epstein network as "labyrinthine," emphasizing that investigators must pursue numerous separate leads rather than a single chain of evidence.

An important qualification: **being identified in the investigation does not mean that an individual has been proven to have committed a crime**, and the 26 figure represents victims identified or counted by prosecutors, not 26 adjudicated cases.

### What is newly established

- **26 victims** are now counted in the French investigation.
- **13 are newly identified** relative to previously known French case files.
- Prosecutors have obtained names of **additional suspected recruiters**.
- **24 victims have been clearly identified**, while prosecutors said eight still need to be interviewed; some are living abroad.
- The investigation extends beyond Paris to **New York, Cannes and Saint-Tropez**.
- The death of Daniel Siad removed a potentially important investigative subject, but did **not** terminate the network investigation.

## Opinions

### Primary (NYT et al)

- **The New York Times** — Frames the development primarily as an expansion of a still-active French criminal investigation: the discovery of 13 additional potential victims and additional suspected recruiters indicates that French authorities believe the known network was larger than earlier investigations established. The article also emphasizes victims' frustration with the pace of French justice.

- **France Inter / Laure Beccuau** — Beccuau's position is that prosecutors are making progress but that the investigation's complexity explains its pace. Her "labyrinthine network" characterization conveys that investigators are pursuing many interconnected leads rather than a single suspect.

- **US Department of Justice** — The DOJ's position is that the January 2026 publication of nearly 3.5 million pages provides a substantial documentary record drawn from FBI investigations and other Epstein-related cases. Those records became an important source of new investigative leads in France.

### Secondary (Reuters et al)

- **Reuters** — Focuses on the significance of Siad's death: French authorities had allegations involving at least 24 women at the time and had not arrested or fully questioned Siad before his death. Reuters stresses that the wider investigation continues.

- **Le Monde** — Places the French investigation in a broader geographic context, particularly Epstein's repeated presence in Saint-Tropez and the role of the French modeling world. Its reporting portrays France as more than a peripheral location in Epstein's network.

- **The Washington Post** — Emphasizes documentary evidence concerning Siad's communications with Epstein, including correspondence involving young women and teenage girls.

- **Reddit / r/Epstein** — Community reaction is broadly supportive of the French investigation's expansion but skeptical about its pace. One recurring sentiment is that French authorities are moving slowly compared with the scale of the allegations; commenters also speculate about the implications of international cooperation. These are **commenter opinions, not verified facts**.
