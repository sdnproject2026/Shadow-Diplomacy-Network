# "Epstein-Connected" Banks 

## Held to Account

### US Lawmakers Unveil Bill

Kalyeena Makortoff 

Banking correspondent

Thu 15 Oct 2026 09.30 EDT

US lawmakers have unveiled a bill aimed at holding Wall Street executives and financial firms accountable for helping finance the late sex offender Jeffrey Epstein's crimes.

The move came as Democratic senator Elizabeth Warren criticized UK bank Barclays for "dodging responsibility" over its failure to vet former chief executive Jes Staley and his ties to Epstein.

A bipartisan group of lawmakers, led by Warren and House representative Robert Garcia, introduced the Epstein Wall Street Records Act on Thursday, 2026-10-15.

The bill follows a months-long investigation into Epstein, who died in prison in 2019 while awaiting trial on child sex-trafficking charges.

If passed, the legislation would require regulators, including the Federal Reserve and the Securities and Exchange Commission, to investigate financial firms and executives connected to Epstein for potential violations of banking, investment or illicit funding laws.

Regulators could fine offenders or ban them from the US financial sector.

The bill, which is also backed by Republican representative Thomas Massie, would require regulators to publicly disclose records relating to Epstein and his associates and refer potential criminal violations to the US Department of Justice.

Warren said: "Since the big banks won't hold themselves accountable - and the Trump administration won't either - Congress must act to force real transparency and accountability for them and their executives who enabled Jeffrey Epstein's crimes."

## Barclays and Jes Staley

Warren's announcement came as she criticized Barclays executives for failing to adequately answer questions about the bank's former chief executive Jes Staley and his connections to Epstein.

Staley's relationship with Epstein ultimately contributed to the end of his career at Barclays.

The American banker resigned from the bank in November 2021 after UK regulators began investigating the nature of his relationship with Epstein, whom he had met in 2000 during his three-decade career at JP Morgan.

The Financial Conduct Authority's investigation was prompted by a collection of more than 1,200 emails between Staley and Epstein.

The regulator concluded that the pair were "indeed close" and that their relationship "went beyond one that was professional in nature".

Staley was banned from the UK's financial sector in 2023 for playing down those ties.

He later failed to overturn the decision in a legal challenge that revealed further details of their friendship.

During the proceedings, Staley admitted to having sex with a member of Epstein's staff in New York, describing the encounter as "consensual".

The case also revived a 2010 exchange referring to Disney princesses.

Staley had written: 

> "That was fun.
>
> Say hi to Snow White." 

During a UK court hearing, he denied knowledge of the exchange.

After losing his legal challenge against the Financial Conduct Authority, Staley said he was disappointed by the outcome but was "never dishonest".

In July 2026, Warren and a group of House Democrats wrote to Barclays chair Nigel Higgins, asking how the bank had vetted Staley before appointing him chief executive.

They also asked whether the bank had investigated potential deficiencies in its executive hiring procedures, given that it had "allowed the board to hire a CEO who held extensive professional and personal ties to a convicted sex offender".

## Barclays' Response

Barclays responded with a letter from Lance Williams, its head of US government relations.

Warren said the response was insufficient.

The letter focused on the findings of the UK regulators' investigation.

Williams said Staley had "concealed information about his actions and the nature of his relationship with Mr Epstein".

He added that only after Staley's resignation had 

>"previously unknown information come to light through various public disclosures".
>
> "This information paints a fundamentally different picture of Mr Staley than what he presented to Barclays," 

Williams said.

He noted that Barclays had relied on external executive search firms to recruit Staley in 2015, when he was "known for his accomplishments in the banking industry".

Warren argued that Barclays' response demonstrated why regulators should launch formal investigations into Wall Street's connections to Epstein.

> "Barclays continues to dodge responsibility over its apparent failure to vet its former CEO Jes Staley's close professional and personal ties to Epstein," 

she said.

Barclays declined to comment.

Staley's legal representatives were contacted for comment.
