# 1940s Shadow Diplomacy 

## Foundational Era

In the context of the 2026 unsealed records and other sources, the 1940s are highlighted as the definitive "foundational era" where the network’s elder figures operated in high-stakes wartime and post-war environments.

This decade was defined by the transition of the OSS into the modern intelligence community and the redistribution of global power.

### Donald Barr

1942–1945

OSS Service

- Served in the Office of Strategic Services (OSS) within the Research and Analysis (R&A) Branch.

- His work focused on synthesizing raw intelligence into strategic briefings - a skill set he later applied to his work in academia and "talent identification."



Primary Contact

- Director of the OSS

- William J. Donovan

- Barr moved in the elite circles of the OSS "Ivy League" recruitment pool, alongside figures like the Dulles brothers and Allen Kent.

Post-War Transition

- By the late 1940s, he transitioned into a teaching role at Columbia University, maintaining the "intellectual recruitment" mindset he cultivated during the war.

### Robert Maxwell

1940–1945

Wartime Heroism

- Born Ludvík Hoch, he escaped Naxi-occupied Czechoslovakia and eventually joined the North Staffordshire Regiment of the British Army.

- He rose from private to Captain and was awarded the Military Cross (MC) by Field Marshal Montgomery in 1945.

1945–1947

Intelligence & Press

- Following the war, he served in the press section of the Allied occupation authorities in Berlin.

- This role gave him direct control over German news distribution and access to scientific assets.

### Adnan Khashoggi

1940–1949

Elite Upbringing

- Spent his childhood in Mecca as the son of the King’s personal physician, Dr. Mohammad Khashoggi.

Educational Networking

- Sent to Victoria College in Alexandria, Egypt - the "Eton of the Middle East."

- During the late 1940s, he established social bonds with future kings (Hussein of Jordan and Faisal II of Iraq), forming the earliest foundations of his "fixer" network.

### Leslie Wexner

1940s

Early Childhood

- Born in 1937, Wexner grew up in Dayton and Columbus, Ohio, in a family of Russian-immigrant clothing merchants.

Exposure to Trade

- His parents, Harry and Bella, included him in business discussions from an early age.

- The decade was defined by the Wexner family’s assimilation and the opening of their downtown retail operations, which served as Leslie’s first classroom in logistics.

### Alan "Ace" Greenberg

1940–1949

Kansas to Wall Street

- Spent the mid-40s in Kansas and Oklahoma before moving to New York after graduating from the University of Missouri in 1949.

The Bear Stearns Entry

- In 1949 started as a $32.50-a-week clerk at Bear Stearns.

- He famously described his first job as "punching pins on a map" for oil well locations, a task that introduced him to the intersection of energy, geography, and finance.

### Edgar Bronfman, Sr.

1940–1949

The Seagram Heir

- Spent the decade as a student in Montreal, attending private Anglophile schools.

Wartime Family Influence

- His father, Samuel Bronfman, utilized the decade to cement Seagram’s status as the world’s largest distiller.

- Edgar Sr. eventually moved to the US. toward the end of the decade to attend Williams College and McGill University, preparing for his apprenticeship in the family empire.

### Ari Ben-Menashe

1945

Family Origins

- His parents, Iraqi Jews, moved from Baghdad to Tehran in 1945.

Intelligence Connection

- His father, Gourdji, had been a member of LEHI (Fighters for the Liberation of Israel) during the early 1940s in Palestine.

- This background in militant and clandestine operations significantly influenced Ari’s worldview and subsequent career in signals intelligence.
