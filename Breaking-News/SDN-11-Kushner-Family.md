- Seryl Stadtmauer married Charles Kushner, becoming Seryl Kushner, and is the mother of Jared, Joshua, Marc, and Dara Kushner.

- Through this marriage, the Stadtmauer line is integrated into the Kushner Family’s New Jersey–New York real‑estate dynasty and its later national political connections via Jared’s role in the Trump administration.

## Richard Stadtmauer

- Seryl’s brother, Richard Stadtmauer, served as second‑in‑command at Kushner Companies, effectively making him a top executive within the Kushner Family’s property empire rather than head of a distinct Stadtmauer conglomerate.

# Convicted in Newark, NJ

## 2008-06-03

Richard Stadtmauer, then 49 and vice chairman/managing partner of Kushner Companies, was convicted by a federal jury in Newark, New Jersey.

He was found guilty of one count of conspiracy to defraud the United States, relating to a scheme to manipulate partnership tax returns for Kushner Companies real-estate entities between 1997 and 2003.

The jury also convicted him on eight counts of aiding and assisting in the filing of false tax returns, while acquitting him on nine additional aiding-and-assisting counts.

The case centered on improperly claiming personal and other non-deductible expenditures as business expenses on partnership returns, thereby reducing reported taxable income and tax liability.

Evidence showed that expenditures unrelated to the partnerships’ business, including personal expenses of Charles Kushner and Richard Stadtmauer, were falsely characterized as deductible business costs.

The conspiracy involved multiple partnerships managed by Kushner Companies, and Stadtmauer was portrayed as a senior participant who reviewed financial statements and signed tax returns despite the improper deductions.

At the time of this conviction, federal authorities described him as the “second-in-command" at Kushner Companies and the seventh individual convicted in connection with the broader Kushner tax fraud scheme.

Under the statutes of conviction, he faced a statutory maximum sentence of five years in prison on the conspiracy count and three years for each of the eight false-return counts, plus up to $250,000 in fines per count.

Guideline calculations referenced by prosecutors suggested an advisory range of roughly six to seven years in prison before judicial discretion and later adjustments.

## 2009-02-08

In a subsequent written sentencing memorandum and guideline ruling, the U.S. District Court detailed that Stadtmauer had been convicted after a two‑month jury trial on the conspiracy count and eight false-return counts, with nine similar counts resulting in acquittals.

The court described the scheme as involving more than $6 million in improper deductions across twelve Kushner Companies partnerships for tax years approximately 1998–2001, including capital expenditures improperly deducted in full in the year incurred.

The court determined a tax loss figure for guideline purposes in the range of roughly $1.7–1.8 million attributable to improper deductions linked to Stadtmauer’s conduct, after analyzing categories such as capital expenditures, personal expenses, and other misclassified costs.

The sentencing opinion noted that the government’s case relied in part on co-conspirators’ plea agreements and on detailed IRS agent analyses of the partnerships’ books and returns.

The court applied the then-current Sentencing Guidelines (effective November 1, 2008), ultimately finding an offense level and criminal-history category corresponding to a guideline range of approximately 46–57 months’ imprisonment.

The judge emphasized that acquitted conduct could still be considered in calculating tax loss for guideline purposes if proven by a preponderance of the evidence, and used this principle to include some items tied to counts on which Stadtmauer had been acquitted.

## 2009

(Sentencing – specific date not detailed in sources)

Contemporaneous reporting states that Stadtmauer was ultimately sentenced to approximately 38 months in federal prison, a term below the upper guideline range but still reflecting the seriousness of the tax fraud scheme.

The sentencing included potential monetary penalties (fines of up to $250,000 per count under the statutes) and obligations for restitution/tax repayment determined through related IRS civil processes, though exact restitution sums were handled separately from the criminal judgment.

Media coverage characterized him as a key figure in the Kushner tax scandal, noting that his conviction came several years after Charles Kushner’s earlier conviction and sentence for related false-return and witness-retaliation conduct.

His conviction and sentence effectively ended his formal leadership role at Kushner Companies and marked him in later descriptions and lawsuits as a “New Jersey convict" tied to the Kushner tax-fraud affair.

# Charles' 2004 Criminal Plea

Charles Kushner’s 2004 criminal plea and related civil settlements marked the start of the Kushner Companies tax-fraud prosecutions, predating Richard Stadtmauer’s 2008 conviction by several years.

## Criminal Plea Details

### August 18, 2004

Charles Kushner pleaded guilty in U.S. District Court in Newark to 16 counts of assisting in filing false tax returns, one count of retaliating against a cooperating witness (his sister), and one count of making false statements to the Federal Election Commission.

For the tax counts, he admitted directing Kushner Companies partnerships to falsely claim over $1 million in charitable contributions as deductible office expenses, causing IRS losses of $200,000–$325,000 across tax years roughly 1998–2001.

On witness retaliation, he confessed to hiring a private investigator for $25,000 to arrange a prostitute seducing his brother-in-law (the witness's husband), secretly filming it, and mailing the tape to intimidate the witness.

### March 4, 2005

U.S. District Judge Jose L. Linares sentenced Kushner to the maximum 24 months in federal prison under his plea agreement, plus a $40,000 fine, calling the crimes "disgraceful and reprehensible."

He was ordered to surrender by May 9, 2005, and served about 16 months before early release.

## Civil FEC Settlement

### June 30, 2004

In a separate FEC conciliation agreement, Kushner and 40 Kushner Companies partnerships paid a $508,900 civil penalty for illegal contributions exceeding $500,000 (1997–2000) attributed to partners without their knowledge or consent.

Kushner, as managing partner, selected recipients, signed checks, and directed staff to send them on company letterhead, violating partnership contribution rules and individual limits.

## Civil IRS Tax Settlement

No specific standalone civil IRS settlement amount or date for Charles Kushner appears distinctly in the sources reviewed here

- tax-related civil recovery typically flowed through IRS audits tied to the criminal case.

Prosecutors in later cases (like Stadtmauer’s) referenced Kushner’s guilty plea and cooperation as influencing tax-loss calculations, but his personal civil tax repayment was handled administratively post-sentencing alongside criminal penalties.

# Key Accountants

Stanley Bekritsky and Anne Amici were key accountants tied to Kushner Companies' external auditing firm, directly implicated in the tax fraud scheme.

## Stanley Bekritsky

### **Professional Role**

Bekritsky served as the head tax partner at Schonbraun Safris McCann & Bekritsky (SSMB), the Roseland, NJ-based accounting firm that acted as outside accountants for Kushner Companies' real estate partnerships from the late 1990s.

He oversaw tax return preparation for multiple Kushner partnerships (1997–2002), working closely with Marci Plotkin (KC's lead accountant at SSMB) on financial statements and partnership filings.

### **As Cooperating Witness**

Bekritsky pleaded guilty to conspiring to defraud the U.S. by aiding false partnership tax returns (e.g., mischaracterizing $6M+ in charitable/political contributions, capital expenditures, and personal expenses as deductible business costs).

## Anne Amici

He testified against Richard Stadtmauer at the 2008 trial, stating Stadtmauer "knew" of "problems" with the returns and participated in their approval/signing; this helped secure Stadtmauer's conviction on conspiracy and eight false-return counts.

### **Professional Role**

Amici was a staff accountant at SSMB (age 46 during 2008 proceedings), working almost exclusively on Kushner Companies matters under Marci Plotkin.

### **As Cooperating Witness**

Amici was indicted alongside Stadtmauer, Bekritsky, and Plotkin for conspiring to defraud the U.S. via false tax returns (1998–2001).

She pleaded guilty to the conspiracy charge but did not testify at Stadtmauer's trial (unlike Bekritsky); her plea agreement and related documents provided evidence of the scheme's mechanics, supporting the government's case on tax-loss calculations (~$1.7–1.8M attributable to Stadtmauer).

She assisted in preparing external financial statements and tax returns for KC partnerships like Oakwood Garden Developers, Elmwood Village, and Westminster Management, spreading improper deductions across schedules (e.g., Schedule M-2 "Other Rental Expenses").

# Platinum Partners

## 2003

Platinum Partners was a New York-based hedge fund founded in 2003, notorious for a massive fraud scheme involving asset overvaluation, Ponzi-like payments, and liquidity concealment, primarily targeting high-net-worth individuals from New York’s Orthodox Jewish community.

Platinum Partners founded by Mark Nordlicht (chief investment officer) and Murray Huberfeld (co-founder), promising steady 17% annual returns through illiquid investments like oil/gas stakes.

## 2010–2012

Fund lent tens of millions to risky borrowers (e.g., electricity resellers, Black Elk Energy oil firm)

- Black Elk platform explosion (Nov 2012) killed 3 workers, tanked production, but Platinum continued overvaluing it at $280M (nearly 40% of assets).

## 2012–2015

Liquidity crisis deepened as oil prices fell

- executives allegedly hid losses via high-interest loans, new investor money to pay redemptions (Ponzi-style), and false reporting of 17% returns while collecting $100M+ fees.

## 2015-12

Nordlicht emailed Huberfeld about fleeing to Israel (“Assume we are not coming back to NY…Take passport") as fund neared collapse

- Nordlicht considered $7.5M home equity loan to prop it up.

## 2016-04

Reuters exposed Platinum’s high-risk loans and red flags, noting reluctance from major institutions.

## 2016-11-12

Huberfeld arrested for bribery scheme: paid NYC corrections union head $100K–$150K/year to steer $20M pension fund to Platinum.

## 2016-12-19

DOJ/SEC indicted Nordlicht, Huberfeld, and 5 others (including Platinum CFOs David Levy, Uri Landesman) on $1B securities fraud/conspiracy

- fund entered liquidation with $1.7B reported assets sold for $89M.

## 2017+

Nordlicht/Huberfeld faced ongoing trials

- fund liquidated, investors recovered pennies on the dollar.
