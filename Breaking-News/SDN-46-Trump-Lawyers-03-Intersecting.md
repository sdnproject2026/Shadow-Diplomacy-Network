## Legal Risks to Trump 

if Coerced Law Firms Are Compelled to Produce Records

### No Executive Shield

- While the White House can assert executive privilege over internal West Wing or Department of Justice deliberations, private law firms cannot assert executive privilege on their own behalf to block a congressional subpoena

### The "Associates" Dragnet

- Raskin, Blumenthal, and Schiff explicitly demanded records of requests made by any public or private associate of the President, meaning that if a settling firm performed legal work, crisis management, or hush-money or NDA structuring for a private Trump ally or business entity to satisfy its "pro bono" quota, those records sit outside government firewalls

### Piercing Attorney-Client Privilege 

- via the Crime-Fraud Exception and Third-Party Waiver

### Ambiguous Client Relationships

- Attorney-client privilege requires a formal, confidential attorney-client relationship for the purpose of obtaining legal advice, so because the 2025 settlements were unwritten political deals announced on Truth Social where Trump acted as the "sole judge" of compliance, communications routed through political intermediaries (such as Boris Epshteyn) or shared with the White House to prove compliance risk third-party waiver of privilege

### The Crime-Fraud Exception

- Lawmakers have characterized the arrangements as potential federal bribery (18 US C. 201), extortion under color of official right, and violations of the Antideficiency Act (31 US C. 1342), meaning that if a court or grand jury finds probable cause that legal services were solicited or used to further a crime or fraud (such as unlawful hush-money concealment, tax evasion, or obstruction of an investigation), the crime-fraud exception strips away both attorney-client privilege and work-product protection

## Wide vs. Narrow Discovery

### Congressional and Grand Jury Discovery 

(Wide Scope)

- Unlike civil plaintiffs bound by Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 26(b)(1) proportionality limits, congressional committees and federal grand juries operate under an extremely wide investigative scope and can compel raw metadata, conflict-check logs, timekeeper narratives, and internal firm emails showing who directed specific tasks, why certain matters were accepted or rejected, and whether any work touched on DOJ document redactions or Epstein-related records

### Civil and Criminal Trial Discovery 

(Targeted Spillover)

- Once congressional committees place law firm billing records or correspondence into the public record, private litigants, such as Epstein survivors suing financial institutions or estates or plaintiffs challenging executive orders, can use those public disclosures to establish relevance for targeted civil discovery (Rule 45 subpoenas) against the firms, while state bar disciplinary committees (such as the New York Appellate Division) can compel document production independent of federal DOJ control

### Corroborating Obstruction Patterns

Obstruction and Financial Trails

- If subpoenaed firm records reveal efforts to use private counsel to bypass congressional oversight or manage sensitive financial or NDA disputes for Trump associates, prosecutors or investigators could pair those records with Deputy AG Todd Blanche's documented intervention in the DEA's Operation "Chain Reaction" inquiry to build a broader pattern-of-obstruction narrative

### Anti-Money Laundering 

(AML) and Tax Referrals

- Because top law firms maintain strict Know-Your-Customer (KYC) and anti-money laundering onboarding files, any representation involving international investment vehicles (such as the 550 billion dollar Japanese fund Kirkland worked on) or private wealth transfers would contain internal compliance memos that could trigger Treasury or FinCEN or IRS scrutiny if turned over to Senate Finance investigators

## Answers

### Crime-Fraud Exception 

- Attorney-client privilege requires a confidential relationship formed to obtain legitimate legal advice, which can be waived if communications are routed through political intermediaries or shared with the White House to prove settlement compliance

- If a court or grand jury finds probable cause that legal services were used to further bribery, extortion, unlawful hush-money concealment, tax evasion, or obstruction, the crime-fraud exception strips away privilege and work-product protections

### Senate Finance Findings 

- Senator Wyden's investigation documented that Leon Black paid Jeffrey Epstein 170 million dollars between 2012 and 2017, including 100 million dollars without a written contract and 10 million dollars through a sham charity to obscure hush-money payments and evade taxes

- Unsealed emails showed former Paul, Weiss chairman Brad Karp coordinated surveillance and inquiries with Epstein targeting a woman receiving payments from Black, and Paul, Weiss previously represented Black in submissions to Wyden's committee

### Limits of Executive Privilege 

- Executive privilege applies to internal executive branch deliberations and cannot be asserted independently by private third-party law firms to resist congressional subpoenas

- Because lawmakers demanded records of work requested by any public or private associate of the President, legal services or NDA structuring performed for private allies sit outside government privilege shields

### Congressional vs. Civil Discovery Scope

- Congressional committees and federal grand juries operate under a wide investigative scope unrestricted by civil proportionality rules, allowing them to compel raw metadata, conflict logs, and internal firm emails

- Once congressional disclosures enter the public record, private litigants can use them to establish relevance for narrower, targeted civil discovery under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 45

### DEA Operation Chain Reaction

- A May 18, 2015 DEA OCDETF memorandum cited by Senator Wyden revealed that Epstein and 14 unnamed co-conspirators were investigated under Operation "Chain Reaction" for illegitimate wire transfers tied to drug and prostitution activities

- Senator Wyden reported that Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, Trump's former personal defense attorney, intervened to block the DEA from producing unredacted versions of those records to the Senate Finance Committee
