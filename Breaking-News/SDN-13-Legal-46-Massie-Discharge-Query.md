## What a Discharge Petition Does

A discharge petition is a House procedure for attempting to remove legislation from committee consideration and bring it before the House despite the normal committee/leadership process.

The critical threshold is:

218 signatures = majority of the full House membership.

Reaching 218 does not itself enact H.R. 9694. It moves the measure into the House's discharge procedure and toward consideration.

## What H.R. 9694 Would Do

H.R. 9694 is not simply another demand for DOJ to publish documents.

Among its provisions, the legislation would establish legal mechanisms involving:

- State attorneys general and certain state officials challenging DOJ withholding.

- Epstein victims challenging withholding involving covered records.

- Members of Congress challenging DOJ compliance.

- Victims obtaining records concerning themselves and the harm they suffered.

- Criminal penalties concerning knowing concealment, destruction, falsification or withholding of covered records.

- Provision of certain records to state and local prosecutors.

- Limitations on DOJ's ability to rely on certain privileges to avoid disclosure requirements.

## The Trump-Johnson Allegation

The viral claim goes further than the available evidence.

There is evidence that:

1. Massie launched the new discharge petition.

2. Johnson changed the House's September schedule.

3. Massie publicly accused the scheduling decision of obstructing his Epstein-files legislation.

4. Johnson had previously rejected suggestions that House leadership would keep lawmakers away from Washington to prevent an Epstein vote.

However, there is no credible evidence establishing that Trump personally ordered Johnson to shut down the House because Trump feared what the remaining Epstein files might reveal.

That portion should therefore be described as an unsubstantiated allegation, rather than as an established fact.

## DOJ Records Dispute

The larger dispute concerns how much Epstein-related material remains withheld.

Massie has argued that substantial numbers of Epstein-related records remain unavailable.

The Justice Department has maintained that its handling of the records complies with applicable legal requirements and that withheld material can include categories such as privileged, duplicative, exempt or otherwise non-publishable material.

The disagreement over the amount and legal status of withheld material is separate from the question of whether Trump personally ordered Johnson to alter the House schedule.

## Important Legal Distinction

The appearance of a person's name in Epstein-related records does not, standing alone, establish:

- criminal conduct;

- participation in Epstein's crimes;

- knowledge of Epstein's crimes;

- or liability for any offense.

Records can contain names for many different reasons, including correspondence, contact information, travel records, testimony, investigative references or other incidental connections.

Petition remains above 100 but below 218.

The claim that Trump personally ordered Johnson to keep Congress away from Washington because Trump fears the Epstein files is not established by the evidence currently available.
