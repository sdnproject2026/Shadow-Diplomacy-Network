# Lack Of Teeth

The central structural weakness identified in the article is that the commission appears to depend on voluntary cooperation unless subpoena authority, enforcement mechanisms, and judicial recognition are clearly established.

If New Mexico lawmakers conclude that the commission's current authority is insufficient, several plausible legislative and institutional responses could follow.

# Followup Legislative

## Enabling Statute

A likely next step would be enactment of a dedicated statute rather than reliance on a House resolution or temporary committee authority.

Possible provisions:

- Formal statutory creation of the commission

- Defined investigative jurisdiction

- Defined subject matter authority

- Explicit subpoena authority

- Authority to compel document production

- Authority to compel witness attendance

- Statutory standing to seek court enforcement

- Defined penalties for noncompliance

The principal objective would be eliminating ambiguity regarding the commission's legal status.

## Most Important Drafting Objective

The single most important objective is reducing ambiguity.

The statute would ideally answer:

- Who can be investigated

- What records can be obtained

## Greater Investigative Power

Rather than attempting to create extraordinary authority, the politically and legally durable approach would likely be:

- Statutory commission creation

- Explicit subpoena authority

- Express district court enforcement mechanism

- Mandatory state agency cooperation

- Strong evidence preservation requirements

- Survivor confidentiality protections

- Formal referral pathways to prosecutors

That model is more likely to survive judicial scrutiny and produce usable evidence than a statute attempting to create unusually broad or unchecked investigative powers.

## Judicial Enforcement Statute

A second measure could establish a streamlined enforcement process.

Possible provisions:

- Direct filing authority in state district court

- Expedited hearing schedules

- Mandatory judicial review deadlines

- Authority for courts to issue compliance orders

- Civil contempt remedies

- Attorney fee recovery for successful enforcement actions

This approach would focus less on subpoena issuance and more on making subpoenas enforceable.

## Preservation Order Legislation

One risk in historical investigations is destruction or loss of evidence.

Possible provisions:

- Mandatory preservation duties

- Record retention requirements

- Litigation hold obligations

- Preservation notices to public agencies

- Preservation notices to private entities

- Civil penalties for spoliation

Such legislation could preserve evidence before litigation over production occurs.

## Survivor Protection Measures

Legislators may seek to encourage participation.

Potential provisions:

- Confidential testimony procedures

- Closed session survivor hearings

- Anti-retaliation protections

- Expanded victim privacy protections

- Witness support services

- Trauma-informed investigative protocols

These measures could increase voluntary cooperation even where compulsory authority remains limited.

# Court Support

## Clarifying Standing

One recurring obstacle for investigatory bodies is proving standing to seek judicial relief.

A future statute could expressly provide:

- Standing to enforce subpoenas

- Standing to seek preservation orders

- Standing to seek declaratory relief

- Standing to challenge noncompliance

Such language reduces procedural disputes before merits questions are reached.

## Expedited Review Framework

Rather than attempting to influence judicial outcomes, legislators could create procedural pathways for rapid review.

Possible provisions:

- Priority docket treatment

- Statutory hearing deadlines

- Accelerated appeals

- Immediate review of subpoena disputes

The purpose would be speed rather than outcome control.

## Building Favorable Case Law

The lawful method for establishing supportive precedent would be careful statutory drafting followed by judicial validation.

A likely sequence:

- Legislature grants explicit authority

- Commission issues narrowly tailored subpoenas

- Courts uphold authority

- Subsequent courts rely on precedent

- Enforcement becomes increasingly predictable

This is distinct from attempting to influence particular judges or cases.

# Plausible Legislative Model

A legislature seeking maximum investigative capability could create:

- Permanent statutory commission

- Independent subpoena authority

- Expedited district court enforcement

- Preservation order authority

- Dedicated investigative staff

- Dedicated legal counsel

- Survivor confidentiality protections

- Mandatory state agency cooperation

- Referral authority to prosecutors

- Annual public reporting requirements

Even under such a model, major limitations would remain regarding:

- Federal agencies

- Federal privileges

- Classified information

- Out-of-state witnesses

- Constitutional protections

- Attorney-client privilege

- Fifth Amendment assertions

Accordingly, the most realistic path to a more effective commission is not unlimited compulsion power, but a combination of clearer statutory authority, faster judicial enforcement, stronger preservation requirements, and closer coordination with existing investigative and prosecutorial institutions.

# Best Practices 

The following is a policy and governance analysis, not a prediction and not legal advice. It assumes the objective is a lawful, evidence-driven investigation of alleged criminal conduct and, where supported by admissible evidence, subsequent prosecution through existing New Mexico institutions.

## Key Principle

The most effective path is generally:

- Preserve evidence first

- Establish investigative jurisdiction clearly

- Separate fact-finding from prosecution

- Build evidentiary records before public accusations

- Coordinate among agencies while preserving independence

- Avoid procedural shortcuts that could jeopardize later prosecutions
