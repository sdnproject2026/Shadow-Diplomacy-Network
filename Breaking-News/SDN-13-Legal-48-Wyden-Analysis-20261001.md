# Wyden Acusations

## Concealing An FBI FD-302

### 2026-10-01

- Wyden accused the Justice Department and FBI of concealing an FBI FD-302 witness-interview report involving a banker connected to Epstein

- Wyden said the missing report suggested that the FBI may possess additional related 302 reports that have not been released

- He demanded all 302 reports from investigations involving Epstein and his associates, including interviews with employees of JPMorgan Chase, Deutsche Bank, and Bank of America

- Wyden linked the request to his August report alleging that the three banks failed to timely report at least $1.4 billion in suspicious Epstein transactions

- A federal judge ordered the Justice Department to submit handwritten FBI notes underlying four 302 reports for private review in a transparency lawsuit filed by journalist Katie Phang

- A House discharge petition intended to address perceived shortcomings in the Epstein Files Transparency Act had 216 signatures, with two additional commitments needed to reach the 218 signatures required to force a House vote

## Timeline

### 2022-06

- Sen. Ron Wyden began an inquiry into Leon Black’s financial relationship with Jeffrey Epstein, asking why Black had paid Epstein $158 million for tax and estate advice

### 2023

- Committee investigators examined a transaction devised by Epstein that was intended to help Black avoid more than $1 billion in future gift and estate taxes

- Black’s representatives acknowledged that the IRS had not audited the transaction or related trusts

### 2024-02

- Bipartisan Senate Finance Committee staff reviewed suspicious-activity reports that banks had filed concerning Epstein in a Treasury Department reading room

### 2025-03

- Wyden publicly released information about Black’s settlement with the U.S. Virgin Islands

- Black paid $62 million for immunity from criminal prosecution in the territory

- The agreement stated that money Black paid Epstein was used in part to fund Epstein’s operations in the Virgin Islands

- Wyden put Black’s total payments to Epstein at $170 million and said a major bank waited seven years to report the payments to Treasury

- Wyden asked Attorney General Pam Bondi, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, and FBI Director Kash Patel for records

### 2025-07-07

- The Justice Department and FBI announced the end of their Epstein-case review

- Investigators said they found no evidence that would support an investigation of uncharged third parties and expected no further charges

### 2025-07-17

- Wyden described Treasury records showing 4,725 wire transfers totaling nearly $1.1 billion through a single Epstein account

- He said the records also included payments processed by Russian banks that are now under U.S. sanctions

- Wyden argued that the transfers represented potential lines of investigation

### 2025-12

- Wyden and four other Democratic senators asked Bondi and Patel why federal investigators reportedly had not questioned Darren Indyke and Richard Kahn

- Indyke, Epstein’s longtime lawyer, and Kahn, his accountant, held signing authority over Epstein’s bank accounts and serve as co-executors of his estate

- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had declined Wyden’s requests for Treasury’s full Epstein file three times during 2025

### 2026-01

- A final batch of Epstein-related files was released

- Todd Blanche rejected the suggestion that the Justice Department was declining to prosecute known individuals

- Wyden expanded his inquiry to Bank of New York Mellon

- A 2019 BNY filing showed Epstein had moved $378 million through 270 wire transfers that the bank could not associate with a legitimate business purpose

- BNY reported the transactions to Treasury more than a decade after they occurred

### 2026-03

- Wyden sent Leon Black another set of questions based on newly released Epstein records

- Wyden alleged that Black paid Epstein at rates 30 times higher than those charged by Black’s other elite tax advisers

- Wyden said $10 million in payments went through a charity in an arrangement intended to avoid public disclosure

- He also cited emails indicating that Black paid women through Epstein and alleged that Epstein shared information about women on Black’s payroll with Russian government officials

- Senate Republicans objected when Wyden sought unanimous consent to pass a bill compelling Treasury to release its Epstein bank records

- Wyden said Blanche prevented the Drug Enforcement Administration from providing an unredacted 2015 memo concerning a multiagency investigation into Epstein and 14 other people over wire transfers connected to drug and prostitution activity

### 2026-04

- Black’s lawyers responded to Wyden’s inquiries by arguing that no other private citizen had received as many written requests from Wyden during the same period

### 2026-06

- Wyden referred findings on Black’s Epstein ties to House Oversight Committee leaders James Comer and Robert Garcia

- Wyden urged the committee to question Black about the amount of his payments to Epstein, the U.S. Virgin Islands settlement, and payments to women

- Black appeared voluntarily before the committee on June 26

- Black left after members questioned him about nondisclosure agreements with women

- Comer served Black with subpoenas for a deposition and for the agreements

### 2026-08

- Wyden issued an 18-page staff memo on JPMorgan Chase’s relationship with Epstein

- The memo said JPMorgan reported just over $4.3 million in Epstein transactions while he was alive, then later filed retroactive reports covering nearly $1.3 billion in transactions dating to 2003

- Wyden released the report “Looking the Other Way,” which concluded that JPMorgan, Deutsche Bank, and Bank of America likely violated federal anti-money-laundering law

- The report said Bank of America accounts were used by Black to send about $170 million to Epstein between 2012 and 2017

- It said Black supplied roughly 90 percent of Southern Trust’s revenue from 2013 through 2017

- The report said Deutsche Bank later identified more than $250 million in suspicious Epstein transfers, including payments to women in Russia and Eastern Europe

- The report identified 13 bankers across JPMorgan, Deutsche Bank, and Bank of America whose conduct it said warranted investigation

- JPMorgan disputed the report’s conclusions, Deutsche Bank said it regretted its prior relationship with Epstein, and Bank of America said it did not facilitate wrongdoing

- A federal judge approved Bank of America’s $72.5 million settlement with Epstein survivors

- JPMorgan and Deutsche Bank had previously paid $290 million and $75 million, respectively, to settle related civil suits

### 2026-09-03

- Black did not appear for a scheduled House Oversight Committee deposition

- He sued the committee, contending that its subpoenas exceeded its authority

### 2026-09-15

- The House Oversight Committee voted 41-0 to recommend holding Black in contempt of Congress

### 2026-09-16

- The House approved the contempt recommendation by unanimous consent and referred the matter to the Justice Department

- The Justice Department said the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia would review the referral

- Black denied knowing about Epstein’s crimes, and his lawyers said he had provided the only confidentiality agreement of which Epstein was aware
