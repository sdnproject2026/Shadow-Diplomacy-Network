# Bear Stearns

## Special Products Group 

(SPG)

The unsealed 2026 files concerning the Bear Stearns "Special Products" Group (SPG) provide a granular look at the high-risk securitization practices and internal risk-management failures leading up to the 2008 financial crisis.

These documents, released following the Epstein Files Transparency Act (EFTA) and subsequent Department of Justice disclosures in early 2026, focus on the firm's consolidated supervised entity (CSE) status and its aggressive mortgage-backed securities (MBS) pipeline.

The content types within these unsealed files primarily consist of internal executive emails, underwriting guideline exceptions, regulatory application assessments, and proprietary mathematical risk models.

The files highlight a systematic shift where internal "Special Products" were prioritized for volume over credit quality, often with the direct authorization of senior executives.

### November 15, 2005

Internal assessment from the SEC Division of Market Regulation regarding Bear Stearns' application to be a consolidated supervised entity (CSE).

The files include reviews of mathematical risk models and internal management controls that critics argue were insufficient to handle the "Special Products" being developed.

### March 14, 2008

Emergency internal communications detailing the "run on the bank" and the collapse of the liquidity pools supporting the Special Products Group.

These files reveal the rapid evaporation of repo market funding for the firm’s mortgage-backed assets.

### July 31, 2013 

(Reference Date for Litigation Discovery)

Unsealed deposition transcripts from senior executives regarding the 179-page response to FHFA lawsuits.

The files describe the "Special Products" unit's hunger for new loans to securitize, which led to a documented abandonment of rigorous underwriting standards to maintain the volume of mortgage pools.

### December 19, 2025

The initial release of Department of Justice materials under the EFTA, which included historical Bear Stearns personnel records and internal memos regarding "Special Products" partners.

This tranche focused on the firm's financial relationships during the early 2000s expansion of the SPG.

### January 30, 2026

The final major production of approximately three million documents by the Department of Justice.

These files contain specific mentions of the Bear Stearns "Special Products" pipeline, executive signatures on SEC filings for $8.7 billion in residential mortgage-backed securities, and internal directives to withhold fees from credit rating agencies that downgraded the firm's bonds.

## SPG Mechanism

### The "Blind Escrow" Protocol

In the 2026 testimony, the process was described as follows.

### 1. Isolation

- A shell entity (often an SPV) was created for a specific transaction.

### 2. Opacity

### 3. Deniability

- Funds were disbursed based on "performance milestones" defined in private side-letters that were never filed with the SEC or the firm’s main compliance office.
