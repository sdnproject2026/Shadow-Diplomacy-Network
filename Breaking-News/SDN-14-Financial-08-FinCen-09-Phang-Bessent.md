## October 28, 2026

Three-judge panel of the US Court of Appeals for the DC Circuit rejected an emergency request from US Attorney General Todd Blanche to pause the review and production of foreign-language documents in the Jeffrey Epstein files.

This ruling represents a major legal victory for attorney and journalist Katie Phang, who sued the Department of Justice (DOJ) to force compliance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act.

### The Appeals Court Ruling

The emergency stay request was evaluated by Chief Judge Sri Srinivasan, Circuit Judge Cornelia Pillard, and Circuit Judge Florence Pan.

### No Irreparable Harm

- The judges dismantled the Trump administration's DOJ arguments, writing that the government

> "failed to show that it is irreparably harmed by merely initiating review of foreign-language documents".

### Review vs. Release

- The court adopted a central argument from Phang's legal team, noting that starting a standard review process to determine what can legally be disclosed is not equivalent to an improper or premature release of sensitive materials.

### Scope of Appeal

- Notably, AG Blanche only sought an emergency stay on the foreign-language portion of the district court's preliminary injunction.

- He did not appeal the orders regarding handwritten FBI interview notes or improperly redacted emails.

### Katie Phang's Analysis

"The Big Tell"

Following the decision, Katie Phang broke down the ruling on her show, highlighting that the DOJ's intense resistance to reviewing these international files signals deeper problems within the department.

Phang noted that the DOJ's efforts to stonewall international law enforcement agencies and fight the review is

> "a big tell that there is something there that they don't want to turn over."

### Background of the Lawsuit

Phang originally filed the lawsuit (_Phang vs. Blanche_) in April 2026, accusing the DOJ of violating the Epstein Files Transparency Act.

The law, passed by Congress in late 2025, mandated the creation of a public, searchable database of all unclassified DOJ files regarding Epstein and his associates.

Phang's suit alleged that the DOJ missed critical deadlines, applied unlawful redactions, and withheld crucial records, including materials referencing President Donald Trump.

The DC District Court judge overseeing the case, Judge Emmet G.

Sullivan, has repeatedly expressed frustration with the DOJ's compliance delays.

He previously ordered Blanche to personally deliver unredacted versions of disputed email chains and handwritten FBI notes directly to his chambers for private judicial review.

## Rejected Delay Tactics

With the appeals court rejecting the DOJ's delay tactics, the government is legally required to move forward with reviewing and processing the foreign-language materials while the broader appeal plays out.

## Local Standing

Foreign judicial authorities, financial intelligence units (FIUs), and law enforcement bodies in host jurisdictions where suspect banks (eg, JPMorgan Chase, Deutsche Bank, Bank of America) maintain local branches, subsidiaries, or domestic accounts possess independent legal standing to initiate local asset-impoundment and freezing actions.

### Option to Impound Accounts

- Foreign jurisdictions can bypass federal DOJ stonewalling by exercising domestic judicial powers to impound, restrain, or freeze accounts maintained within their borders by multi-national institutions.

### Criteria for Foreign Impoundment Actions

Dual Criminality Standard

- Demonstrating that the underlying activity (money laundering, human trafficking, public corruption, or terror financing) constitutes a recognized predicate offense within the host country's criminal code.

Prima Facie Cause and Reasonable Suspicion

- Establishing reasonable grounds to suspect that specific local client accounts or correspondent balances are derived from, or were used as instrumentalities of, illicit multi-jurisdictional flows.

FATF Recommendation 4 Compliance

- Adopting temporary provisional measures to prevent the dissipation, transfer, or conversion of criminal assets during ongoing investigations.

### Methods of Execution

In-Country Administrative Freeze Orders

- National FIUs or judicial magistrates issue immediate ex parte freeze directives directly to domestic bank branches.

Non-Conviction-Based (NCB) Asset Forfeiture

- Exercising civil in rem jurisdiction to impound funds tied to systemic money-laundering failures without requiring a prior U.S. criminal conviction.

Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty (MLAT) Invocations

- Foreign governments lodge formal MLAT requests to compel U.S. financial institutions to preserve and produce corresponding transaction records held in local subsidiaries.
