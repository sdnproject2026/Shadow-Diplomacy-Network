# Heritage Foundation

## 1973  

The Heritage Foundation is founded on February 16, 1973, in Washington, D.C., by conservative activists Paul Weyrich and Edwin Feulner with major seed funding from beer magnate Joseph Coors; it is set up as a 501(c)(3) think tank to be a conservative counterpart to institutions like the Brookings Institution, focused on pro-business, anti-communist policy and traditionalist social positions.

From its founding period onward, the Heritage Foundation articulates a core agenda of free enterprise, limited government, individual freedom, traditional American values, and strong national defense, which later becomes the ideological backbone shared across many of the organizations in your list.

## 1980s  

During the early 1980s, The Heritage Foundation becomes a central policy shop for the Reagan administration, with its Mandate for Leadership blueprint heavily used to staff and orient executive-branch policy, cementing Heritage’s role as a hub coordinating conservative policy networks and influencing later institutional allies like Claremont Institute and Competitive Enterprise Institute, which share pro-market and socially conservative priorities.

## 1984  

The Competitive Enterprise Institute (CEI) is founded in 1984 as a free-market public policy organization focused on deregulation, limited government, and challenging environmental and other federal regulations, aligning its mission with Heritage’s long-standing emphasis on free enterprise and small government, and positioning itself as a specialized ally within the broader conservative think-tank ecosystem.

## 1979–1990s

The Claremont Institute is established (late 1970s) by scholars associated with Claremont McKenna College and the Claremont Graduate School, promoting a Straussian, “founding principles” reading of the U.S. Constitution; over time, it becomes a key intellectual partner in the same ideological space as Heritage, stressing natural rights, nationalism, and a strong executive aligned with conservative cultural priorities, which feeds into later collaborations like Project 2025’s advisory structure.

## 1990s–2000s

By the 1990s and 2000s, Heritage, Claremont Institute, and CEI function as parts of a shared conservative infrastructure: Heritage provides broad policy and political strategy, Claremont supplies constitutional and philosophical justifications for a robust conservative presidency and culture war positions, and CEI concentrates on regulatory rollback in areas such as energy, environment, and business; overlapping donors, personnel circulation among Republican administrations, and joint coalitions on issues like deregulation and judicial appointments deepen their inter-relations.

## 2010  

In April 2010, Heritage launches Heritage Action for America as a 501(c)(4) advocacy arm to translate its think-tank research into direct political pressure, campaigning, and scorecards, a model that parallels and complements the issue-advocacy roles of CEI and Claremont-affiliated activists in pushing Republican officials toward more hardline conservative positions.

## 2019  

Former HUD Secretary Ben Carson founds the American Cornerstone Institute (ACI) after leaving government, positioning it as a conservative think tank emphasizing faith, liberty, community, and life; ACI quickly integrates into existing Heritage-led coalitions, drawing on similar donor networks and overlapping Trump-era personnel, and adopts an agenda consistent with Heritage and Claremont on limited government, pro-life policy, and culture issues.

## 2023  

Project 2025 emerges publicly as a Heritage Foundation–led presidential transition project, designed to produce a comprehensive policy and personnel roadmap for the next conservative administration, including the multi-hundred-page Mandate for Leadership volume and a large database of vetted conservative staff; it formalizes Heritage’s role as coordinator of a broad coalition that includes American Cornerstone Institute, Claremont Institute, and Competitive Enterprise Institute on its advisory board.

As of 2023, Project 2025 is structured as an initiative housed at The Heritage Foundation rather than a completely independent organization, sharing Heritage’s email domain and leadership oversight; its advisory board lists American Cornerstone Institute, Claremont Institute, and Competitive Enterprise Institute among partners, demonstrating explicit institutional inter-relations and a shared governing agenda.

## 2024  

By February 19, 2024, Heritage announces that Project 2025’s coalition has reached at least 100 partner organizations, including American Cornerstone Institute, Claremont Institute, and Competitive Enterprise Institute, all united under a common program to staff the next conservative administration with aligned personnel and implement a coordinated policy agenda across federal agencies.

In the 2024 Mandate for Leadership and related Project 2025 materials, the shared agenda across these entities emphasizes: large-scale rollback of federal regulatory power (especially in environment, labor, and civil rights enforcement); centralization of power in the executive branch; aggressive restriction of abortion and gender-related medical and educational policies; prioritization of fossil-fuel-friendly energy policy; and an “America First” foreign-policy posture—all framed as restoring constitutional government and traditional values.

# Shared Agendas

## Limited government 

The Heritage Foundation functions as a coordinating **hub** for the listed organizations, providing the institutional home, funding networks, and strategic direction that bind Project 2025, American Cornerstone Institute, Claremont Institute, and Competitive Enterprise Institute into a coherent coalition, especially visible in the Project 2025 advisory board and the use of Heritage’s infrastructure (email domain, branding, events).

The Competitive Enterprise Institute brings litigation, regulatory-comment, and media campaigns aimed at dismantling or weakening federal regulations, especially in environmental and consumer-protection domains; within the Project 2025 coalition, CEI’s expertise supports detailed plans for restructuring agencies like EPA, DOE, and regulatory offices across government in line with Heritage’s and Trump-aligned deregulatory goals.

## Strong executive   

Project 2025 serves as the operationalization mechanism for the coalition’s shared agenda, translating the ideological work of Heritage and Claremont (limited government, nationalism, Christian-inflected social conservatism) and the deregulatory priorities of CEI into specific personnel lists, agency-by-agency policy blueprints, and training pipelines for future appointees, with American Cornerstone Institute contributing Trump-aligned, faith-oriented messaging and policy concepts.

The Claremont Institute contributes a distinct intellectual and legal framework—often called “national conservative” or “post-liberal” by commentators—that justifies a strong presidency willing to confront perceived progressive dominance in culture and institutions, which dovetails with Project 2025’s emphasis on asserting sweeping executive control over the bureaucracy and aligning federal policy with cultural conservatism.

## Culturally Conservative   

American Cornerstone Institute, led by a high-profile Trump cabinet alumnus, gives the network direct experiential insight into the Trump administration’s inner workings and provides a bridge between grassroots religious conservatives and the more technocratic policy centers at Heritage, Claremont, and CEI, reinforcing a shared agenda of embedding socially conservative positions into executive agencies and administrative law.

Collectively, the Heritage-led network envisions a future conservative administration that uses pre-vetted personnel, detailed agency transition plans, and coordinated outside pressure to rapidly implement a long-standing conservative wish list: shrinking the administrative state, boosting fossil fuels, expanding religious and “conscience” exemptions, tightening immigration enforcement, reshaping federal civil-rights enforcement, and prioritizing culturally conservative education and family policy; the shared email domains, advisory-board memberships, and cross-appointments among these organizations reflect a tightly interlocked ecosystem rather than isolated entities.
