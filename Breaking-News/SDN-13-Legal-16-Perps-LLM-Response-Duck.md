## Senior Financial Facilitators

### Suggested legal class name

- Financial Facilitator / Co-Conspirator in Money Laundering and Obstruction

### Core factual focus

- Routing funds through complex entity structures to conceal beneficiaries and purposes

### Roles

- Creating and managing shell companies

- Approving or initiating large transfers and wire activity

- Failing to perform or falsifying KYC and AML due diligence

### Apparent intent, means, motive

- Use of opaque entities and repetitive layering to obscure source/destination of funds

- Motive to protect reputations, preserve business relationships, and avoid liability

### Prosecutorial theories

- RICO enterprise with predicates such as money laundering, wire fraud, and obstruction

- Substantive money laundering

- (18 U.S.C. 1956/1957) and conspiracy to launder

- Bank Secrecy Act violations for willful failure to file SARs

### Investigative best practices

- Obtain bank records, SWIFT/wire instructions, intermediary bank data, and KYC files

- Subpoena corporate formation and nominee records in relevant jurisdictions

- Use forensic accounting to trace layering and circular transactions

- Pursue MLATs and 1782s for offshore records

### Preservation steps

- Issue preservation letters to financial institutions and service providers

- Seek expedited holds on identified accounts and transaction records

## Corporate Principals and Executives

- Who Made or Approved Payments

### Suggested legal class name

- Corporate Officer Aider-and-Abettor / Conspirator to Obstruct and Conceal

### Core factual focus

- Executives who authorized settlements, retained counsel to negotiate NDAs, or directed payments to opaque entities

### Roles

- Signing checks or authorizing transfers

- Directing legal strategy to minimize disclosure

- Maintaining or approving payment channels to nominees

### Apparent intent, means, motive

- Conscious facilitation to avoid reputational harm or civil exposure

- Repeated approvals suggest awareness of risk and intent to conceal

### Prosecutorial theories

- Aiding-and-abetting (18 U.S.C. 2), conspiracy to conceal, obstruction, false statements, and false instruments

### Investigative best practices

- Subpoena internal emails, board minutes, approval memos, and expense records

- Seek testimony from in-house and external counsel with careful privilege analysis

- Reconstruct authorization chains and signature authorities

### Preservation steps

- Issue litigation holds and preservation notices for corporate systems and custodial employees



## Lawyers, Fixers, and Gatekeepers

- Domestic and International)

### Suggested legal class name

- Facilitator / Conspirator in Obstruction and Witness Management

### Core factual focus

- Counsel, PR operatives, and fixers who negotiated NDAs, advised on concealment, or coordinated victim communications

### Roles

- Drafting and enforcing confidentiality agreements

- Structuring releases and payment mechanisms

- Coordinating public narratives and third-party denials

### Apparent intent, means, motive

- Use of legal tools to silence victims and block disclosure indicates intent to impede exposure

- Potential motive to preserve influential relationships and financial arrangements

### Prosecutorial theories

- Obstruction of justice, conspiracy to obstruct, misprision of felony, and application of crime-fraud exception to pierce privilege

### Investigative best practices

- Seek non-privileged records and consider in camera review where crime-fraud is implicated

- Subpoena communications with intermediaries and third parties

- Use proffers or immunity to elicit testimony from lower-level participants

### Preservation steps

- Preservation letters to law firms, PR firms, and known intermediaries



## Corporate Service Providers 

- Nominee Directors

### Suggested legal class name

- Corporate Service Facilitator / Conspirator in Concealment

### Core factual focus

- Registered agents, formation companies, nominee directors, trustees, and corporate secretaries enabling opaque ownership

### Roles

- Creating entities, providing nominee officers or directors, and managing filings to mask beneficial owners

### Apparent intent, means, motive

- Repeated use of nominee services and falsified beneficial information indicates intent to shield principals

- Motive includes insulating principals from scrutiny and liability

### Prosecutorial theories

- Conspiracy to commit money laundering, aiding-and-abetting, and violations of AML/compliance obligations

### Investigative best practices

- Subpoena formation records, engagement agreements, invoices, and ID records

- Forensic review of filing patterns and reuse of nominee details

- MLAT requests for offshore service providers

### Preservation steps

- Preserve corporate formation files and nominee engagement records



## Trusted Social Circle

- Friends, Associates, and Hosts

### Suggested legal class name

- Social Facilitator / Conspirator in Trafficking and Obstruction

### Core factual focus

- Individuals providing travel, residences, introductions, venues, or hospitality facilitating access

### Roles

- Providing private aircraft, properties, and hosting events

- Arranging travel and logistics for principals and guests

### Apparent intent, means, motive

- Regular provision of access and resources with knowledge of risks suggests facilitation or willful blindness

- Motive includes social capital, reciprocal benefits, or business advantages

### Prosecutorial theories

- Conspiracy and aiding-and-abetting for trafficking or transportation offenses

- Travel-related statutes for interstate or international facilitation of unlawful sexual activity

### Investigative best practices

- Collect flight logs, charter contracts, property staff communications, reservation records, and guest lists

- Correlate travel itineraries with transactional and phone location data

### Preservation steps

- Preservation notices to charter companies, pilots, property managers, and hospitality vendors



## Financial Beneficiaries 

- Investors in Epstein-Linked Ventures

### Suggested legal class name

- Financial Beneficiary / Conspirator in Fraud and Money Laundering

### Core factual focus

- Investors and entities receiving preferential deals, consulting retainers, or opaque compensation tied to Epstein-linked entities

### Roles

- Receiving transfers, consulting fees, or equity arrangements with unusual terms

- Acting as apparent conduits for value transfer to principals

### Apparent intent, means, motive

- Opaque compensation or outsized returns may function as quid pro quo or concealment of improper payments

### Prosecutorial theories

- RICO enterprise theories, wire/fraud, and forfeiture remedies to recover proceeds

### Investigative best practices

- Audit investment agreements, retainer contracts, and payment histories

- Trace capital flows and interview executives under proffer or immunity

### Preservation steps

- Preserve transactional documents, contracts, and board minutes related to investments



## Complicit Public Actors

- Law Enforcement, Government Officials

### Suggested legal class name

- Public Official Obstruction / Conspirator in Coverup

### Core factual focus

- Officials alleged to have suppressed inquiries, leaked information, or delayed investigative steps

### Roles

- Intervening in investigative priorities, sharing nonpublic material, or influencing prosecutorial decisions

### Apparent intent, means, motive

- Deviations from standard investigative behavior and politically sensitive communications suggest influence or protective intent

### Prosecutorial theories

- Obstruction by public officials, corruption/bribery statutes, evidence-tampering, and witness-interference

### Investigative best practices

- Review timelines, internal memos, call logs, and communications between agencies and political actors

- Use oversight tools to compel documents and testimony where criminal thresholds are uncertain

### Preservation steps

- Seek preservation of agency records and relevant communications



## Reputation Management Actors

- Media, PR Firms

### Suggested legal class name

- Reputation Management Conspirator / Facilitator of Concealment

### Core factual focus

- Firms and individuals crafting narratives, coordinating denials, or arranging third-party statements to suppress allegations

### Roles

- Paying influencers, coordinating statements, or commissioning research to discredit accusers

### Apparent intent, means, motive

- Coordinated messaging and payments timed to settlements or disclosures indicate intent to control public narrative and limit exposure

### Prosecutorial theories

- Criminal exposure where false statements to investigators occurred or where actions formed part of a broader concealment scheme

- Civil defamation claims where false public statements harmed victims

### Investigative best practices

- Subpoena contracts, invoices, and communications between PR firms and principals

- Timeline analysis of public statements against internal documents

### Preservation steps

- Preservation notices to PR firms, media consultants, and associated vendors

## Digital Facilitators

- IT Providers, Email Hosts, Data Destruction Operators

### Suggested legal class name

- Technical Facilitator / Accessory to Concealment

### Core factual focus

- IT administrators, hosting providers, and contractors maintaining data infrastructure or performing deletions/migrations

### Roles

- Managing servers, backups, and email systems; executing data deletion or migration; operating private infrastructure

### Apparent intent, means, motive

- Targeted deletion or migration timed to investigative pressure suggests active concealment rather than routine maintenance

### Prosecutorial theories

- Obstruction and evidence-destruction statutes, and aiding-and-abetting where services knowingly defeated investigations

### Investigative best practices

- Preserve metadata, backups, and service-provider logs

- Employ digital forensics to recover deleted/migrated content and analyze chain-of-custody

### Preservation steps

- Emergency preservation orders for servers, storage media, and cloud accounts

## Prosecution Strategy

- Cross-Cutting Elements

### Build enterprise theory

- Frame allegations as an interconnected support network enabling concealment and facilitation rather than isolated acts

### Use RICO strategically

- Aggregate predicate acts across actors to demonstrate pattern, continuity, and an enterprise

### Combine criminal and civil tools

- Parallel civil RICO, forfeiture, and asset-recovery actions to disrupt networks and recover proceeds

### Leverage immunity and proffers

- Offer narrow immunity to lower-level actors to secure testimony against higher-level facilitators

### Grand jury and subpoena tools

- Use broad grand jury subpoenas, third-party subpoenas, and witness subpoenas to compel documents and testimony

### Forensic financial analysis

- Employ forensic accountants to reconstruct layering, circular flows, and connections between shell entities and principals

### International cooperation

- Coordinate MLATs, 1782s, and foreign enforcement to pierce secrecy jurisdictions

### Crime-fraud exception

- Seek in camera review where communications show legal advice used to further concealment, to pierce privilege where standards met

### Preservation and expedited relief

- Rapid preservation letters, TROs, and seizure warrants for electronic evidence and documents

### Oversight and public accountability

- Use congressional and public mechanisms to compel production and maintain momentum where criminal thresholds are ambiguous

### Evidentiary cautions

- Do not rely on uncorroborated allegations; corroborate with records, travel and transaction data, and witness testimony
