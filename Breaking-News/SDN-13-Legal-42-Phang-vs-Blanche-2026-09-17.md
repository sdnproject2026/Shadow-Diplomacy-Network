## September 24, 2026 Deadline

US District Judge Emmet Sullivan has officially given the Department of Justice a deadline of September 24, 2026, to produce the handwritten FBI interview notes and other related records or face potential contempt of court charges.

## Scope and Hypotheticals

The supplied article's characterization should not be treated as establishing that criminally incriminating material exists.

The analysis below therefore separates the litigation consequences of nonproduction from the separate criminal-justice consequences that could follow only if authenticated documents, corroborating evidence, and applicable law establish an independent basis for criminal investigation or prosecution.

# Branching Possible Timelines

A-D

## Branch A

Todd Blanche Does Not Release Required Documents

### 2026-09-24

- The first question would be whether the government actually violated a clear and operative order, rather than merely whether production was incomplete

- Phang's strongest procedural move would be to identify each specific command in the order, the document category covered by that command, the production deadline, what was actually produced, and the precise deficiency

- The request should distinguish outright nonproduction from disputed redactions because the court may treat those issues differently

### Immediate Litigation Response

- Seek an order requiring compliance by a definite date

- Request a sworn or otherwise sufficiently verified accounting identifying what responsive materials exist, what was searched, what was produced, what remains withheld, and the legal basis asserted for each withholding

- Request in-camera production where the government's asserted privilege, victim-protection, or other confidentiality rationale cannot be evaluated from the public record

- Request preservation of the disputed records and associated metadata

- Ask the court to require identification of the officials or custodians responsible for the representations being made to the court if factual disputes make that necessary

### Contempt Branch

- Civil contempt is potentially relevant when a party violates a clear and unambiguous court order and the violation is established by the applicable evidentiary standard

- The Supreme Court has recognized the federal courts' authority to enforce their lawful orders through contempt

### Precedent

- Chambers v. NASCO also recognizes inherent judicial authority to address litigation conduct that abuses the judicial process, although courts are expected to exercise that authority with restraint 

### Important Limitation

- A contempt proceeding is not automatically a criminal prosecution of the Attorney General

- The Supreme Court distinguishes coercive civil contempt from punitive criminal contempt

### Precedent

- International Union, United Mine Workers v. Bagwell explains that serious punitive contempt sanctions can trigger criminal-procedure protections, including jury-trial requirements in appropriate circumstances

# Branch C

Documents Are Released And Appear Potentially Incriminating

This branch requires particular caution

The discovery of a document that appears incriminating does not itself establish probable cause, guilt, or even admissibility at trial.

A handwritten interview note may contain an allegation, hearsay, an investigator's shorthand, an incomplete account, or information later contradicted by other evidence.

The legally significant sequence would therefore be:

### Step 1: Preserve The Original Evidence

- Preserve the original file exactly as produced

- Preserve the production envelope, Bates number, docket filing, metadata, certification, and chain of custody

- Do not modify the original document

- Maintain a working copy for analysis

### Step 2: Authenticate The Material

- Establish where the document came from

- Establish who created it or maintained it

- Establish when it was created

- Establish that the produced version is complete and has not been altered

- Compare it against related FD-302 reports, interview records, exhibits, and other independently sourced materials

### Step 3: Separate Allegation From Evidence

- Identify statements based on personal knowledge

- Identify statements based on hearsay

- Identify statements attributed to another person

- Identify statements that are merely investigative leads

- Identify independently corroborated facts

- Identify facts that remain disputed

### Step 4: Establish The Applicable Offense

- Identify the particular federal or state statute potentially implicated

- Identify every statutory element

- Determine whether the known evidence potentially establishes each element

- Determine the applicable statute of limitations

- Determine jurisdiction and venue

- Determine whether the alleged conduct occurred within federal jurisdiction

### Step 5: Identify Independent Corroboration

- Locate records independent of the disputed document

- Identify witnesses with firsthand knowledge

- Preserve contemporaneous communications

- Compare dates, locations, financial records, travel records, communications, and other objective evidence where legally obtainable

- Avoid treating repetition of the same allegation in multiple documents as independent corroboration when the documents derive from the same original source

### Step 6: Make An Appropriate Criminal Referral

- Phang, as a civil litigant, does not herself indict a federal defendant

- If the evidence establishes a legitimate basis for criminal investigation, the appropriate mechanism would be referral to the competent prosecutorial authority

- Depending on the alleged offense and jurisdiction, that could involve a US Attorney's Office, the Department of Justice, another federal investigative agency, or an appropriate state authority

- The prosecutor independently determines whether further investigation is warranted

### Step 7: Investigative Process

- Investigators may obtain additional evidence through lawful investigative methods

- Prosecutors assess whether the evidence establishes probable cause and whether prosecution is authorized and appropriate under applicable law

- If federal prosecution is pursued for an offense requiring indictment, the government ordinarily presents the matter to a grand jury

### Step 8: Charging

- A grand jury may return an indictment if the applicable requirements are satisfied

- Alternatively, federal criminal procedure permits other charging mechanisms in circumstances authorized by law

- The charging document should identify the specific offense and factual allegations necessary to constitute that offense

### Step 9: Initial Appearance And Arraignment

- After charging, the defendant receives the procedural protections applicable to federal criminal defendants

- The court addresses issues such as counsel, release or detention, and the next procedural deadlines

### Step 10: Discovery And Motions

- The prosecution must comply with applicable disclosure obligations

- The defense can challenge admissibility, constitutional violations, jurisdiction, sufficiency of the charging instrument, and other issues

- The court resolves appropriate pretrial motions before trial

### Step 11: Trial Scheduling

- If the prosecution survives the applicable pretrial proceedings, the court schedules trial consistent with the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, the Speedy Trial Act, the court's calendar, and any legitimate continuances

- A trial date therefore cannot responsibly be promised merely because documents appear incriminating

# Branch D

Documents Show Possible False Statements To The Court

This is legally distinct from documents showing that somebody committed the underlying conduct being investigated.

### Potential Consequence

- If evidence suggested that a party or attorney knowingly made materially false statements to the federal court, the issue could become relevant to sanctions, contempt, professional discipline, or potentially a separate criminal investigation depending upon the facts

### Required Proof

- The relevant statement must first be identified precisely

- The underlying factual proposition must be established

- Materiality must be established

- The evidence must distinguish an intentional falsehood from mistake, ambiguity, incomplete information, or a good-faith legal argument

- Any criminal theory would require identification of a specific statute and proof of its elements

### Procedural Principle

- Chambers recognizes inherent judicial authority to address bad-faith litigation conduct

- That authority does not mean that every inaccurate filing constitutes a crime or that every litigation dispute warrants criminal proceedings 

## Best-Practices Approach 

For Phang's Document-Production Litigation

### 1. Convert The Dispute Into A Compliance Matrix

- For every ordered document category, identify the exact order paragraph

- Identify the production deadline

- Identify what DOJ says it produced

- Identify what Phang's team received

- Identify what remains disputed

- Identify the government's stated legal basis for withholding

- Identify the requested judicial remedy

This approach minimizes the risk that the dispute becomes a generalized argument over transparency rather than a series of objectively testable compliance questions.

### 2. Request In-Camera Review

Rather Than Relying On Competing Descriptions

- Where DOJ says a document contains protected information, ask the court to inspect the actual document

- Request both the redacted and unredacted versions where appropriate

- Request a declaration explaining the redaction methodology

- Ask the court to determine whether the asserted protected information can be segregated from otherwise producible information

The existing proceedings already demonstrate that in-camera review is available as a mechanism in this litigation.

### 3. Force Document-Level Specificity

- Identify the Bates number or other unique identifier

- Identify the specific redaction

- Identify the statutory provision asserted

- Identify the factual basis for applying that provision

- Identify whether the same category of information was disclosed elsewhere

- Ask the court to resolve disputed categories individually

This is especially useful where the dispute concerns whether "substantially similar" records can simply be treated as duplicates.

### 4. Request A Verified Production Statement

- Require the government to identify the custodians and repositories searched

- Require identification of responsive categories

- Require a statement of what was withheld

- Require identification of the reason for each withholding category

- Require supplementation if additional responsive materials are discovered

The objective is to create a record that makes later enforcement comparatively straightforward.

### 5. Seek Enforcement Before Seeking Punishment

- First establish precisely what the court ordered

- Then establish noncompliance

- Then request an order compelling compliance

- Then seek coercive remedies if noncompliance continues

- Reserve punitive contempt issues for conduct that independently satisfies the applicable requirements

This sequencing is consistent with the Supreme Court's distinction between coercive civil contempt and punitive criminal contempt.

### 6. Use Rule 37 

Only Where It Actually Applies

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37 authorizes motions to compel and sanctions for failures involving discovery obligations and discovery orders.

It can provide substantial enforcement mechanisms, including additional orders and, in appropriate circumstances, contempt.

But the present Phang litigation is principally an APA/statutory disclosure case, so the precise source of the court's enforcement authority should be tied to the actual order and applicable statute rather than assuming Rule 37 governs every dispute.

## Potentially Criminal Evidence

### Evidence Preservation

- Preserve the documents exactly as produced

- Preserve the docket record showing when and how they were produced

- Preserve accompanying DOJ declarations and certifications

- Preserve prior versions and related public records

- Avoid public alteration, annotation, or selective editing of the originals

### Evidentiary Assessment

- Determine whether the document is authentic

- Determine whether the underlying information is firsthand

- Determine whether the information is independently corroborated

- Determine whether the conduct described actually constitutes a statutory offense

- Determine whether the statute of limitations remains open

- Determine whether federal jurisdiction exists

### Referral

- Prepare a factual memorandum rather than an accusation

- Attach or identify the primary evidence

- Separate established facts from allegations and inferences

- Identify the potentially applicable statutes without asserting guilt

- Refer the matter to the appropriate prosecutorial authority

- Permit the prosecutor or investigating agency to determine whether additional investigation is warranted

### Charging

- Criminal charges should be brought only by the legally authorized prosecutorial mechanism

- The charging authority must determine whether the evidence establishes the required legal basis

- A grand jury may be required depending on the offense and charging mechanism

- The defendant then receives the procedural protections applicable to the criminal case

### Trial

- A trial is not automatically scheduled following production of incriminating documents

- The sequence normally includes investigation, charging, initial proceedings, discovery, pretrial litigation, and determination of whether the case is ready for trial

- Any proposed timeline should therefore be expressed as procedural contingencies rather than a promised prosecution date
