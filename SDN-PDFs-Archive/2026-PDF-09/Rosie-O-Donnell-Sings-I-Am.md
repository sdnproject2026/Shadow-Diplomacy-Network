With Epstein Survivors To Hit ‘Corrupt’
Trump Admin
The comedian reminded the women that they’re “all heroes” before the group

Epstein’s abuse and joined them in song to push back at the Trump
administration over its handling of the Epstein files.
“I know it’s been very hard for you with very little support, and I’m sorry
that our country’s so fucked up that this is what you have to live through,”
she told the women in a clip shared Thursday, exactly one year after
survivors demanded the release of government files about Epstein’s
crimes in their entirety.
“You are all heroes, and you’re going to change how the world deals with
child sex abuse,” O’Donnell told the women.
The comedian wrote in the caption of Thursday’s post that she had “called
some Epstein survivors” after “corrupt” Attorney General Todd Blanche,
President Donald Trump’s former personal attorney, “implied that he did
not believe men accused of sex abuse would be charged” following the
Justice Department’s review of the files.
In July, a group of survivors slammed Blanche for protecting “the people
who committed crimes against us” after the Justice Department released
personal information and nude photographs of some survivors but
redacted information about their abusers.
“Thirty years, 1,200 victims, and 6 million files — a mountain of evidence.
But Todd Blanche says there are no investigative leads,” they said in a
video denouncing him. “Todd Blanche failed us. He failed victims
everywhere. We deserve better. Our country deserves better.”

In the clip posted Thursday, O’Donnell can be seen wearing all white and
singing Helen Reddy’s feminist anthem “I Am Woman” alongside the
women before raising her fist in solidarity with them.
O’Donnell — who has a long-running feud with Trump — wrote that the
“powerful” song by the late singer taught her to use her voice when she
was 10 years old.
“Show ur support for abuse survivors - Brave enough to speak out,”
O’Donnell wrote in the post’s caption before quoting Reddy’s lyrics.
“‘Yes I am wise - but it’s wisdom born of pain,’” the comedian added before
encouraging others to sing along and put their “#fistsUP,” too.
