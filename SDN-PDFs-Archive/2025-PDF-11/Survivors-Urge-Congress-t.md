Survivors Urge Congress to
Release Epstein Files in Powerful
Open Letter
Survivors demand Congress end decades of secrecy and deliver
long-overdue accountability.
Aaron Parnas • 11 hours ago

Twenty-four survivors of Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell’s crimes—joined by
members of Virginia Roberts Giuffre’s family—have issued an emotional and urgent
appeal to the United States Senate and House of Representatives, demanding that
Congress vote to release the long-awaited Epstein files.
In a letter delivered to lawmakers today, the signatories call on Congress to “deliver a
promise the American people have awaited for far too long” by voting YES on the
Epstein Files Transparency Act. The survivors argue that releasing the files is critical to
achieving true accountability for the vast network of individuals who enabled Epstein
and Maxwell’s abuse.
The letter opens with a reminder of the testimony several survivors recently gave on
Capitol Hill—accounts that visibly shook many members of Congress.

“We urge you, when you cast your vote, please do not forget the horror you felt
that day,” the survivors write. “There is no middle ground here. There is no
hiding behind party affiliation.”

The authors stress that those testimonies represent only a fraction of the trauma
endured by Epstein and Maxwell’s victims—numbering more than a thousand,

The survivors argue that the public release of the files is essential to confronting what
they describe as a longstanding “double standard of justice,” in which powerful
individuals connected to Epstein have escaped meaningful consequences.
The letter emphasizes that despite years of investigation, most of the pair’s alleged coconspirators “remain completely free, continuing to amass power and prestige.” The
survivors urge lawmakers to consider their own loved ones as they cast their votes:

“Look into the eyes of your children, your sisters, your mothers, and your aunts.
Imagine if they had been preyed upon. Imagine if you yourself were a survivor.”

They also make clear that their communities are watching, warning legislators that
their decisions “will be remembered at the ballot box.”
One of the most poignant sections of the letter centers on Virginia Roberts Giuffre, the
survivor whose courage made her one of the most prominent voices in exposing
Epstein’s trafficking network. Giuffre, who passed away in April, is remembered in the
letter as “a true American hero” and “a beacon of strength to survivors everywhere.”
Her family and fellow survivors say her bravery—despite threats, public attacks, and
immense personal hardship—helped transform the case and emboldened others to
step forward.

“Virginia was failed over and over by the systems that consistently believed and
supported her abusers over her,” the letter states. “Today, we urge you not to
fail another survivor.”

In urging Congress to release the files, they ask lawmakers to “embody Virginia’s
courage” and take a stand in her absence.

The letter concludes with a direct appeal for Congress to pass the Epstein Files
Transparency Act, describing the vote as a test of justice, accountability, and national
integrity.

“Vote YES on the Epstein Files Transparency Act. Vote for justice, accountability,
and the truth.”

The signatories close the letter with “gratitude, respect, and resilience,” representing not
only themselves but the broader community of survivors and loved ones carrying on
this fight.
