The US Department of Justice (DoJ) appeared to ignore a Friday deadline set
by Raúl Torrez, the attorney general of New Mexico, to share information
about the abuse of girls at a remote ranch formerly owned by the disgraced
financier and sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.
Torrez’s office asked the DoJ in February for unredacted files after he
reopened a dormant criminal investigation into activities at Zorro ranch, a
remote 10,000-acre property in desert scrub outside New Mexico.
It set a 31 July deadline for the department to share relevant records. As it
approached on Friday, the justice department did not respond to a request
for comment.
The Guardian reported in February that recent releases of the so-called
Epstein files included thousands of mentions of the ranch, and numerous
photographs of men and young girls there. A succession of survivors had
previously given testimony about abuse they were subjected to.

However, unlike other properties prominent in Epstein’s sex-trafficking
conspiracy in Palm Beach, New York, and his private Caribbean island, no
federal search of the ranch was ever confirmed to have taken place.
Torrez set a deadline of Friday for federal prosecutors from the justice
department, and the US attorney’s office for the southern district of New
York (SDNY), which led the original Epstein investigation, to allow access to
whatever documents they had.
Despite promises of cooperation from Todd Blanche, the acting attorney
general, and Jay Clayton, former US attorney for the SDNY, now director
designate of national intelligence, Torrez said only 31 pages were ever
handed over.
They were mostly old news clippings and heavily redacted pages of little use
to state investigators, he said.
Torrez said in a 14 July letter to Blanche he would “pursue all available legal
remedies without further notice” if the 31 July deadline passed, having made
“repeated, documented, good-faith efforts to obtain the unredacted
investigative records in the USDOJ’s custody”.
The same week, in an interview with Albuquerque ABC News affiliate KOAT,
Torrez said: “I’m not taking anything off the table. I’m not ruling out a
lawsuit.”
Epstein died in jail in New York in August 2019 while awaiting trial on child
sex trafficking charges. The fight for documents relating to his activities at
Zorro ranch, which he bought in 1993 from the estate of the Democratic
former New Mexico governor Bruce King, became something of a cat and
mouse affair after Torrez’s reopening of an inquiry first launched by his
predecessor Hector Balderas in February 2019.
According to the attorney general, the southern district of New York called
him in July to announce that it would not cooperate.
But Clayton, in his confirmation hearing in the US Senate last month, told
Martin Heinrich, Democratic senator for New Mexico: “My folks, I think as
we speak, are seeking dialogue with the attorney general to see if we can
reach an accommodation”. Clayton noted that the documents were subject
to certain “protective orders”, namely redactions to hide the identities of
victims.
Blanche, accused in February by Democrats of a “full-blown cover-up” of the
Epstein files, told his own confirmation hearing last month that he was
“continuing to work” with the New Mexico department of justice.
But Torrez said he had heard nothing further, either from Blanche, or the
SDNY.
Meanwhile, the US attorney’s office for the district of New Mexico, in a
statement to the Guardian, said it had “substantively responded” to Torrez’s
request on 30 June by emailing the 31 documents.
Some, a spokesperson said, were communications directing Torrez to the
SDNY. The New Mexico US attorney’s office “did not lead or handle the Zorro
Ranch investigation and therefore neither collected nor retained any
investigative materials”, the statement said.
Torrez did not respond to a request for comment from the Guardian on
Friday. He told MS Now’s the Briefing with Jen Psaki on 23 July that he was

annoyed by months of stonewalling from Blanche, Donald Trump’s pick for
attorney general, whose confirmation is in peril.
“It appears that the administration has been really focused on the
management of communications around the Epstein case, rather than acting
in a way that demonstrates a commitment to getting to the truth and getting
to accountability,” said Torrez.
Especially frustrating, he told MS Now, was the conduct of the SDNY after
the first, Balderas-ordered investigation was shelved. Torrez said: “We stood
down that investigation at the explicit request of the southern district of
New York, and so to have them fail to honor their agreement and then not
give us access to this file is extremely unusual, and it’s complicating our
ability to advance this criminal investigation.”
Blanche “has the authority right now, with the stroke of a pen, to grant us
this access, to let us, our investigators and prosecutors, come and take a look
at the files, identify co-conspirators, witnesses, corroborating evidence,
identify new potential leads,” Torrez added.
“Every day that goes by that that doesn’t happen is another day that we’ve
delayed delivering justice for the survivors.”
