Convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein may have acted as an
unregistered foreign agent — and tried to leverage his past
relationship with President Trump, when doing so, the top
Democrat on the House Judiciary Committee alleged in a
letter demanding records.
The big picture: Rep. Jamie Raskin's (D-Md.) inquiry
previews the types of investigations the party could launch
with subpoena power if they retake a chamber this fall.
Driving the news: Raskin wrote to administration officials
that the Epstein files show the disgraced financier
seemingly tried to become an emissary for Saudi Arabia and
appeared to advise leaders in Kuwait, Israel and Russia.
In one exchange, Epstein appeared to pitch himself to
Saudi Arabia as a "Financial Confidant to the Prince

/court / Ks.a whatever entity suits you . . .I am happy to
represent KSA interests. period."
Raskin wrote that Epstein's "close relationship" with
Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman "became
obvious" in the emails.
There were also indications Epstein coached the Kuwaiti
emir on how to deal with Trump, the letter alleges.
Epstein told a redacted sender in 2017 there was "much to
do around the trump trip" and asked what "concrete"
things the emir would "like to see happen from the US."
The letter also alleged Epstein advised former Israeli Prime
Minister Ehud Barak, serving as his "sherpa through U.S.Russia-Middle East diplomacy," and, "most disturbingly …
appeared to give advice on how to handle President Trump
to high-level officials in Russia."
Epstein appeared to have given Russia's late United
Nations representative, Vitaly Churkin, advice on Trump,
writing in 2018, "churkin was great. he understood trump
after our conversations, it is not complex. he must be seen
to get something its that simple."
What he's saying: "Jeffrey Epstein never registered as a
foreign agent," Raskin wrote to acting Attorney General
Todd Blanche, Secretary of State Marco Rubio and and Jay
Clayton, the U.S. attorney for the Southern District of New
York and Trump's nominee for director of national
intelligence.
"Yet numerous recently released documents now show he
acted aggressively on behalf of multiple foreign
governments, including several with interests adverse to
the United States, in order to influence policies of the first
Trump Administration," he continued.
Raskin warned that Epstein's ties with foreign leaders
posed a major national security liability and raised
questions about what information he could have fed
them about the president.

White House spokesperson Abigail Jackson said in a
statement to Axios, "Just as President Trump has said, he's
been totally exonerated on anything relating to Epstein."
The DOJ did not immediately respond to Axios' request
for comment.

What we're watching: Raskin gave the government until
5pm on Aug. 3 to provide records tied to Epstein's contacts
with 19 nations — including Saudi Arabia, Israel, Kuwait,
Russia and Qatar — as well as entities that may have been
structured to avoid federal registration.
He also asked DOJ whether there have ever been Foreign
Agents Registration Act investigations into Epstein and,
if not, why it hadn't initiated any.
FARA requires what its name suggests: for foreign agents
representing or doing advocacy work on behalf of foreign
governments, people or organizations to register with the
DOJ.
The bottom line: The files exposed the breadth of Epstein's
far-reaching business and political web, prompting
additional investigations and derailing careers.
And the web keeps unraveling, piling political and legal
pressure on the administration to release withheld
records.
