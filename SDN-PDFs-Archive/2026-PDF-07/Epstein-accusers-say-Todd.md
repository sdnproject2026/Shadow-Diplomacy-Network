The women's comments come as Blanche’s nomination hangs in the balance due to
unrelated concerns about him. Sens. Thom Tillis and John Cornyn, two Senate
Judiciary Committee Republicans, have stalled his nomination as they fight with
Trump over the terms of a controversial settlement deal with the IRS. A vote to
confirm Blanche to the top job has been postponed.
Tillis told Blanche on July 16 that he would only vote to confirm him if Blanche met
with Epstein’s accusers, a request that came minutes after Dani Bensky testified that
she and other Epstein survivors had been trying fruitlessly for a face-to-face with him.
The DOJ announced the same day that Blanche would meet with them.

USA TODAY reached out to the Justice Department multiple times regarding details of
the July 16 meeting. The Justice Department referred USA TODAY to a statement by
Alessandra Serano, the trafficking and child exploitation coordinator.
Serano said that Blanche attempted to address all of the women’s questions and that
“the Justice Department is determined to bring justice for all victims of human
trafficking and sex crimes.”
Here’s what we know about how the meeting went.

‘Acting like a defense attorney’
Bensky, a teacher from Hawaii, said that the day she met with Blanche, July 16, was
already intense because she also testified against him at his confirmation hearing. She
said someone from the Justice Department first offered her a staff meeting. Later, she
said they offered a meeting with Blanche.
Bensky said she and a handful of other Epstein survivors who live all over the country
attended. So did family members of the late Virginia Roberts Giuffre, the most
outspoken and well-known Epstein survivor. She said Blanche brought Serano, other
staffers, and another man from the FBI. She said the women's lawyers joined via a
video call.

Lara Blume McGee, 47, of Arkansas, another survivor who was in the room, said these
women have already gone to the FBI to report Epstein's conduct. She referenced

abuse material from Ohio to New York.
She said Blanche effectively disregarded what they already did.
“He told us to go back to the FBI and start all over again,” McGee said.

Has Todd Blanche read the Epstein files?
Both Bensky and McGee described an exchange in which members of the group asked
Blanche whether he had read the Epstein files. They both interpreted his answers to
indicate that he had never read any of the documents the department he runs
published in an online database.
In his former post as deputy attorney general, Blanche oversaw the release of more
than 3 million files under the Epstein Files Transparency Act. Some of the women who
say naked pictures of them were released in the documents have gone to court to get
those records taken down.
Bensky said that when the group first asked Blanche whether he had read the Epstein
files, he “basically put it back on us,” referencing the massive volume of files and
implying it was too extensive to read in full. “We (then) asked, ‘Have you read any of
them?’”
Bensky said Blanche didn’t give a clear response, but instead pivoted. She said that
their questions gave him the opportunity to say he had read some of the files or
searched through them.

The women in the group are universally opposed to Blanche being confirmed as
attorney general. If all Democrats on the Senate Judiciary vote “no,” and two
Republicans join them, his nomination could be doomed.
If his nomination makes it to the Senate floor, and all Democrats vote against the
nomination, a handful of Republican senators could also block his nomination. That’s
what Epstein’s accusers are hoping happens. But it's not yet clear whether the vote
would happen before 2027.
“Trump is going to be Trump and do what he feels like has to be done, but I do believe
that the Republican Party will have some courage,” McGee said. “We’ve been meeting

with parties on both sides … and they’re going to stand on the right side of history.”
Stein said they may request another meeting with the Justice Department, because she
wants to remain open to working with them in good faith. But she said she wouldn’t
participate in “performative” meetings ever again.
“Just because Todd Blanche doesn’t want to work with us, that’s – I’m sorry, but that’s
not going to stop us,” she said.
