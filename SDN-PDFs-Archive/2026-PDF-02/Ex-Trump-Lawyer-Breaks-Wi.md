Christina Bobb, President Donald Trump's former lawyer who promoted his claims that the
2020 election was stolen, has broken with the Department of Justice (DOJ) regarding the Jeffrey
Epstein files.
“This is the rich and powerful with a lot of really, really bad information,” Bobb said during a
podcast interview with journalist Caitlin Sinclair. “Now, I don't know that any of it is provable
beyond a reasonable doubt at this point, and that the Department of Justice has come out and
said, we can't do anything with these documents, whatever. I don't necessarily think that's true
either.”

Newsweek reached out to the Department of Justice for comment.

During the latest episode of The Caitlin Sinclair Show, Bobb said the rich and powerful have a
lot of “really damning information” coming out about them.
“The American people are going, ‘We want somebody held accountable. We want this
information public,’” Bobb said. “The public should know all of this before elections, before
anybody is appointed to positions. Certainly Bill Gates and a lot these people have received
government funding for various contracts in the past.”
Bobb also criticized Trump for his previous actions to keep the Epstein files from being
released.
“I don't know what Donald Trump's position was on why he didn't want this released or
whether he didn't want to embarrass his friends or whatever it was, but he has some decisions
to make on this,” Bobb said. “Is this Department of Justice going to pursue any of these lines?
And it may be the type of situation where they have to basically start over. Because this is such
a mess, this pile of information that we have.”
Before the Epstein Files Transparency Act was enacted in November 2025, Trump and senior
members of his administration questioned efforts to compel the release of the files.
The Trump White House and DOJ were reluctant to disclose many of the records and tried to
discourage Republican support for a congressional push to make them public. But when it
became clear a House vote would happen anyway, Trump reversed course and said he would
sign the bill rather than fight it.
Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche said last month that the department is not making any
attempts to protect Trump as it releases the roughly 3 million files related to the Epstein
investigations.
Trump has repeatedly denied wrongdoing related to Epstein and has not been charged or
accused by prosecutors in connection with the billionaire’s crimes.

The mentions do not imply wrongdoing. Many include news clippings, internal DOJ

Why Didn't Biden Release the Epstein Files?

During Joe Biden's presidency, most of the material was legally sealed or restricted by court
orders, grand jury secrecy rules, and ongoing investigations.
More largely, the Biden administration touted DOJ independence and did not interfere in
prosecutorial decisions. Victim privacy protections also substantially limited what could be
publicly disclosed.
There was also no legal obligation or deadline to force a full public release while Biden was in
office. Only after Congress passed the Epstein Files Transparency Act in late 2025 did the DOJ
begin releasing large batches of documents, but those releases still faced legal challenges and
redactions.
What Is the Epstein Files Transparency Act?

The Epstein Files Transparency Act was passed by Congress and signed by Trump in November
2025. The law forced the DOJ to publicly disclose investigative materials related to Epstein and
his network, including travel logs, correspondence, records of investigations and prosecutions,
and other government files.
Within 30 days of the law's enactment, the attorney general was required to make all
unclassified DOJ records associated with Epstein and related investigations publicly available
in a searchable, downloadable format.
However, the DOJ did not fully meet that deadline, sparking criticism that only a fraction of the
files were publicly released on time and that many documents remain heavily redacted or
unreleased.
