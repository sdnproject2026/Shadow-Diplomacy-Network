Rachel Fischer, a forensic nurse expert and sexual assault nurse examiner specializing in anti-trafficking efforts, said in the trafficking sphere, a
recruiter is referred to as a “bottom girl.” Fischer says this is a “well-documented dynamic in organized sex trafficking operations” that is not a
result of free choice and does not erase the victimhood from the survivors who became recruiters.
“Traffickers use isolation, psychological manipulation, threats, intermittent rewards, and dependency to create what we clinically describe as
trauma bonds. Over time, victims are conditioned to comply in order to reduce violence, avoid punishment, or gain small protections,” she said.
Fischer said the dynamic is common for some victims, usually framed as a way to make more money and get better treatment from the traffickers.
“In many cases, victims are told they will be harmed, replaced, or further exploited if they do not recruit. When someone is living under chronic
coercion, their brain shifts into survival mode,” she said.
When this happens, Fischer said it can blur a person’s moral boundaries as exploitation becomes normalized. Abuse can create a distorted sense
of power alignment with the traffickers, with victims feeling they may be safer the closer they get to the person in power.
“It is important to understand that this does not erase their victimization. It reflects the way coercive systems perpetuate themselves by forcing
victims into roles that help sustain the exploitation structure. The transition from being trafficked to recruiting others is often an extension of the
original abuse, not a departure from it,” she said.
