# Draft Indictment

## 2007

The Department of Justice officially released this heavily requested 2007 federal draft indictment against

It provides definitive proof of what federal investigators knew about the scale of his crimes before he was granted a highly controversial non-prosecution "sweetheart deal."

## Core Facts

### The Proposed Charges

- The 56-page document, drafted by federal prosecutors in 2007, outlined a 32-count indictment against Epstein and several of his employees.

- The charges included sex trafficking and the enticement of minors.

### Industrial Scale of Abuse

- The document details a systematic conspiracy to procure minor girls as young as 14 years old between 2001 and 2005.

- It describes a pattern where victims were brought to his Palm Beach mansion under the guise of paid "massage services," which then escalated into sexual abuse.

### Jane Doe #17

- The specific victim text visible in the video snippet matching Jane Doe #17 is directly from the unsealed pages.

- The indictment detailed that Epstein used interstate commerce (telephones) to entice her and others while they were under the age of 18.

### Fear and Intimidation

- The files confirm that Epstein used active intimidation to keep his victims silent, including explicitly telling a 16-year-old girl that "bad things could happen to her" if she ever told anyone what happened.

### The Shelved Indictment

- Despite having compiled massive grand jury testimony and an extensive case, top brass at the U.S.

- Attorney's Office in Miami - led by then-US Attorney Alexander Acosta - shelved the federal indictment. They instead signed a non-prosecution agreement, allowing Epstein to plead guilty to minor state-level solicitation charges and serve only 13 months with daily work-release privileges.
