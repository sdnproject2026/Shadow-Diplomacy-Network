# Epstein Yahoo Emails

## Kyrie - DDoSecrets

The key distinction is that the Kyrie/DDoSecrets Yahoo cache and Bloomberg's Yahoo cache are closely related but were not the same acquisition.

The strongest available evidence is:

### Primary/first-party evidence

- HOPE 26's official conference materials identify “Kyrie” as the hacker who gained access to Jeffrey Epstein's emails and subsequently provided a copy to Distributed Denial of Secrets (DDoSecrets). 

- The conference program also lists Kyrie alongside Emma Best, Mikael Thalen, Alexander J.

- Urbelis, and Raphael Satter for a session on investigating the Epstein files.+1

### DDoSecrets' archive

- DDoSecrets maintains an Epstein-files repository containing an `Emails` directory. 

- Its broader Epstein collection was published in November 2025 and includes documents, emails and recordings.

- DDDoSecrets+1

### Bloomberg's independent acquisition

- Bloomberg reported in September 2025 that it obtained more than 18,000 emails from Epstein's personal Yahoo account. 

- Bloomberg says it independently authenticated the material using cryptographic/DKIM checks, metadata analysis and corroboration with outside sources; 

- four independent experts reviewed its methodology. 

### Kyrie's account

- The most direct published account I found is a recent report from the San Francisco Standard describing Kyrie's HOPE conference appearance. 

- It says Kyrie described finding multiple active login sessions after Epstein's death, suggesting to him that other people were accessing the account simultaneously. 

- The report says Bloomberg subsequently obtained a similar Yahoo cache independently. 

### Important caveat

- I did not find a primary technical record publicly identifying Kyrie by a legal name, documenting exactly how he obtained the Yahoo credentials, or independently proving the claimed simultaneous login sessions. 

- Those details currently rest primarily on Kyrie's account as reported from the conference. 

- The official HOPE materials deliberately identify him only as “Kyrie.”

## Publicly Downloadable Emails

The DDoSecrets cache is publicly downloadable, and it is technically possible to compare it against the Bloomberg material that Bloomberg has published.

The bigger issue is that Bloomberg has not published its entire 18,000-email cache as a downloadable corpus, so a complete one-to-one comparison cannot presently be performed from Bloomberg's public website alone.

There is, however, enough material to perform a meaningful partial forensic comparison.

## What can actually be compared?

### 1. Message identity

The first step would be extracting identifiers from the DDoSecrets messages:

- `Message-ID`

- date/time

- sender

- recipient

- CC

- subject

- `In-Reply-To`

- `References`

Those can then be compared with the corresponding metadata Bloomberg publishes in its reproduced emails.

This could establish message-level overlap without needing Bloomberg's entire underlying database.

### 2. Message contents

Where Bloomberg reproduces an email in full, you can compare:

- subject

- sender

- recipient

- timestamp

- body text

- quoted replies

- signatures

- Yahoo footer material

Exact matches across numerous independently published emails would provide strong evidence that the DDoSecrets and Bloomberg datasets overlap.

### 3. Attachments

This could be even more useful.

DDoSecrets reportedly includes attachments, while Bloomberg says its authentication process examined enclosures. 

If an attachment exists in both datasets, researchers could calculate:

- SHA-256

- SHA-1

- MD5, where useful for legacy identification

- file size

- filename

- MIME type

An identical cryptographic hash would demonstrate that the two collections contain the same attachment.

### 4. Raw email headers

This is potentially the most valuable comparison.

If the DDoSecrets files preserve the original raw email, researchers can examine:

- `Received:` headers

- `Message-ID`

- `Date`

- `DKIM-Signature`

- `Authentication-Results`

- MIME headers

- Yahoo-specific headers

Bloomberg's authentication article specifically says it performed cryptographic and metadata analysis. 

That gives researchers a basis for comparing Bloomberg's published examples against the DDoSecrets originals.

## 20,900 vs. 18,000

DDoSecrets describes its release as approximately 20,900 emails and attachments, while Bloomberg describes its collection as more than 18,000 emails. 

A secondary summary of the release reports the DDoSecrets collection as approximately 18,700 emails plus 2,200 attachments and says DDoSecrets characterized its source as independent from Bloomberg's, with slight differences indicating separate exfiltrations of the same Yahoo account.

That is highly relevant to your original question.

It means the most interesting hypothesis is no longer:

> "Are these two completely unrelated datasets?"

but rather:

> "How much of two independently exfiltrated copies of the same Yahoo account overlaps?"

If the DDoSecrets statement about an independent source is accurate, then Kyrie's account and Bloomberg's acquisition could represent two separate compromises/exfiltrations of the same mailbox at different times.

## What I would do technically

If the goal is to prove or disprove overlap, I would not start by comparing the 20,900 vs. 18,000 totals.

I would build a corpus from DDoSecrets and create a fingerprint database:

- SHA-256 of every raw email

- SHA-256 of every attachment

- `Message-ID`

- normalized sender

- normalized recipient

- timestamp

- subject

- body hash

- attachment hashes

- DKIM signature, if present

- `Received:` chain

Then I would take every Bloomberg-published email for which enough metadata is available and search for exact and fuzzy matches.

The resulting categories would be:

- Exact raw-email match

- Same Message-ID, different serialization

- Same body, different headers

- Same attachment, different email

- Probable match

- No corresponding DDoSecrets record found

That would give us something much more useful than simply saying that the two caches contain "similar emails."

## What we cannot do

Bloomberg has not publicly released its complete 18,000-email database in the sources I found.

Therefore, we cannot honestly calculate:

> "X% of Bloomberg's 18,000 emails are present in DDoSecrets."

We can calculate the overlap among Bloomberg's publicly reproduced emails, but not necessarily the complete Bloomberg-to-DDoSecrets intersection.

Likewise, Bloomberg's authentication methodology can establish the authenticity of portions of its own cache; it does not automatically authenticate every file in DDoSecrets.

## Bottom line

A technically serious comparison is possible right now, but it would be a partial comparison, because DDoSecrets provides a downloadable corpus while Bloomberg provides selected emails and reporting rather than its complete underlying dataset.

The evidence now points toward an especially interesting possibility:

- DDoSecrets/Kyrie and Bloomberg appear to have obtained separate collections from the same Yahoo account, potentially through separate exfiltrations.

- The decisive experiment would be a Message-ID + raw-header + body-hash + attachment-hash comparison.

If thousands of DDoSecrets messages can be matched to Bloomberg's published messages on those dimensions, we could move from "apparently the same Yahoo mailbox" to a much stronger forensic demonstration of dataset overlap.

## Overlapping Collections

The DDoSecrets and Bloomberg collections appear to be substantially overlapping collections from Jeffrey Epstein's personal Yahoo mailbox, but there is not enough publicly documented forensic evidence to say they are bit-for-bit identical.

The key distinction is same source vs. same copy:

### Same source

- Both are reported to contain material from Epstein's personal Yahoo account.

### Same copy

- Not publicly established.

### Same complete mailbox

- Not established.

### Substantial overlap

- Strongly plausible, and later reporting gives reason to think the collections overlap significantly.

### Independent authentication

- Bloomberg published a substantially more detailed authentication methodology for the material it obtained.

## Technical comparison

### DDoSecrets / Kyrie

- Kyrie reportedly told the HOPE conference that he gained access to Epstein's Yahoo inbox after Epstein's death.

- He said he supplied DDoSecrets with approximately 20,900 emails and file attachments.

- The DDoSecrets repository currently contains a dedicated Emails directory within its Epstein collection. DDDoSecrets

- The public DDoSecrets directory itself does not provide a forensic report demonstrating how the Yahoo material was acquired.

- I have not found a publicly available DDoSecrets report providing a systematic analysis of:

  - DKIM signatures

  - `Message-ID` values

  - complete `Received:` headers

  - MIME structure

  - attachment hashes

  - Yahoo server metadata

  - acquisition timestamps

  - cryptographic hashes for the complete collection

### Bloomberg

- Bloomberg reported obtaining more than 18,000 emails from Epstein's personal Yahoo account.

- Bloomberg said it independently authenticated the material.

- Its reported methodology included:

  - cryptographic analysis

  - examination of email metadata

  - comparison with outside information

  - review by independent experts

- Bloomberg also reported finding evidence that some messages had been deleted, which is potentially important when comparing two separate acquisitions.

- Bloomberg's authentication therefore provides considerably more publicly documented forensic support for its own cache than is publicly available for the DDoSecrets collection.

## Not Different Datasets

The numerical difference is interesting, but it isn't sufficient to establish that the collections are different.

Possible explanations include:

- Different mailbox states

  - Kyrie may have captured the account at a different point in time than Bloomberg's source.

- Different folders

  - One acquisition could contain Sent, Archive, Trash, Drafts or other mailbox folders that weren't included in the other.

- Deleted messages

  - Bloomberg specifically reported evidence of deleted emails.

  - A mailbox acquisition performed at a different time could therefore contain a different population of messages.

- Deduplication

  - A raw mailbox extraction can contain multiple copies or representations of the same message.

- Different counting conventions

  - Kyrie's reported figure combines emails and attachments, whereas Bloomberg's published figure is generally described as more than 18,000 emails/messages.

  - Those aren't necessarily equivalent counting units.

- Partial transfer

  - Kyrie may have obtained approximately 20,900 items but DDoSecrets or a subsequent researcher may have processed only part of the collection.

## What would prove the two caches overlap?

A proper forensic comparison would start with the individual messages rather than the headline counts.

Researchers could compare:

- `Message-ID`

- sender

- recipient

- date/time

- subject

- `In-Reply-To`

- `References`

- complete `Received:` headers

- MIME boundaries

- message body

- attachment names

- attachment sizes

- attachment SHA-256 hashes

- DKIM signatures

- Yahoo-specific metadata

- folder/location information

The most convincing evidence would be repeated matches of something like:

### Message-ID

- identical

### Raw MIME

- identical

### Message body

- identical

### Attachment

- identical

### Attachment SHA-256

- identical

### Relevant headers

- identical

If thousands of messages matched at that level, it would be extremely strong evidence that the two collections originated from the same underlying mailbox state or from copies of the same acquisition.

## Why Message-ID is particularly useful

The `Message-ID` field can act as a useful cross-dataset fingerprint.

For example:

- DDoSecrets contains message `ABC123@example`

- Bloomberg contains the same `Message-ID`

- Both contain the same timestamp

- Both contain the same MIME structure

- Both contain the same attachment

- The attachment produces the same SHA-256 hash

One match is interesting.

Thousands of independent matches would be highly significant.

It still wouldn't necessarily prove that Bloomberg physically received the *same files* that Kyrie gave DDoSecrets. It could instead demonstrate that both sources possessed copies derived from the same underlying mailbox data.

## DKIM is even more interesting

Bloomberg's reported use of cryptographic analysis is important because authentication based on preserved email cryptographic information is much stronger than simply looking at the text of an email.

A preserved DKIM signature can help establish that signed portions of a message correspond to what passed through the relevant mail infrastructure.

### But there is an important limitation

Authenticating an email does not automatically authenticate the provenance of the entire archive containing that email.

In other words:

> A genuine Yahoo email inside Bloomberg's collection is evidence that the email itself is genuine.

It does not, by itself, prove:

> Kyrie obtained that email in the manner he described.

Those are separate propositions.

## Unresolved Question

The biggest missing piece is a public side-by-side forensic comparison of the DDoSecrets and Bloomberg datasets.

Ideally, researchers would publish something like:

- Number of DDoSecrets messages

- Number of Bloomberg messages

- Exact `Message-ID` matches

- Exact body matches

- Exact attachment matches

- Unique DDoSecrets messages

- Unique Bloomberg messages

- Number with matching DKIM signatures

- Number with matching raw MIME

That would answer the question much more definitively than comparing the headline counts of 20,900 and 18,000.

## My assessment

Based on the publicly available evidence, I'd rank the propositions this way:

### Very well supported

- Bloomberg obtained a large collection of Epstein Yahoo emails and performed substantial authentication work on it.

### Well supported

- DDoSecrets possesses a substantial Epstein email collection; its current repository explicitly contains an Emails directory. DDDoSecrets

### Strongly plausible

- The DDoSecrets/Kyrie material and Bloomberg material contain substantial overlap from the same Yahoo mailbox.

### Not demonstrated

- The DDoSecrets collection and Bloomberg collection are identical.

### Not demonstrated

- Bloomberg obtained the exact copy originally supplied by Kyrie to DDoSecrets.

### Not independently demonstrated

- Kyrie's account of seeing simultaneous active sessions establishes that other people were accessing the Yahoo account at the same time.

## I would describe...

Two independently acquired collections apparently originating from the same Epstein Yahoo mailbox, with substantial overlap likely, but without a publicly documented forensic diff proving exactly how much they share.

That is a more defensible characterization than saying simply that Bloomberg confirmed Kyrie's cache.
