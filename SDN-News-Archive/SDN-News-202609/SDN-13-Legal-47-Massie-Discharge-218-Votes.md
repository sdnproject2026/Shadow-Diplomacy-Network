## Summary

### September 28, 2026

The article is broadly describing a real bipartisan effort by Rep. Thomas Massie and Rep. Ro Khanna to force House consideration of the Epstein Files Transparency Act II, H.R. 9694.

But there is an important procedural distinction that the article blurs.

Massie and Khanna announced on September 18, 2026 that they had secured commitments for the 218 signatures needed to force another House vote. Axios reported that development and said significant action was expected after the midterm elections. 

The 218 number is important because a House discharge petition requires signatures from a majority of the House, currently 218 members. Once the required number is formally signed, the discharge procedure can move forward under House Rule XV. 

However, "218 commitments" is not exactly the same thing as "218 signatures formally recorded by the House Clerk."

As of September 28, 2026, I can verify the Axios report of 218 commitments, but I cannot verify from the House Clerk record that 218 formal signatures on an H.R. 9694 discharge petition have been publicly recorded.

There is a potentially confusing House Clerk record dated August 31, 2026 involving Thomas Massie and a discharge petition that later accumulated signatures. 

### H.Res. 1430

But that petition is for H.Res. 1430, a resolution concerning consideration of H.R. 185. It is not itself a discharge petition for H.R. 9694. 

### So the safest interpretation is:

- Massie and Khanna say they have enough commitments

- The stated target is 218 formal signatures

- The House is in recess

- The actual legislative confrontation is therefore expected when the House returns

- The 218 commitments do not mean that H.R. 9694 has already passed the House

# What H.R. 9694 Actually Does

The original Epstein Files Transparency Act became Public Law 119-38 on November 19, 2025. It directed the Attorney General to make specified unclassified Epstein-related records publicly available, subject to statutory limitations. 

H.R. 9694 is designed as an enforcement mechanism for that existing law.

According to the sponsors, EFTA II would:

- Give Epstein survivors a private right of action concerning unlawful withholding or redaction

- Give state attorneys general and certain state and local officials standing to challenge DOJ withholding

- Give members of Congress a means to challenge alleged noncompliance

- Allow victims to seek unredacted records concerning themselves and the harm they suffered

- Require DOJ to provide certain unredacted material to state and local prosecutors

- Apply existing federal criminal penalties to certain knowing concealment, destruction, falsification or withholding of records

- Restrict DOJ's ability to rely on certain common-law privileges to avoid disclosure

Those provisions come from the sponsors' description of H.R. 9694, so claims about what the bill would actually accomplish should be distinguished from claims about what DOJ has or has not unlawfully done. 

# Why There Is A Second Bill

The basic problem is that Congress passed the original transparency law, but there has continued to be a dispute over whether DOJ has completely complied with it.

The sponsors have alleged that millions of responsive files remain withheld and that some released material is excessively redacted. Those are the sponsors' allegations, not an independently established fact that should automatically be treated as proven. 

DOJ's own Epstein Library shows that it has released a substantial body of material and that victim-identifying information is redacted. The DOJ library was updated September 11, 2026. 

There is therefore a genuine dispute over both the quantity of material that remains withheld and the legality or appropriateness of particular redactions.

# What Happens Next

There are several separate steps.

## Step 1

### House Returns

The House must return from its pre-election recess before the next major floor confrontation can occur.

The timing matters because the House schedule has already pushed the Epstein issue into the post-election period. 

## Step 2

### The 218 Commitment Claim Becomes A Procedural Test

If the supporters actually obtain and maintain 218 formal signatures on the relevant discharge mechanism, House leadership has substantially less ability to prevent the underlying issue from receiving floor consideration.

The important distinction is that a discharge petition is a procedural mechanism. It does not itself enact the bill.

The House still has to take the appropriate procedural vote and then consider the legislation.

## Step 3

### House Vote On EFTA II

If the discharge procedure succeeds, the House could consider H.R. 9694.

At that point, the question changes from:

"Can Massie and Khanna force consideration?"

to:

"Will the House actually approve EFTA II?"

Those are two different questions.

I would not assume the outcome of that vote.

## Step 4

### Senate

There is a Senate version sponsored by Jeff Merkley and Ben Ray Luján.

For EFTA II to become law, both chambers would ultimately have to approve the same legislation and it would then go to the President.

Therefore, even a successful House vote would not automatically produce new disclosure requirements.

## Step 5

### DOJ And The Courts Continue Separately

This is potentially the most important part of the situation.

The congressional process is not the only mechanism operating.

There is already litigation concerning DOJ's compliance, and a federal judge has warned Blanche about potential contempt proceedings if DOJ fails to comply with a relevant court order. 

That means the next significant disclosure could theoretically come from:

- A congressional vote

- A DOJ release

- A court order

- A negotiated DOJ response

- Or some combination of those

## Scenario 3

### House Passes EFTA II But Senate Becomes The Bottleneck

Even if the House approves the bill, the Senate would have to act on its version.

The Senate version already exists, but introduction is not passage. 

The Senate therefore represents a separate legislative hurdle.

### Litigation Moves Faster Than Congress

The court proceedings involving DOJ could produce a concrete result before Congress finishes acting.

If a court orders additional disclosure or rejects particular redactions, that could produce changes to the files independently of EFTA II.

The September warning to Blanche makes this pathway particularly relevant.
