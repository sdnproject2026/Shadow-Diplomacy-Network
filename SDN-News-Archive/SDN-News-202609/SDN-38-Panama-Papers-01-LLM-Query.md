### The crucial methodological limitation

The study was not an accounting of money actually proven to have been hidden through Panama Papers entities.

Its €88 billion figure concerned modeled tax-base erosion, particularly profit shifting.

The researchers noted that the Orbis database captured only about 10% of the relevant individuals in their sample and that the analysis concentrated on corporate income tax.

Personal income, capital gains, inheritance, property taxes and other channels were not comprehensively incorporated.

So the correct interpretation is:

> The European Parliament study estimated the fiscal exposure associated with Panama-Papers-type offshore structures; it did not establish that €109–237 billion was literally stolen from EU treasuries by the Panama Papers defendants.

This distinction is especially important because tax avoidance, aggressive tax planning, tax evasion and money laundering are legally different phenomena.

## 2. Network theory

### How Global Jurisdictions Become Tax-evasion Nodes

The network-theory research takes a fundamentally different approach.

Instead of treating the Panama Papers as a collection of individual scandals, it treats the data as a network. Offshore financial entities are modeled as links connecting jurisdictions: the source jurisdiction is where the beneficial owner is located, while the host jurisdiction is where the offshore entity is registered. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

### The Network Structure

- The Panama Papers dataset contains information on more than 200,000 offshore financial entities established through Mossack Fonseca.

- Each offshore entity can function as a connection between jurisdictions, allowing wealth and ownership relationships to cross national borders while obscuring the identity of the beneficial owner.

- The resulting global network is not random.

- Researchers identified a core-periphery structure, meaning a relatively interconnected core of important jurisdictions is surrounded by less-connected peripheral jurisdictions.

- The network also exhibits a fat-tail degree distribution, meaning a relatively small number of jurisdictions account for a disproportionately large number of connections. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

### Central Jurisdictions

Network centrality provides a way to identify jurisdictions that occupy strategically important positions in the offshore system.

A jurisdiction with high centrality can influence or connect many other parts of the network even when the jurisdiction itself is not the final destination of the money.

This changes the policy question from:

> Which individual offshore company should regulators investigate?

to:

> Which jurisdictions occupy the most important positions in the overall system?

The researchers therefore argue that highly central tax-haven jurisdictions should receive priority in international enforcement and transparency efforts. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

## 3. Acquittal

### June 2024 Panama Mass Acquittal

On June 28, 2024, Panamanian judge Baloisa Marquínez acquitted the defendants who remained in the Panama Papers money-laundering trial.

The ruling covered 28 defendants, including Jürgen Mossack, one of the founders of Mossack Fonseca. Ramón Fonseca, the firm's other founder, died in May 2024 before the trial concluded, resulting in the dismissal of proceedings against him. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

### The Central Legal Problem

The acquittal was not a judicial finding that the Panama Papers revelations were false, that offshore companies were inherently legitimate, or that money laundering had never occurred.

Instead, the court concluded that the prosecution had failed to establish the defendants' criminal responsibility to the required legal standard.

A central problem concerned the evidentiary integrity of the information obtained from Mossack Fonseca.

The court found that the evidence extracted from the firm's computer systems did not satisfy the required chain-of-custody standards.

As a result, important electronic evidence could not properly be relied upon in establishing the criminal charges. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

### Insufficient Proof Of Illicit Funds

The court also found insufficient evidence establishing the necessary connection between alleged illicit funds and the money-laundering activity charged in the case.

In particular, the prosecution failed to sufficiently demonstrate that illicit funds originating from Brazil entered Panama in the manner necessary to establish the alleged concealment of criminal proceeds associated with the Operation Car Wash investigation. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

### What The Acquittal Means

The distinction between the Panama Papers investigation and the Panamanian criminal prosecution is critical.

The Panama Papers exposed an enormous offshore corporate infrastructure and generated evidence that governments and journalists used for investigations, tax enforcement and regulatory reforms.

The 2024 criminal case, however, required prosecutors to prove specific criminal conduct by specific defendants using evidence admissible under Panamanian law.

The acquittal therefore does not mean:

- The Panama Papers were fabricated.

- Mossack Fonseca's entire offshore business was illegal.

- Offshore companies are inherently criminal.

- The worldwide offshore network did not exist.

- Tax evasion and money laundering documented elsewhere were disproven.

Instead, it means that the prosecution in this particular criminal proceeding failed to prove the charged money-laundering offenses with evidence that the court considered legally sufficient. :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}

### Why The Acquittal Is Significant

The case illustrates the difference between investigative evidence and criminal-trial evidence.

The Panama Papers contained millions of leaked records and generated extensive international reporting. But a criminal conviction requires prosecutors to establish the admissibility, authenticity, provenance and evidentiary chain of the specific material used against the accused.

The court's rejection of key evidence therefore created a decisive gap between the enormous investigative significance of the Panama Papers and the narrower evidentiary requirements of the criminal prosecution.

The result was a mass acquittal despite the Panama Papers' continuing significance as evidence of the global structure of offshore finance. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}

## Bottom Line

The June 2024 acquittal should be understood as an evidentiary and legal failure of the prosecution, not as a judicial exoneration of the entire offshore system exposed by the Panama Papers.

The network research and the criminal verdict address fundamentally different questions:

- Network research: How is the global offshore system structurally connected?

- Panama Papers investigation: What relationships, entities and transactions were exposed?

- Criminal trial: Did prosecutors prove the charged money-laundering offenses against these particular defendants with admissible evidence?

The first two questions can remain significant even when the prosecution fails on the third.
