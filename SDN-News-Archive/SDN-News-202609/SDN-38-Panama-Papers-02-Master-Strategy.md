# Panama Papers Acquittal 

## How Not To Construct The Next Case

The central legal insight is that the 2024/2026 Panama litigation failure may actually teach a future investigator how not to construct the next case.

### Authenticated Primary Evidence

Rather than attempting to resurrect a failed prosecution through the same compromised digital corpus, a foreign investigation could use the Panama Papers to locate the transactions and then obtain fresh, independently authenticated primary evidence from the banks, registries, accountants, trustees, counterparties and government agencies that originally created the records.

### Another Sovereign

That would not be "retrying the Panama Papers case." It would be a new prosecution based on independently established conduct occurring within the jurisdiction of another sovereign.

And that distinction is potentially decisive.

## Detailed Legal Analysis

The strongest legal conclusion is that the 2024 Panama Papers acquittal does not create a worldwide "acquittal" of the underlying financial conduct. It creates a major obstacle to prosecuting the same defendants for the same Panamanian criminal cause, while leaving substantial room for independent investigations and prosecutions in other sovereign jurisdictions based on independently obtained evidence.

There is also an important update to the article supplied: the acquittal has now been affirmed on appeal in Panama. In August 2026, Panama's Superior Tribunal of Liquidation of Criminal Cases confirmed the June 28, 2024 acquittal, including the finding that the Mossack Fonseca server evidence failed chain-of-custody requirements and that the remaining evidence was insufficient and inconclusive.

## 2024 acquittal actually decided

Judge Baloísa Marquínez's June 28, 2024 judgment acquitted 28 defendants in the Panama Papers proceeding. The Panamanian judiciary stated that the electronic evidence taken from Mossack Fonseca's servers and introduced in paper form did not satisfy chain-of-custody requirements and lacked hash values sufficient to establish authenticity and integrity. The court also found the remaining evidence insufficient to establish criminal responsibility.

That is materially different from a factual declaration that:

- the Mossack Fonseca records were fabricated;

- the offshore entities did not exist;

- the transactions did not occur;

- every offshore structure was lawful;

- tax evasion did not occur anywhere;

- money laundering did not occur anywhere; or

- individuals appearing in the Panama Papers were innocent of unrelated crimes.

The distinction is critical.

The judgment principally concerns proof of criminal liability against the defendants in that proceeding.

The evidentiary failure was particularly consequential because the prosecution's digital evidence was not sufficiently authenticated under the applicable Panamanian rules.

## The 2026 appellate development

This is no longer simply a question of whether Panama's prosecution could appeal.

The prosecution did appeal after the 2024 decision.

As of August 2026, the Superior Tribunal has confirmed the acquittal.

That makes a strategy based on simply "reprosecuting the Panama Papers defendants in Panama with better evidence" substantially harder.

Panama's constitutional framework protects against being tried more than once for the same criminal cause. The principle is also reflected in Article 8(4) of the American Convention on Human Rights and Article 14(7) of the ICCPR.

The practical result is:

> Better evidence does not ordinarily permit Panama to simply restart the same criminal prosecution against the same person for the same criminal cause after a final acquittal.

The fact that the first prosecution may have been poorly constructed does not itself eliminate double-jeopardy protection.

The Panama judgment does not automatically immunize conduct occurring in:

### Basic architecture

A foreign prosecutor could theoretically investigate:

1. Person.

2. Bank account.

3. Shell company.

4. Transaction.

5. Underlying crime.

6. Criminal proceeds.

7. Laundering transaction.

rather than simply:

1. Person.

2. Mossack Fonseca.

3. Panama Papers.

4. Panama money-laundering case.

That distinction could be decisive.

For example, a U.S. prosecution might concern:

- U.S. tax offenses;

- wire fraud;

- bank fraud;

- money laundering;

- conspiracy;

- obstruction;

- securities offenses;

- violations involving U.S. financial institutions;

- foreign proceeds subsequently entering the United States.

A European prosecution could instead concern:

- tax fraud;

- aggravated tax evasion;

- laundering of criminal proceeds;

- false declarations;

- corruption;

- securities violations;

- participation in a criminal organization.

The same underlying transaction can generate different sovereign interests and different offenses, although double-jeopardy and international *ne bis in idem* questions must be analyzed jurisdiction by jurisdiction.

# No Redo through another country

This is the major limitation.

A prosecutor cannot necessarily say:

> "Panama failed to prove it, so we'll prosecute the exact same defendants for exactly the same conduct somewhere else."

Courts can scrutinize:

- double jeopardy;

- *ne bis in idem*;

- abuse of process;

- collateral estoppel;

- jurisdiction;

- extradition restrictions;

- treaty obligations;

- specialty limitations;

- evidentiary independence;

- and constitutional due process.

Panama's jurisprudence describes the relevant double-jeopardy inquiry in terms of identity of the person, act and prosecutorial cause.

Therefore, a foreign case should not be constructed as:

1. Panama Papers acquittal.

2. Same defendants.

3. Same charged offense.

4. Same factual transaction.

5. New trial.

The stronger theory is:

1. Independently established conduct.

2. Conduct occurring within the foreign state's jurisdiction.

3. Independently obtained evidence.

4. Different statutory offense or sovereign injury.

5. Compliance with that sovereign's constitutional and procedural requirements.

# The Epstein angle 

## Potentially More Interesting

There is a potentially significant investigative avenue here, but it requires separating documentary leads from proof of criminality.

The released Epstein-related material establishes extensive historical connections to financial institutions and offshore structures.

EFTA material contains Bear Stearns account records associated with Financial Trust Company, Inc., including records going back to the 1990s.

Other EFTA material documents disputes between Epstein/Financial Trust Company and Bear Stearns concerning investments in Bear Stearns structured-credit and overseas funds.

There is also documentary evidence of Epstein's early relationship with Bear Stearns. Contemporary reporting describes his recruitment by Bear Stearns executive Ace Greenberg and his use of the Bear Stearns connection as an important early source of financial relationships.

But there is an important evidentiary caution:

The released material should not presently be characterized as proving that Bear Stearns hired Epstein specifically to operate illegal offshore shell-company accounts.

The documents establish financial relationships, accounts, investments and offshore structures. They do not, standing alone, establish the mens rea required for money laundering, tax evasion or RICO.

That distinction is essential.

## Offshore Evidence Powerful

The investigation becomes much more interesting if investigators construct a chronological financial network.

For example:

1. 1970s-1980s financial employment and relationships.

2. Bear Stearns relationship.

3. Financial Trust Company.

4. Offshore entities, trusts and investment vehicles.

5. Panama and other offshore jurisdictions.

6. Bank accounts.

7. Wire transfers.

8. Beneficial owners and counterparties.

9. Assets and properties.

10. Potential criminal proceeds.

The objective should not be to prove:

> "Epstein appears in offshore records, therefore money laundering occurred."

Instead, investigators would attempt to establish:

1. Who owned the entity?

2. Who controlled it?

3. Who authorized the transaction?

4. Where did the money originate?

5. What was the stated purpose?

6. What was the actual economic purpose?

7. Who ultimately received the money?

8. Was there an underlying crime?

9. Did the transaction conceal ownership, source, destination or control?

10. Did the defendant know the money represented criminal proceeds?

That converts an investigative database into a potentially prosecutable financial case.

## G. Maxwell is relevant

The federal Maxwell prosecution provides a potentially useful independent evidentiary foundation, but her conviction does not establish that every financial transaction associated with Epstein was criminal.

The federal court's description of the evidence supporting Maxwell's conviction included testimony from victims, law enforcement, former employees, photographs, videos, contact books, physical evidence, FedEx records and flight logs. The court found that Maxwell participated in an approximately decade-long scheme with Epstein involving grooming, transportation and trafficking of young women and girls.

Thus:

1. Maxwell conviction establishes certain adjudicated criminal conduct.

2. That establishes a potential predicate factual foundation.

3. It does not automatically establish that every Epstein offshore transaction involved criminal proceeds.

4. A prosecutor would still need to establish the financial nexus.

That nexus could potentially be constructed through:

1. Predicate criminal activity.

2. Money or property generated or acquired through that activity.

3. Epstein-controlled account or entity.

4. Transfer to a shell company.

5. Panama or other offshore intermediary.

6. Concealment, conversion, layering or another legally relevant financial transaction.

7. Later transfer or asset acquisition.

If that chain can be independently proven, the Panama Papers become corroborative intelligence rather than the indispensable evidentiary foundation.

## RICO more disciplined approach

A possible RICO theory should not simply be:

> "Epstein had lots of shell companies, therefore RICO."

RICO requires considerably more.

A federal RICO prosecution would generally require proof of an enterprise, a pattern of qualifying racketeering activity, and the required relationship between the defendant and the enterprise.

Potentially relevant predicate offenses could include certain forms of:

- fraud;

- money laundering;

- trafficking-related offenses;

- obstruction;

- bribery;

- extortion;

- and other enumerated predicate crimes.

The key investigative question therefore becomes:

> Can the financial network be connected to two or more qualifying predicate acts forming a legally sufficient pattern?

The shell-company architecture is then evidence of how the enterprise operated, rather than the crime itself.

# Tax evasion a different route

Tax cases can be particularly useful because they do not necessarily require proving that the entire offshore network was created for laundering.

Suppose investigators establish:

1. Taxable income.

2. Beneficial ownership concealed.

3. Offshore company.

4. Income routed through the entity.

5. False tax return or omission.

6. Tax deficiency.

7. Intentional evasion.

That could potentially constitute a tax offense independently of whether the same transaction satisfied a money-laundering statute.

This is also where the distinction in the supplied article regarding the European Parliament research is important.

The €88 billion figure described in the article is modeled tax-base erosion, not proof that €88 billion was criminally stolen.

That research can identify risk architecture.

It cannot substitute for:

1. Bank record.

2. Taxpayer.

3. Income.

4. Concealment.

5. Statutory violation.

6. Intent.

## Papers Are An Intelligence Index

This is probably the most legally useful way to conceptualize the data after the acquittal.

Instead of presenting:

> "Here is the Panama Papers database."

the investigator presents:

> "The database identifies Entity X, person Y, intermediary Z and transaction period A-B."

Then investigators obtain the underlying records from the entities that actually created or maintained them.

For example:

1. Panama Papers identify an entity.

2. Entity identifies registered agent.

3. Registered agent identifies bank.

4. Bank identifies account.

5. Account identifies beneficial owner.

6. Beneficial owner identifies transaction.

7. Transaction identifies counterparties.

8. Investigators obtain original bank and corporate records.

9. Records are independently authenticated.

10. Evidence is assembled without depending upon the leaked dataset as the principal trial exhibit.

At that point, the Panama Papers may no longer be the critical evidence.

They become the lead that tells investigators where to obtain admissible evidence.

## Reacquire The Underlying Data

Several routes potentially exist.

### Bank records

A prosecutor can seek:

- account-opening documents;

- KYC records;

- beneficial-owner certifications;

- transaction histories;

- SWIFT records;

- wire instructions;

- correspondent-bank records;

- suspicious-activity reports where legally accessible;

- internal compliance files;

- account correspondence;

- signature cards;

- corporate resolutions.

The key advantage is that these records are maintained by the bank that created or received them, not by the Panama Papers leak.

### Corporate registries

Investigators could obtain:

- incorporation documents;

- director appointments;

- shareholder registers;

- beneficial-owner information;

- annual filings;

- powers of attorney;

- registered-agent records;

- dissolution records.

Where public, these records can be obtained directly.

Where nonpublic, investigators may use:

- subpoena;

- search warrant;

- court order;

- mutual legal assistance;

- regulatory request;

- treaty mechanism.

### Accounting firms

Accounting firms may possess:

- general ledgers;

- journals;

- invoices;

- tax workpapers;

- reconciliations;

- beneficial-owner documentation;

- correspondence;

- transfer-pricing records.

These can be extraordinarily useful because they can connect transactions to economic purpose.

### Lawyers and registered agents

The obvious limitation is privilege.

But privilege is not necessarily absolute.

Investigators must distinguish:

legal advice

from

ordinary business services, transaction execution or corporate administration.

Crime-fraud exceptions and related doctrines may also become relevant, but whether particular records are protected is jurisdiction-specific.

## Mutual legal assistance

This is where the "secondary sovereign" concept becomes realistic.

Suppose:

1. United States investigation identifies a Panama entity.

2. The entity used a Panamanian bank.

3. Panamanian corporate records identify another offshore entity.

4. That entity used a British Virgin Islands structure.

5. The BVI structure used a Swiss bank.

6. The U.S. investigation seeks the relevant records through applicable international legal-assistance mechanisms.

7. Foreign authorities lawfully obtain and transmit authenticated evidence.

The resulting evidence is not:

> "a leaked Panama Papers spreadsheet."

It is:

> "A certified bank or corporate record produced pursuant to lawful process."

That is dramatically stronger.

# "Tainted" Panama Papers 

> does not mean "useless"

This is an important evidentiary distinction.

A leaked document can be:

- inadmissible itself;

- unreliable;

- incompletely authenticated;

while simultaneously providing a legitimate investigative lead.

For example:

1. Investigator sees a leaked bank-account number.

2. The investigator does not necessarily introduce the leaked spreadsheet at trial.

3. The investigator identifies the bank.

4. The investigator obtains the original account record.

5. The bank authenticates its own records.

6. The investigator establishes the actual transaction independently.

The fact that the lead came from questionable evidence does not necessarily mean every subsequently obtained independent record is automatically tainted.

The precise doctrine depends upon the jurisdiction, but the fundamental strategy is to establish an independent evidentiary source for the records ultimately offered at trial.

## Problem With The Epstein Theory

There is a temptation to construct a giant network theory:

1. Epstein.

2. Maxwell.

3. Bear Stearns.

4. Panama.

5. Mossack Fonseca.

6. Offshore accounts.

7. Trafficking.

8. Money laundering.

Legally, that is too broad.

A prosecutor needs to decompose it into individual propositions.

For example:

### Proposition A

- Epstein controlled Entity X.

### Proposition B

- Entity X maintained Account Y.

### Proposition C

- a specified amount entered Account Y.

### Proposition D

- the source of that money was criminal proceeds.

### Proposition E

- Defendant knew or had the requisite mental state concerning those proceeds.

### Proposition F

- Defendant conducted Transaction Z to conceal, disguise, transfer, promote or otherwise deal with those proceeds in violation of the applicable statute.

Only after establishing those propositions does the network become a criminal case.

## Powerful Investigation Opportunity

The released financial material shows that Epstein's financial network extended through numerous entities and banking institutions.

An independently assembled 2026 financial-record project reports a corpus of thousands of transactions across 14 financial institutions and multiple shell entities, based on public government and court records. That project is investigative rather than itself authoritative evidence, but it illustrates how the released records can be reconstructed into transaction-level evidence.

The more useful question therefore becomes:

> Which transactions can be reconstructed from primary bank records rather than inferred from leaked or secondary datasets?

That is exactly where a future prosecution would gain evidentiary strength.

### Primary Source Reconstruction Model

The ideal investigation would produce a transaction graph consisting of:

1. Predicate criminal activity.

2. Source funds.

3. Bank account.

4. Shell company.

5. Offshore account.

6. Asset or property.

7. Beneficial owner.

Every connection should ideally have an underlying primary document.

The evidentiary graph then becomes:

1. Financial relationship.

2. Underlying document.

3. Document custodian.

4. Authentication.

5. Hash or other integrity evidence where applicable.

6. Admissibility.

This is essentially the opposite of the evidentiary weakness identified in the Panama prosecution.

## RICO a multinational network

Potentially, but the prosecution would need to establish the statutory elements rather than simply demonstrate that the network was multinational.

A sophisticated theory could potentially allege:

1. Enterprise.

2. Common purpose or relationships.

3. Predicate offenses.

4. Pattern of qualifying activity.

## Reinforce One Another

Tax Evasion + Laundering + RICO

Theoretically, a mature investigation could develop multiple independent charging theories:

### Tax offense.

   - Establishes concealed income or other tax misconduct.

### Money laundering.

   - Establishes a qualifying financial transaction involving criminal proceeds and the required knowledge or intent.

### Fraud 

or another qualifying predicate offense.

   - Establishes a racketeering predicate.

### Pattern.

   - Connects multiple qualifying acts.

### Enterprise.

   - Connects the actors and activities into the legally required enterprise.

But prosecutors should not assume that proving one automatically proves the others.

Each statute has its own:

- actus reus;

- mens rea;

- jurisdictional requirements;

- limitations period;

- evidentiary requirements.

## Legal Assessment

### The Panama acquittal

Strong protection:

The 2026 appellate confirmation makes it substantially more difficult to reopen the same Panamanian criminal case against the same defendants for the same cause. Panama's constitutional *ne bis in idem* protection is significant.

### Foreign sovereign

Potentially viable:

A separate sovereign can investigate and prosecute conduct within its jurisdiction, subject to its own double-jeopardy, jurisdictional, limitation and treaty rules.

### Panama Papers

Still highly useful:

Primarily as an investigative map rather than automatically admissible proof.

### EFTA

Potentially highly useful:

The released records can provide independent documentary leads concerning Epstein's financial relationships, accounts and entities. The Bear Stearns-related records are particularly relevant to reconstructing his historical financial architecture.

### Maxwell

Potentially important predicate evidence:

Her federal conviction establishes adjudicated criminal conduct involving Epstein's trafficking scheme, but it does not automatically establish that any particular offshore transaction involved criminal proceeds.

### RICO

Possible but demanding:

Requires an actual enterprise, pattern and qualifying predicate-act theory, not merely a network diagram.

### Tax evasion

Potentially independent:

Could be pursued based on tax residency, income, ownership, declarations and concealment independently of the Panama prosecution.

### Money laundering

Potentially strongest financial theory where provable:

The critical issue is establishing both the criminal source of funds and the defendant's knowledge and intent concerning the laundering transaction.

### Chain of custody

The key lesson of Panama:

Reacquire the underlying evidence from the original banks, corporate registries, accountants, trustees, counterparties and government agencies using lawful compulsory process.
