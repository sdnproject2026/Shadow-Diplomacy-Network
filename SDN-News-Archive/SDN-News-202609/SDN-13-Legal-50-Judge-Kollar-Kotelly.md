## Article

Yes, this is true and remains an active legal development today.

On September 30, 2026, federal judge Colleen Kollar-Kotelly officially ordered the Department of Justice to prioritize and fast-track the processing of these specific records.

Key facts about where this stands right now:

### The Ruling

- The order dictates that the DOJ must process Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) requests submitted by the watchdog group Democracy Defenders Fund on an expedited timetable rather than its standard, slower schedule.

### Current Status

- This order forces the DOJ to speed up its search and review process, though the department is still legally permitted to review the files for standard FOIA redactions before any public release takes place.

## Timeline

### 2025-07-22

- Democracy Defenders Fund submitted a FOIA request to the DOJ Criminal Division seeking Epstein and Maxwell investigative records that mention Donald Trump, Mar-a-Lago, or related pseudonyms

- DOJ could fail to identify, preserve, search, or segregate responsive records within the requested scope, particularly records held across different offices or systems

### 2025-07-28

- DDF submitted additional FOIA requests to the DOJ Criminal Division and the Office of the Attorney General and Office of the Deputy Attorney General seeking records concerning Epstein and Maxwell and communications among specified senior officials

- responsible officials could delay routing, searching, reviewing, or referring records, resulting in administrative noncompliance

### 2025-08-21

- DDF filed the federal lawsuit, Democracy Defenders Fund v. U.S. Department of Justice, challenging DOJ's handling of the FOIA requests

- continued delay after litigation began could expose the agency to additional court proceedings and possible compelled production

### 2026-09-30

- Judge Colleen Kollar-Kotelly ruled that DDF was entitled to expedited processing under the DOJ regulation covering matters of widespread and exceptional media interest involving possible questions about government integrity

- The court granted expedited processing for the July 22 request and most of the July 28 requests

- The court excluded portions of the July 28 requests seeking records from "any" DOJ official because those portions were considered overbroad

- The court did not find that Donald Trump or any named official committed misconduct

- The court also did not order immediate public release of the records

- The statutory "compelling need" theory under FOIA was rejected because DDF had not established that it was primarily engaged in disseminating information

- after this ruling, failure to treat the qualifying requests as expedited could create a new basis for DDF to seek judicial enforcement or additional relief

### 2026-10-04

- the expedited-processing ruling is active

- There is no verified court-imposed document-production deadline in the September 30 order

- The most important distinction is that "expedited processing" is not the same thing as "immediate disclosure"

- DOJ may still conduct searches, review responsive material, apply lawful FOIA exemptions and redactions, and address other procedural requirements before releasing records

- It would be speculation to state that DOJ is currently violating the order merely because the records have not yet been released

- DOJ could fail to process the qualifying requests promptly, conduct an inadequate search, improperly withhold responsive records, apply unsupported redactions, or otherwise fail to implement the expedited status. Those are hypothetical compliance risks, not established present violations

### Future Deadline

- No specific future production date has been established by the September 30, 2026 court ruling that I could verify

- The legally significant near-term event is therefore DOJ's actual implementation of expedited processing, followed by any production, withholding, redaction, or further litigation that results

- If DOJ does not comply with the expedited-processing requirement, DDF could potentially return to court seeking enforcement or other relief, but the precise remedy and timing cannot be predicted with certainty
