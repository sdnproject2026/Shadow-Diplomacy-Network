# Epstein Document Timeline

Congressional oversight concerning Department of Justice handling of Jeffrey Epstein-related records remains the focus of ongoing public and legislative attention.

House Oversight activity has expanded in recent months to include requests for additional document production, subpoenas directed at private individuals and DOJ officials, and continued scrutiny of redactions and disclosure practices.

The timeline also summarizes upcoming requested production dates, committee deadlines, and anticipated litigation milestones.

# Requests and Deadlines

July 25–31, 2026, there were significant developments involving the US Department of Justice (DOJ) and the Epstein files.

- On July 28, 2026, a federal judge ordered the DOJ to either provide unredacted versions of certain Epstein-related records to the court for _in camera_ (private judicial) review or explain why the redactions should remain.

- On July 27–29, 2026, news reports stated that the DOJ was expected to submit unredacted documents to the judge, not to the public. The judge would then decide whether additional material should be released publicly.

- As of July 30, 2026, the DOJ's public Epstein document library had not been updated since July 17, 2026.

### Synopsis

On June 26, 2026, the House Oversight Committee subpoenaed Leon Black after he declined to answer questions concerning NDAs during a voluntary interview.

The Committee sought documents that could identify individuals or entities who entered into confidentiality agreements related to Jeffrey Epstein or associated matters.

The subpoenas required production of documents by July 10, 2026, followed by a compelled deposition on July 16, 2026.

### Current Status

- The House Oversight Committee has not publicly released the subpoenaed NDA documents or a transcript of Black's deposition.

### Consequences

If Leon Black failed to comply with both subpoena deadlines - July 10, 2026 for document production and July 16, 2026 for the deposition - and did so without obtaining an extension or other accommodation from the Committee, he would be subject to potential congressional enforcement measures.

These could include:

- A formal finding that he failed to comply with duly issued congressional subpoenas.

- A Committee vote recommending that the House hold him in contempt of Congress.

- Referral to the full House for a contempt resolution.

- If the House adopted the resolution, referral to the US Attorney for the District of Columbia for potential criminal contempt proceedings under applicable federal law, or pursuit of civil enforcement through the courts.

- Additional subpoenas or other compulsory process to obtain the requested testimony and documents.

As of July 30, 2026, the House Oversight Committee has not publicly announced that Leon Black ignored both deadlines, nor has it publicly initiated contempt or other enforcement proceedings.

Therefore, any consequences described above remain the potential legal and procedural outcomes if the Committee determines that the subpoenas were not complied with.
