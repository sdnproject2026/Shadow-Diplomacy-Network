## Current Status

as of August 4, 2026

On July 15, 2026, Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY), Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA), and Rep. Teresa Leger Fernández (D-NM) introduced H.R. 9694, the Epstein Files Transparency Act II, which was referred to the House Judiciary Committee.

A companion Senate bill is being led by Sens. Jeff Merkley (D-OR) and Ben Ray Luján (D-NM).

The bill's purpose is to strengthen enforcement of the original Epstein Files Transparency Act (Public Law 119-38), which became law in November 2025. Sponsors argue the DOJ failed to fully comply with that law's disclosure requirements.

## Key Provisions

- Grants state attorneys general standing to sue the US Attorney General for noncompliance.

- Grants victims standing to sue.

- Grants members of Congress standing to seek judicial enforcement.

- Creates legal remedies if the DOJ unlawfully:
  - withholds records,
  - delays publication,
  - excessively redacts records,
  - conceals records, or
  - otherwise fails to publish covered materials.

## Political Outlook

The legislation has bipartisan sponsorship and was introduced amid criticism that the DOJ had not fully complied with the disclosure requirements of the original Epstein Files Transparency Act.

Supporters contend the original law lacked meaningful enforcement mechanisms and that EFTA II would allow victims, states, and members of Congress to seek judicial enforcement when disclosure obligations are not met.
