# Phang vs. Blanche

There is an active legal battle over the federal government’s compliance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act (EFTA). 

The civil lawsuit was brought by independent journalist and attorney Katie Phang against the Department of Justice (DOJ) and Attorney General Todd Blanche. 

The case is being handled by veteran Senior US District Judge Emmet Sullivan.

## Detailed Case Timeline

### Late 2025 – Early 2026

- The Transparency Act Takes Effect

Congress passes the bipartisan Epstein Files Transparency Act, mandating that the federal government review and release all non-classified files and investigative records related to Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell.

### January 2026

- DOJ Claims Completion

The DOJ issues a statement asserting that it has fully complied with the Transparency Act’s mandates, establishing a "final agency action."

### April 2026

- Katie Phang Files Lawsuit

Frustrated by sweeping redactions, Katie Phang files a lawsuit under the Administrative Procedure Act.

She argues that the DOJ is unlawfully withholding documents, blacking out key email chains (such as exchanges regarding a "torture video"), and concealing information referring to Donald Trump.

### June 2026

- Preliminary Injunction Granted

Judge Sullivan grants Phang a preliminary injunction.

He orders the DOJ to either clear specific redactions or show legal cause why they cannot comply.

The records of interest include FBI interviews with an anonymous woman ("Jane Doe 4") who alleged she was abused by Trump when she was 13 years old.

### July 25–28, 2026

- In-Camera Review Ordered

Displeased with government resistance, Judge Sullivan orders Attorney General Blanche to submit the unredacted materials directly to his chambers for private (in camera) review.

### August 13, 2026

- Judge Frustration & Contempt Warning

Judge Sullivan convenes an explosive status hearing.

He expresses deep frustration with government attorneys, telling them, 

> The public has a right to know what the hell is going on in this case 

and reminds them that he has held government lawyers in contempt before.

### Mid-August 2026

- The "Handwritten Notes" Dispute

The legal battle focuses heavily on the original, handwritten interview notes taken by FBI agents when interviewing Trump's accuser.

DOJ counsel Andrew Block argues to Judge Sullivan that the government doesn't need to produce handwritten notes because the typewritten "FD-302" summary forms should suffice, adding that the DOJ "does not generally produce handwritten notes."

### August 20, 2026

- Phang's Lawyers Call Out Misrepresentation

Phang's legal team files a formal notice to the court debunking the DOJ's argument.

They point out that within the *very same* released Epstein database, the DOJ had already produced over 100 examples of handwritten notes for other individuals - meaning the government uniquely carved out an exception to hide the raw notes concerning Donald Trump.

### August 21, 2026

- Sullivan Orders "Show Cause" on Lying

Judge Sullivan issues an immediate order directing the DOJ to clarify whether its attorneys explicitly misled or lied to the court regarding the availability and historical production of these handwritten records.

### Monday, August 24, 2026

- DOJ Files Notice of Appeal

Rather than yielding to the handwritten document release, the DOJ signals its plan to escalate the battle, filing a notice to appeal Judge Sullivan's unredacting mandates to a higher federal court.

### Tuesday, August 25, 2026

- Proposed Order Submitted

Phang's team submits a proposed order mandating that AG Blanche produce the underlying handwritten FBI interview notes to protect the integrity of the Epstein Files Transparency Act.
