# Sephardic Jews

## 1492

### Spanish Expulsion of Jews

In 1492, the Spanish monarchy ordered the expulsion of Jews from Spain.

Sephardic Jews were forced to leave their homes and migrate to other regions, including the Caribbean.

Many Sephardic Jews became conversos (converted to Christianity) to avoid expulsion, though some continued to practice Judaism secretly.

### Players

- Queen Isabella I - Queen of Spain who ordered the expulsion of Jews from Spain in 1492.

- King Ferdinand II - King of Spain who, along with Isabella, enforced the expulsion of Jews.

- Moses ben Samuel Ibn Ezra - A prominent Sephardic Jewish scholar who was among the expelled Jews from Spain.

## 1500s

### Caribbean

After the expulsion from Spain, many Sephardic Jews settled in the Caribbean.

They were primarily attracted to Dutch-controlled islands like Curacao and Suriname for their relative religious tolerance and trading opportunities.

### Players

- David de Mercado - A Sephardic Jew who helped establish the Jewish community in Curacao.

- Joseph Pardo - A Sephardic Jewish merchant involved in the early trade in the Caribbean.

- Isaac Aboab da Fonseca - A notable rabbi and scholar who played an important role in the Sephardic Jewish community in the Dutch Caribbean.

## 1600s

### Commercial Activity

During the 1600s, Sephardic Jews in the Caribbean became involved in various commercial enterprises, including shipbuilding, banking, and plantation agriculture.

Many were involved in the transatlantic slave trade, bringing enslaved Africans to work on sugar and tobacco plantations.

### Players

- Samuel Cohen - A wealthy Sephardic Jewish merchant involved in Caribbean trade.

- Abraham de Lyon - A Sephardic Jewish financier and merchant who played a significant role in Caribbean commerce.

- Aaron Lopez - A successful Sephardic Jewish merchant and shipbuilder involved in both the Caribbean and New England trade.

## 1650s - 1700s

### Dutch Caribbean

Sephardic Jews established thriving communities in Curacao and other Dutch colonies.

These Jewish communities contributed significantly to trade, particularly the sugar trade and slave trading.

### Players

- David Nassy - A Sephardic Jewish plantation owner and influential figure in Curacao during the 1700s.

- Mordecai Abraham - A Sephardic Jewish merchant and plantation owner in Suriname during the 18th century.

- Benjamin de Pinto - A prominent Sephardic Jew involved in trade and plantation agriculture in the Caribbean.

## 1700s

### Jewish Plantations

Some Sephardic Jews in the Caribbean owned large sugar plantations, often utilizing enslaved Africans as labor.

This made them significant figures in the Caribbean economy during the 18th century.

### Players

- David Nassy - A Sephardic Jewish plantation owner and influential figure in Curacao during the 1700s.

- Mordecai Abraham - A Sephardic Jewish merchant and plantation owner in Suriname during the 18th century.

- Benjamin de Pinto - A prominent Sephardic Jew involved in trade and plantation agriculture in the Caribbean.

### (1980s-1990s)

- A Sephardic-born Israeli politician and diplomat, Hillel was involved in key peace negotiations and intelligence work, particularly in the realm of Arab-Israeli relations. His background in Sephardic heritage enriched his contributions to Israeli foreign affairs.

His work in intelligence and security, particularly around the Middle East, reflected a broad Sephardic Jewish influence on Israeli governmental affairs.

### (1990s-Present)

### (20th Century-Present) 

- A Jewish scholar and historian of Sephardic Jewish heritage, Laniado has published works on the history and culture of Sephardic Jews, particularly in the Caribbean. His academic work helps preserve the memory and legacy of Sephardic Jewish contributions to history and society.

# Other Jewish Clans

Besides Sephardic and Ashkenazi Jews, several other Jewish clans or communities remain particularly noteworthy in the context of global influence, including those whose members are involved in politics, business, and other areas of power between the 1980s and 2020s.

## Mizrahi Jews

### Region

- Middle East and North Africa (Iraq, Syria, Egypt, Iran, Yemen)

### History

- Mizrahi Jews have ancient roots in the Middle East, dating back to the Babylonian exile. They have a rich, diverse culture that evolved through centuries of living under Islamic rule.

### People

Tzipi Livni (1980s-Present) - Former Israeli Foreign Minister, and key player in peace negotiations with the Palestinians. Livni is of Mizrahi Jewish descent, born to a Sephardic father and Mizrahi mother.

Eli Yishai (1990s-Present) - Former Deputy Prime Minister of Israel and a leader of the Shas political party, which represents Mizrahi Jews and promotes their interests in Israeli politics.

## Beta Israel

### Region

- Ethiopia

### History

- Beta Israel, or Ethiopian Jews, have a unique and ancient heritage, dating back to biblical times, with traditions that remained isolated from other Jewish communities for centuries. They were brought to Israel in large numbers during Operation Moses (1984) and Operation Solomon (1991).

### People

Pnina Tamano-Shata (2010s-Present) - The first Ethiopian-Israeli woman to serve as a member of the Knesset and the Israeli Minister for Immigration and Integration. She is a key advocate for Ethiopian Jewish rights and integration into Israeli society.

Yosef Shagal (2000s-Present) - Prominent figure in Ethiopian-Jewish community organizations, advocating for the political and social rights of Ethiopian Jews in Israel.

## Romaniote Jews

### Region

- Greece, Turkey

### History

- Romaniote Jews trace their origins back to ancient Greece, with some of the oldest Jewish communities in Europe. They are distinct from Ashkenazi and Sephardic Jews, speaking Yevanic (a Greek-Jewish dialect).

### People

David Harris (1980s-Present) - A leader in the Jewish community, particularly influential in the American Jewish Committee (AJC). While of Ashkenazi descent, he has played a significant role in maintaining relations between Romaniote Jews and the broader Jewish world.

George M. Markowicz (2000s-Present) - A prominent advocate for the preservation of Romaniote Jewish heritage, involved in several Jewish cultural preservation initiatives in Greece.

## Karaite Jews

### Region

- Eastern Europe, Middle East (primarily Crimea, Turkey, Israel)

### History

- Karaite Jews reject rabbinic Judaism and adhere to a literal interpretation of the Hebrew Bible. This group has its origins in the 8th century, especially in the Middle East and Crimea.

### People

Dr. Mordechai Nisan (1990s-Present) - A prominent Karaite scholar and educator based in Israel, involved in the preservation and promotion of Karaite traditions.

Menashe Eilon (1980s-Present) - A well-known Karaite Israeli politician, advocating for the rights of Karaite Jews in Israel and their recognition within the broader Jewish community.

## Yemenite Jews

### Region

- Yemen (Arabian Peninsula)

### History

- Yemenite Jews have one of the oldest and most distinct Jewish traditions. Their religious practices and customs were largely isolated until their mass immigration to Israel during the 20th century.

### People

Shimon Peres (1980s-2010s) - Though his political career was primarily shaped by his Ashkenazi heritage, Peres' family background was part of the broader Sephardic and Yemenite Jewish diaspora, influencing his understanding of Israel's diverse cultural roots.

Naftali Bennett (2010s-Present) - Israel's Prime Minister, with a mixed heritage, including Yemenite Jewish ancestry on his father's side. He is one of the leading figures in Israeli politics, especially known for his right-wing stances.

## Persian (Iranian) Jews

### Region

- Iran (formerly Persia)

### History

- Persian Jews have a history that dates back over 2,000 years. They were once a dominant Jewish community in the Middle East but faced persecution following the Iranian Revolution in 1979.

### People

Natan Sharansky (1980s-Present) - Former Soviet dissident and Israeli politician, Sharansky’s heritage is rooted in both Ashkenazi and Persian Jewish communities. He has been influential in the global Jewish diaspora and human rights movements.

David Ben-Gurion (1940s-1970s) - While primarily associated with Ashkenazi roots, Ben-Gurion’s support for the Mizrahi Jews in Israel and the integration of Persian Jews into Israeli society remains a significant part of his legacy.

## Indian Jews 

(Bene Israel, Cochin Jews)

### Region

- India

### History

- The Bene Israel community in Maharashtra and the Cochin Jews in Kerala have roots that trace back over 2,000 years. The Bene Israel is believed to have been shipwrecked in India, while the Cochin Jews established a thriving community in Kerala.

### People

Samson H. S. (1910s-1980s) - A Bene Israel leader who helped establish Indian-Jewish influence in India and beyond, particularly in business and politics.

Ariela (Benny) Ghosh (2000s-Present) - A contemporary leader from the Bene Israel community, focusing on Jewish education and promoting the understanding of Indian-Jewish culture.

# Clans

## Sephardic Jews

(c. 1500s)

Region: Iberian Peninsula (Spain, Portugal)

Descendants of Jews expelled from Spain and Portugal in 1492, who spread across the Ottoman Empire, North Africa, the Caribbean, and the Americas. They have a rich cultural and religious heritage with a distinct Judeo-Spanish language (Ladino) and traditions in trade, commerce, and diplomacy.

## Ashkenazi Jews

(c. 1100s)

Region: Central and Eastern Europe (Germany, Poland, Russia)

The largest Jewish group today, originally from medieval German-speaking areas, later spreading to Eastern Europe. Known for their Yiddish language, distinctive religious practices, and significant contributions in intellectual, political, and cultural spheres, especially during the Enlightenment and modern times.

## Mizrahi Jews

(c. 2000s BCE)

Region: Middle East and North Africa (Iraq, Syria, Egypt, Iran, Yemen)

Jews from the Arab world with ancient roots in the Middle East, often culturally distinct from Ashkenazi and Sephardic Jews. Mizrahi communities have maintained ancient Jewish traditions and languages like Judeo-Arabic, and many were displaced following the establishment of Israel in 1948.

## Beta Israel 

(c. 1000 BCE)

Region: Ethiopia

An ancient Jewish community with a long history in Ethiopia, distinct in religious practices and customs. They maintained Jewish traditions despite isolation from other Jewish communities for centuries. In the 1980s-90s, many Ethiopian Jews immigrated to Israel.

## Romaniote Jews

(c. 300 BCE)

Region: Greece, Turkey

A Greek-speaking Jewish community that predates the Roman Empire. They have their own liturgical practices and language (Yevanic), and historically, they lived in Greece and Turkey, with a unique blend of Greek culture and Jewish traditions.

## Karaite Jews

(c. 700s)

Region: Eastern Europe, Middle East (primarily Crimea, Turkey, Israel)

A non-Talmudic Jewish group that rejects the rabbinic interpretation of the Torah. Karaites rely solely on the Hebrew Bible (Tanakh) and have distinct religious practices and customs. They have significant communities in Israel, the US, and Eastern Europe.

## Yemenite Jews

(c. 1000 BCE)

Region: Yemen (Arabian Peninsula)

An ancient community with unique religious practices, including a distinct prayer tradition and Torah script. Yemenite Jews have a deep connection to the Bible and early rabbinic teachings, and many emigrated to Israel in the mid-20th century.

## Persian Jews

(c. 600 BCE)

Region: Iran (formerly Persia)

An ancient Jewish community with roots dating back to the Babylonian exile. Persian Jews have maintained unique customs and a distinct dialect (Judeo-Persian) and continue to influence Jewish life in Iran and Israel today.

## Indian Jews 

(Bene Israel, Cochin Jews)

(c. 1000 BCE)

Region: India

A small but historically significant Jewish community in India, with distinct cultural and religious practices. The Bene Israel community in Maharashtra and the Cochin Jews in Kerala both have centuries-old histories, contributing to Jewish trade and culture in India.

## Chabad-Lubavitch

(c. 1700s)

Region: Eastern Europe (Poland, Russia)

A Hasidic movement founded in the 18th century, Chabad-Lubavitch is one of the most influential Jewish movements worldwide. Known for its outreach efforts and the Chabad network of schools, synagogues, and centers around the globe, it is a major force in Jewish education and spirituality.

## Breslov Hasidim

(c. 1800s)

Region: Ukraine, Israel

A Hasidic movement founded by Rabbi Nachman of Breslov, known for its focus on joy, prayer, and personal spiritual development. Breslov Hasidim maintain a strong presence in Israel, Ukraine, and the United States.

## Satmar Hasidim

(c. 1900s)

Region: Hungary, Israel, United States

A Hasidic group known for its strict adherence to traditional practices and its opposition to Zionism. They have a significant presence in the United States, particularly in Brooklyn, and maintain a distinct community-focused way of life.
