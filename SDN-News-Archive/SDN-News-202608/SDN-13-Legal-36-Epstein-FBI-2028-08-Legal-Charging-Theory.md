### Count theory A

### Count theory B

### Count theory C

-  18 USC. §1505: Defendant corruptly obstructed a pending congressional / federal proceeding by concealing or withholding specified documentary evidence.

### Count theory D

-  18 USC. §1001: Defendant knowingly and willfully made materially false statements or representations to a federal entity concerning the existence, production, withholding, or status of responsive records.

### Count theory E

-  18 USC. §371: Two or more defendants knowingly agreed to commit one or more of the foregoing offenses and committed an overt act in furtherance of the agreement.

Whether any of those counts is actually chargeable depends upon the evidence.

### Phase II  -  Record reconciliation

Build:

Master FBI inventory

- DOJ responsive inventory

- Documents actually released

- Documents redacted

- Documents withheld

- Documents later discovered / missing

That can reveal precisely where a record disappeared.

### Phase III  -  Intent evidence

For every discrepancy, ask:

- Who knew?

- When did they know?

- What did they do?

- Why did they do it?

- What did they tell others?

- What did they tell Congress?

- What did they tell the court?

This is where a potential criminal case either becomes strong or collapses.

### Phase IV  -  Independent investigation

The DOJ OIG's existing EFTA audit is directly relevant.

For allegations against DOJ / FBI officials, an independent investigative mechanism is especially important because the ordinary DOJ chain of command creates an obvious institutional conflict if the people investigating are themselves potentially implicated.

### Phase V  -  Referral

Depending upon the evidence:

- DOJ OIG;

- Congress;

- an appropriate US Attorney;

- another authorized federal prosecutor;

- potentially a special counsel / other independent mechanism if legally warranted;

could refer the matter for criminal investigation.

### Phase VI  -  Grand jury

Federal prosecutors present evidence to a grand jury.

The grand jury determines whether probable cause exists for an indictment.

### Phase VII  -  Indictment

The indictment identifies:

- defendant;

- statute;

- conduct;

- dates;

- records;

- intent;

- overt acts where required;

- conspiracy relationships where applicable.

### Phase VIII  -  Trial

The government must prove each element beyond a reasonable doubt.

### Phase IX  -  Conviction

Only then is the person legally a criminal defendant who has been adjudicated guilty.

### Phase X  -  Sentencing

Only after conviction does sentencing occur.

### 19. The key distinction

- unlawful withholding vs criminal concealment

This is the central issue.

Imagine two cases.

### Case A

DOJ says:

- “We believe Record X is privileged.”

A court later says:

- “No, it isn't.”

That can be unlawful withholding / noncompliance.

It isn't automatically a felony.

### Case B

DOJ official says privately:

- “We know Record X isn't privileged. Delete it from the production because it identifies a politically powerful person.”

Then the official creates a false record indicating X was released.

That potentially moves into:

§1519 + §1512(c)(1) + potentially §1001 + potentially §371.

### Case C

Congress subpoenas X.

Official intentionally refuses to produce it.

Potentially:

§192, and depending upon the conduct surrounding the refusal, potentially other obstruction statutes.

### Case D

A federal judge orders production of X.

Official deliberately destroys X to prevent compliance.

Now the court-order component makes the obstruction / contempt analysis substantially more serious.

## 20. So what is the legally accurate answer to your question?

As of August 16, 2026:

### Established

DOJ failed to fully comply with the Epstein Files Transparency Act.

A federal judge expressly found that DOJ was in violation and granted injunctive relief concerning specified records / redactions.

### Not established

That DOJ / FBI / POTUS committed a federal felony.

The existing public evidence described here does not establish beyond the statutory violation that a particular individual knowingly and corruptly destroyed, falsified, concealed, or obstructed records.

### Potential criminal theory

If the underlying evidence demonstrates intentional concealment / destruction / falsification, the most technically relevant federal offenses are:

Primary:

18 USC. §1519  -  destruction / alteration / falsification / concealment of records  -  up to 20 years.

Potentially additional:

- 18 USC. §1512(c)(1)  -  corrupt concealment / destruction of evidence  -  up to 20 years.

Depending on proceeding:

- 18 USC. §1505  -  obstruction of federal / congressional proceedings  -  ordinarily up to 5 years.

If materially false federal representations were knowingly made:

- 18 USC. §1001.

If records were willfully and unlawfully destroyed / concealed:

- 18 USC. §2071  -  up to 3 years.

If two or more people actually agreed to commit an offense:

- 18 USC. §371  -  conspiracy.

If a valid congressional subpoena was willfully disobeyed:

- 2 USC. §192  -  criminal contempt of Congress, with §194 providing the congressional referral mechanism.

And none of those should be treated as established charges against Trump, DOJ, FBI, or any named individual without evidence satisfying the elements.

The most productive next step is therefore not a generalized “charge DOJ / FBI / POTUS” case.

- It is a document-by-document criminal-evidence matrix identifying each allegedly missing / altered / withheld record, the legal duty covering it, the person who controlled it, what the original record says, what the released version says, what happened to it, who knew about that discrepancy, and the communications showing intent.

- That is the evidence needed to turn the existing proven statutory noncompliance into - or rule out - a prosecutable §1519/§1512/§1505/§1001/§371 case.
