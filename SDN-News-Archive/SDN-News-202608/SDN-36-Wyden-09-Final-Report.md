# Obstruction, Settlements and Lack of Accountability

## Obstruction from the Trump Administration and Congressional Republicans

During the course of Senator Wyden’s investigation, staff encountered significant obstruction from officials within the Trump Administration and from Congressional Republicans. This obstruction limited access to records, delayed responses to oversight requests, and hindered efforts to obtain information from federal agencies responsible for enforcing anti‑money‑laundering laws.

The investigation found that:

- federal agencies were slow to respond to document requests  

- key records related to Epstein’s financial activity were withheld or delayed  

- Congressional Republicans opposed efforts to expand oversight into Wall Street banks  

- attempts to obtain testimony from relevant officials were blocked or ignored  

These actions impeded the Committee’s ability to fully examine how federal regulators and law enforcement handled Epstein’s financial activity over nearly two decades.

## Banks and Epstein’s Estate Have Paid \$900 Million to Settle Epstein‑Related Lawsuits

Multiple financial institutions and Epstein’s estate have paid substantial settlements related to Epstein’s sex trafficking operations. These settlements total approximately \$900 million and include payments to:

- Epstein’s victims  

- the Government of the U.S. Virgin Islands  

- other plaintiffs who alleged wrongdoing by banks that enabled Epstein  

Major settlements include:

- JPMorgan Chase’s settlement with the U.S. Virgin Islands  

- Deutsche Bank’s settlement with Epstein’s victims  

- additional payments made by Epstein’s estate  

These settlements acknowledge that banks failed to meet their legal obligations and played a role in enabling Epstein’s criminal activity.

## Banks Refused to Cooperate with Senator Wyden’s Investigation

Despite the severity of the allegations, major Wall Street banks refused to fully cooperate with Senator Wyden’s investigation. This included:

- refusing to provide internal documents  

- declining to make executives available for interviews  

- withholding compliance records  

- limiting access to due diligence files  

- failing to answer detailed questions about their handling of Epstein’s accounts  

The lack of cooperation prevented investigators from obtaining a complete picture of how banks made decisions regarding Epstein’s accounts and why they failed to report suspicious activity.

## Top Bankers Have Faced No Consequences

Although banks have paid large settlements, individual executives responsible for compliance failures have faced no meaningful consequences. Many of the bankers who:

- managed Epstein’s accounts  

- ignored compliance warnings  

- coached Epstein on how to evade detection  

- failed to file timely SARs  

- continued working with Epstein after his termination  

remain employed in senior positions at their institutions.

The investigation found no evidence that:

- federal regulators disciplined these executives  

- banks imposed internal penalties  

- any individuals were referred for criminal prosecution  

This lack of accountability stands in stark contrast to the scale of the compliance failures and the harm caused by Epstein’s trafficking operations.

## Summary

Obstruction from federal officials and Congressional Republicans, combined with the refusal of major banks to cooperate, significantly hindered the investigation. Although banks have paid nearly \$900 million in settlements, the individuals responsible for enabling Epstein’s financial activity have not been held accountable. This systemic failure underscores the need for stronger oversight, enforcement and legislative reform.
