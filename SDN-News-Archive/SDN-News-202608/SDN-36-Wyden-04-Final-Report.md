# Bank of America Failed to Properly Screen and Report Leon Black’s Payments to Epstein

Between 2012 and 2017, billionaire financier Leon Black paid Jeffrey Epstein approximately \$170 million for purported tax and estate planning services. According to a settlement agreement signed by Black with the U.S. Virgin Islands, Epstein used the money Black paid him to fund his sex trafficking operations in the Virgin Islands.

Finance Committee investigators reviewed Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) filed by Bank of America in 2020 regarding Black’s accounts. These SARs were filed:

- years after the payments occurred  

- eight months after Epstein’s 2019 arrest  

- five to seven years after the wire transfers were completed  

The SARs raise serious questions about Bank of America’s handling of large wire transfers from Black to Epstein.

## Lack of Due Diligence

Treasury Department records show that Black and related entities maintained accounts at Bank of America that were used to send Epstein roughly \$170 million over five years. The payments were sent to two Epstein‑controlled entities:

- Southern Trust Company, Inc. (Deutsche Bank)  

- Financial Trust Company, Inc. (JPMorgan Chase)  

The transfers consisted of 18 large wire payments, generally between \$8 million and \$10 million, with some significantly larger. One payment in July 2014 totaled \$20 million.

Most of the transfers occurred between 2013 and 2015, during which \$140 million was sent from Black’s accounts to Epstein’s Southern Trust Company accounts at Deutsche Bank.

### Total Amount Wired from Black’s Bank of America Accounts to Epstein

- 2012: \$5,500,000  

- 2013: \$50,000,000  

- 2014: \$70,000,000  

- 2015: \$30,000,000  

- 2016: \$6,300,000  

- 2017: \$8,000,000  

- **Total: \$169,800,000**

Despite the extraordinary size of these payments and Epstein’s lack of credentials in tax or estate planning, Bank of America did not conduct meaningful due diligence. The bank did not report the transactions to the Treasury Department until February 2020.

## Bank of America’s SAR Findings

In its February 7, 2020 SAR filing, Bank of America flagged \$156 million in payments from Black to Epstein and stated:

- “the wire transfer activity does not have a verifiable business purpose”  

- the transactions had “no apparent economic, business or lawful purpose”  

Eight months later, in October 2020, Bank of America filed a second SAR flagging two additional wires it had previously missed:

- \$5.5 million (November 2012)  

- \$8 million (2017)

These findings demonstrate a significant compliance failure. A major bank allowed \$170 million in payments from a billionaire to a registered sex offender without verifying the purpose of the transactions.

## Why Bank of America Looked the Other Way

Bank of America appears not to have asked Black or Epstein for business records to substantiate the claimed tax and estate planning services. Epstein was not a credentialed tax advisor, and proper due diligence likely would have revealed that the payments lacked a legitimate business purpose.

Black’s accounts were supervised by Jane Heller, identified in DOJ records as one of Black’s principal bankers. Internal emails show:

- “Jane Heller is calling every day for this statement”  

- Bank of America had a \$400 million art‑secured credit line with Black  

- Heller and Weiss were familiar with Black’s financial activities and Epstein’s involvement  

Bank employees managing ultra‑wealthy clients develop deep knowledge of their clients’ finances. Heller and Weiss likely understood that \$170 million in tax advisory payments over five years was not normal.

Bank of America had strong financial incentives to retain Black as a client. Billionaires generate millions in fees across:

- private wealth management  

- brokerage  

- credit lines secured by art, yachts, jets, and real estate  

Examples include:

- four loans worth more than \$500 million related to Black’s art collection, yacht, and private jet  

- a \$484 million loan against Black’s art collection at 1.43 percent interest  

- an \$18 million loan collateralized by Black’s yacht, arranged with Epstein’s involvement  

With Black’s art collection valued at more than \$2.6 billion and his Apollo shares worth at least \$4 billion, Bank of America had substantial financial motivation to avoid scrutinizing his payments to Epstein.

## Required Federal Investigations

The Treasury Department and Federal Reserve must investigate Bank of America’s handling of Black’s payments to Epstein. These investigations should determine:

- whether bank systems flagged the payments  

- whether compliance officials escalated concerns  

- whether proper due diligence was conducted  

- whether Bank of America used Patriot Act Section 314(b) to request information from other banks  

The evidence suggests Bank of America failed to comply with federal anti‑money‑laundering laws and protected its lucrative relationship with Leon Black at the expense of legal obligations and public safety.
