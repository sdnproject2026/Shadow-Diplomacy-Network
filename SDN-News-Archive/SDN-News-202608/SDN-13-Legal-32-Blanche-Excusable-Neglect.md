# AG Todd Blanche

## Excusable Neglect

### 2026-08

The "excusable neglect" argument put forward by US Attorney General Todd Blanche has come under severe scrutiny in a lawsuit regarding the Department of Justice's handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files.

### Katie Phang

Independent journalist Katie Phang filed a legal brief demonstrating that the Trump administration’s DOJ made a calculated, deliberate choice to miss a major response deadline, rather than an _accidental_ or _inadvertent_ error.

## Case Background

### The Lawsuit

- Phang sued Blanche over the DOJ's handling of public records mandated by the Epstein Files Transparency Act.

### The Injunction

- US District Judge Emmet Sullivan granted a preliminary injunction on June 25, 2026, ordering the DOJ to release specific information and justify extensive redactions.

### The Missed Deadline

- Blanche's legal team missed a subsequent, critical court deadline.

### Excusable Neglect

- They requested that the court forgive the delay under the legal principle of "excusable neglect," calling it an "inadvertent oversight".

## That Argument Is Crumbling

According to recent legal filings and court arguments, the DOJ's excuse is failing for three primary reasons:

### Deliberate Delay, Not an Accident

- Opposing counsel argues that the DOJ’s own filings reveal the delay was tactical. Instead of an oversight, Blanche's team allegedly made a conscious choice to withhold a response until they deemed the preliminary injunction proceedings concluded.

### Strict Legal Precedent

- Under binding Supreme Court and DC Circuit precedent, simply "overlooking" or accidentally missing a deadline does not legally meet the high bar required for "excusable neglect".

### Sworn Evidence Missing

- To successfully claim excusable neglect, a party must typically submit an affidavit - a detailed, sworn document signed under penalty of perjury explaining the exact cause of the failure. 

- Blanche's team failed to provide one.

## Broader Political Stakes

### August 7, 2026

### Quarterbacking A Cover-up

Lawmakers and survivors have accused the DOJ of quarterbacking a cover-up, highlighting botches that accidentally exposed victims' private details while simultaneously shielding powerful public figures from scrutiny. 

### August 13, 2026

A federal court hearing focused on these compliance failures was scheduled for August 13, 2026.
