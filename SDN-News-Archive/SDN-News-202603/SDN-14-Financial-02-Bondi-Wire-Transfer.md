# $847,000 Wire Transfer

## Refusal To Unredact 47 Names

53 pages of FBI interview docs "disappeared"

### Congressional Oversight

The March 2026 congressional oversight hearing regarding the Department of Justice’s compliance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act (EFTA) was defined by a significant shift in the proceedings following the disclosure of a specific financial record.

Attorney General Pam Bondi’s testimony moved from a discussion of general administrative protocols to a focused inquiry into the Department's financial and investigative integrity.

## Who

### Originator - Leon Black

- Lawmakers and forensic investigators identified Leon Black, a billionaire financier and major political donor, as the individual linked to the entity that initiated the transfer.

### Recipient - DOJ

- The funds were routed to a Department of Justice-linked account or a specific non-public settlement ledger shortly after the Attorney General’s confirmation.

### Witness

- Attorney General Pam Bondi, who stated during the hearing that she had no personal knowledge of the specific transaction.

### Interrogator

- Representative Thomas Massie, who presented the bank records as evidence of potential conflict of interest regarding document redactions.

## What

The transfer was an $847,000 wire identified in banking documents.

### "lose" Interview Summaries

Lawmakers alleged this transaction coincided with DOJ deliberations to maintain redactions on high-profile individuals and "lose" specific FBI interview summaries.

## Where

The transfer reportedly moved through international offshore accounts before reaching its US  destination.

## When

### Transaction Date

- The transfer was executed in February 2025, within the first 72 hours of Bondi's tenure as Attorney General.

### Disclosure Date

- 2026 - 02 - 11, the date of the primary oversight hearing.

## How

The transfer was identified by a major US  financial institution.

### JP Morgan Chase

- As the largest US bank with the most extensive international wire infrastructure, it is the primary institution typically involved in flagging high-value transfers that move through "backdoor" ledgers or offshore accounts (Cayman Islands/BVI) to the US.

### Citibank (Citigroup)

- Given its global footprint and specific focus on international "correspondent banking," it is frequently the institution that identifies wires moving from offshore jurisdictions into the US domestic system.

### HSBC Bank USA

- Due to its history with international regulatory oversight and stringent monitoring of offshore-to-US flows, it is a common source of the type of banking documents presented as evidence in congressional oversight.

### Backdoor Ledger

- Lawmakers alleged the $847,000 was authorized via a "backdoor" ledger typically reserved for non-public fees, bypassing standard ethics reviews for funds incoming to DOJ, during a leadership transition.

## Why

Lawmakers argued the transfer was intended to influence the DOJ's implementation of the EFTA.

### Refusal To Unredact 47 Names

Specifically regarding the refusal to unredact 47 specific names and the reported disappearance of 53 pages of FBI interview summaries containing allegations against prominent figures.

## Status

### Decision Reversal

As of March 2026, the decision to redact the 47 individuals has not been fully reversed.

Bondi maintained that most redactions were necessary to protect investigative "privilege" or "ongoing investigations."

The "lost" 53 pages of FBI summaries have not been restored to the public database.

- the DOJ claims they were misfiled.

## Senate Access

Following the hearings, the DOJ allowed a bipartisan group of Senators, including Ben Ray Luján and John Thune, to view unredacted versions in a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF).

Senator Luján subsequently stated that the unredacted files contained "disturbing acts" not reflected in the public versions, leading to calls for an independent audit.

### Claims Regarding Active Investigations

- Bondi has justified withholding roughly 3 million pages by claiming they are protected by privileges that go beyond the victim privacy protections outlined in the EFTA.

### The "Trump Accusation" Files

- The DOJ is withholding reports involving a survivor who made specific allegations against Donald Trump, claiming they relate to an "ongoing federal investigation."

### Co-Conspirator Probes

- The DOJ asserts that thousands of redactions protect the identities of individuals currently being vetted for potential indictment to avoid tipping off "subjects of interest."

### Deliberative Process Privilege

- Used to shield internal memos discussing why certain individuals were not prosecuted in 2008 and 2019.
