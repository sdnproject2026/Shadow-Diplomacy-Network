# Todd Blanche’s language

An analysis of Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche’s language reveals a strategy of semantic narrowness and compartmentalization. 

By isolating the "Epstein Files" from the broader mission of the DOJ, Blanche creates a rhetorical shield that allows him to defend the department's current activity while avoiding accountability for the lack of new prosecutions.

### The "Provable Evidence" Pivot

- When asked why the UK can prosecute associates while the US has "not one prosecution," Blanche shifts the burden of proof to the public. By stating, 

> I can do as the deputy general is invite anybody with evidence to come to the FBI

- he implies that the failure to prosecute is a result of a lack of external "tips," effectively evading the question of why existing internal files have not yielded charges.

### The "Ice Cream" Ambiguity

- Blanche’s response to the Pizzagate theory -

> I’m not saying ice cream is never a coded word for something else

- is a classic example of hedging. While he eventually dismisses the conspiracy, this specific phrase creates a double interpretation

- it allows him to appear open to "hidden truths" to appease a skeptical audience while technically committing to nothing.

### Definitions of "Sexual References"

- When asked if files refer to "something sexual related to underage girls," he shifts to "underage women." This linguistic adjustment may be a tactical attempt to distance the DOJ's current findings from the most legally sensitive and emotionally charged category (minors/children), even though the two often overlap in the context of the Epstein case.

### Defensive Affirmation

- By repeatedly stating, "I’m not trying to defend Epstein," Blanche uses prolepsis - anticipating an objection to discredit it before it is made. 

- This allows him to frame any criticism of the DOJ's "handling" of the case as an unfair accusation that he is "defending" a sex offender.

### "Public" and "Provided Evidence"

- Blanche’s invitation for the "public" to "come to the FBI and tell us" creates a false narrative of an evidentiary vacuum. 

- This rhetoric acts as a redirection of agency, suggesting that the DOJ is a passive recipient of information rather than an active investigative body.

### The Victim Form Paradox

- The "Epstein Files" released in early 2026 include thousands of FBI complaint forms and 302 witness interview summaries dating back years. 

- By asking the public to provide what is already in the DOJ’s possession, Blanche treats existing victim statements as non-existent or "stale," despite them being the very "public-provided evidence" he claims to be seeking.

### The "Screaming from the Rooftops" Hyperbole

- This phrase is used to signal transparency and desperation for justice. 

- However, when contrasted with the discovery of buried complaint forms, it functions as performative outreach - projecting an image of an aggressive investigation to mask the fact that prior "public" leads were never pursued to indictment.

### Provable vs. "Buried" Evidence

- The distinction Blanche draws between "provable evidence" and "evidence" is the core of the DOJ’s evasive maneuvers.

### The Internal 6 Million Pages

- Blanche frames the 6 million pages as a haystack in which no "provable" needle exists. 

- In legal terms, "provable" typically means evidence that can withstand cross-examination and meet the burden of "beyond a reasonable doubt." 

- By labeling internal files as "not provable," he avoids admitting to investigative negligence.

### The Investigative Gap

- There is a sharp linguistic distinction between "the evidence is not provable" and "the FBI never investigated the evidence." 

- The former implies a legal dead end; the latter implies a cover-up or systemic failure. Blanche uses the former to bury the latter.

### Amnesia via Volume

- By emphasizing the sheer volume of data (6 million pages), the DOJ uses information flooding to suggest that if no one is being prosecuted, it must be because the evidence is insufficient, rather than the possibility that the evidence - specifically the complaint forms - was never properly corroborated or was intentionally sidelined.

### Domestic Recourse via Florida RICO

### Legal Bars and the State Interest

- Because the victim in a RICO case is the State of Florida (or the nation France) and the offense includes the specific element of an organized enterprise, a previous foreign conviction does not act as a legal bar to state-level (nation-level) prosecution. 

- This provides a potential pathway for accountability that bypasses federal inaction.

## Legal Privileges vs. EFTA

The conflict between the DOJ and Congress centers on the use of broad legal privileges to withhold approximately 2.5 million pages. 

The following describes how these citations align or conflict with the Epstein Files Transparency Act (EFTA).

### Deliberative Process Privilege

- Used to redact internal DOJ/FBI discussions and memoranda. 

- While the DOJ argues these are "pre-decisional," critics argue this is used as an "embarrassment loophole" to hide politically sensitive discussions, which the EFTA explicitly prohibits.

### Work-Product Doctrine

- Applied to documents prepared by attorneys in anticipation of litigation. 

- This is often used to shield trial strategies from 2008 and 2019 that might reveal why certain plea deals were struck.

### Attorney-Client Privilege

- Cited to protect communications between DOJ officials and government legal counsel.

### Grand Jury Secrecy

(Rule 6(e))

- A statutory requirement used to redact testimony. 

- Lawmakers argue the DOJ is over-applying this to include general evidence that was merely shown to a grand jury, rather than testimony itself.

### Active Investigation Exception

- Blanche cites this to protect "ongoing" matters, creating a linguistic contradiction where he simultaneously claims there is "no new evidence" for prosecutions.

### Victim Privacy vs. Over-Redaction

- While the EFTA allows withholding for victim PII or medical records, the DOJ is accused of using this to redact the names of powerful associates and "women in images" who may not technically qualify as victims under the Act's protections.

### The Scoping Evasion

- The DOJ primary reviewed "case files" (3.5 million pages). 

- The remaining 2.5 million pages were labeled "non-responsive" or "duplicates." Advocacy groups argue this violates the EFTA's mandate to release all records "relating to" Epstein, not just those the DOJ self-selected.
