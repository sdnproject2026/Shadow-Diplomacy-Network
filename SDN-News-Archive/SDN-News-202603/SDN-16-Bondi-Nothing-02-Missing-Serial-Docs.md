# Grounds for a Special Master

The Department of Justice's (DOJ) admission on 2026-03-05 that 16 pages were "incorrectly coded as duplicative" has provided the primary factual basis for renewed legal efforts to appoint a Special Master. ## Procedural Arguments

### Failure of the Presumption of Regularity

- In federal law, courts typically grant agencies a "presumption of regularity" in how they process documents. However, the discovery that the DOJ "missed" high-profile allegations against the President by labeling them as duplicates—only to release them 24 hours after a subpoena vote—is being cited as evidence of bad faith.

- Lawmakers like Ro Khanna and Thomas Massie argue that this admission proves the DOJ's internal reviewers cannot be trusted to distinguish between unique evidence and duplicate files, meeting the "exceptional circumstances" threshold required under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 53 for a Special Master.

### Violation of the Epstein Files Transparency Act (EFTA)

- Section 3 of the EFTA requires a "comprehensive and authoritative accounting" of all withheld records.

- Legal experts suggest that the "duplicative" error indicates the DOJ's mandated reports to Congress were factually inaccurate. This "material misrepresentation" to the Legislative Branch provides a unique jurisdictional hook for a court to appoint an independent monitor to verify the remaining 3.5 million pages.

### The "37-Page Discrepancy" as Judicial Map

- Because investigative journalists successfully predicted the existence of the 16 "missing" pages using serial number gaps, the remaining 37-page gap is now viewed by legal scholars as "known evidence" rather than speculative theory.

- The presence of a "roadmap" to specific missing records makes it easier for a judge to justify a Special Master, as the scope of the independent review can be narrowly tailored to investigating these specific numerical discrepancies.

# Document Tracking

The investigation into missing records within the Epstein files centers on the conflict between two primary tracking mechanisms: the FBI internal serialization system and the external DOJ library stamps applied for public release.

## System Definitions and Logic

- FBI Sequential Tracking (Serials)

### System

- Central Records System (CRS).

### Format

- Documents are assigned a unique "serial number" based on the order they are entered into a case file (e.g., 18-20-6).

### Internal Logic

- Serials are added chronologically. If a case file contains Serial 1 and Serial 3, but not Serial 2, it is an objective indicator that a record has been removed or withheld from the internal investigative file.

- DOJ Library Stamps (Bates Numbering)

### System

- Epstein Files Transparency Act (EFTA) Database.

### Format

- Sequential alphanumeric stamps (e.g., EFTA-00001) applied during the document production phase.

### Internal Logic

- These stamps are applied to the "output" rather than the "source." They track what has been handed over to the public, not the total volume of evidence held by the agency.

## Discrepancy Analysis

### The "Middle Stamp" Gap

- In recent Epstein file releases, a third set of stamps (internal tracking from the Ghislaine Maxwell trial) was identified in the "middle" of the page footers.

- Investigative reporting noted that while the public DOJ EFTA stamps increased by 1 (indicating a single next page), the internal "middle" stamps jumped by over 50.

- This discrepancy mathematically proves that dozens of pages of investigative material exist between the two released pages but were excluded from the public set.

### Non-Testifying Witness Logs

- Discrepancies often appear in the serial ranges reserved for "enclosures behind the file" (EBFs).

- When FBI serials skip numbers in sequences related to witness interviews, it suggests the removal of 302 forms (interview summaries) or secondary evidence like digital media logs that the DOJ may categorize as "ongoing" investigative material to avoid public disclosure.
