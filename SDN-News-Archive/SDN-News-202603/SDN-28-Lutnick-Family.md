# Lutnick Family


## 1920s

- Lutnick family ancestors establish early roots in the United States, part of broader Eastern European Jewish immigration waves to New York amid post World War I economic shifts.  

  - Grandparents and great grandparents navigate urban tenements and early labor markets, laying groundwork for later generations' emphasis on education and professional advancement in academia and business.  


## 1930s

- Lutnick extended family endures the Great Depression, with Solomon Lutnick's parents likely facing job scarcity in New York while prioritizing children's schooling despite widespread hardship.  

  - This era instills intergenerational resilience, influencing Solomon's later academic career and the family's focus on self reliance during Howard's upbringing.  


## 1940s

- Solomon Lutnick, Howard's father, born into wartime America, experiences World War II rationing and the Holocaust's distant shadows on Jewish communities while growing up in New York.  

  - Postwar optimism shapes Solomon's path toward higher education, setting a model of intellectual pursuit that Howard internalizes amid family expectations.  


## Mid 1950s

- Solomon Lutnick advances academically, earning degrees and beginning his history professorship at Queens College as the postwar boom expands public universities.  

  - Jane Lutnick, Howard's mother, develops her artistic talents as a sculptor and art teacher at Long Island University, creating a culturally enriched household environment.  


## 1961-07-14

- Birth of Howard William Lutnick in Jericho, New York, to Solomon and Jane Lutnick in a middle class Jewish family.  

  - Raised alongside siblings Edie, Gary, and Brandon, with early exposure to parental careers in academia and arts fostering discipline and ambition.  


## Mid 1970s

- Howard attends Jericho High School, excelling in academics and sports amid suburban Long Island life during economic stagflation.  

  - Family dynamics tighten after personal losses, with Howard, Edie, and Gary forming a close trio as extended relatives distance themselves.  


## 1978

- Enrolls at Haverford College in Pennsylvania, studying economics at the elite liberal arts institution.  

  - Graduates in 1983 with a B.A., immediately entering Wall Street by joining Cantor Fitzgerald as a trainee under founder B. Gerald Cantor.  

## Late 1980s

- Rises to managing director at age 27, mastering bond trading during the junk bond era and deregulation wave.  


## 1991

- Becomes president and CEO of Cantor Fitzgerald at 29 after power struggles post founder health issues.  

  - Navigates legal battles with Iris Cantor, securing control while expanding electronic trading platforms.  

## 1996

- Assumes chairmanship following executive changes, driving growth amid dot com buildup.  


## 2001-09-11

- Survives 9/11 by taking son to school; loses brother Gary and 658 Cantor employees in World Trade Center attacks.  

  - Rebuilds firm, launches Cantor Relief Fund raising over 180 million dollars for families.  

## Mid 2000s

- Spinoffs BGC Partners and Newmark Group diversify into brokerage and real estate services.  


## 2010s

- Pioneers cryptocurrency trading at Cantor, testifies on blockchain before Congress.  

  - Shifts political donations toward Republicans, including early Trump support.  


## 2024

- Co chairs Trump transition, raises millions for campaign as tariff advocate.  

  - Nominated Commerce Secretary November 2024; confirmed February 2025.  

## 2025-2026

- Oversees tariffs on China, Mexico, sparking trade wars, market volatility, and congressional probes.

# Business Affiliations

## Cantor Fitzgerald, L.P.

- Core financial services firm founded 1945, specializing in investment banking, fixed income, equities, and prime brokerage; Howard Lutnick served as chairman and CEO until 2025 divestiture for Commerce Secretary role; maintains controlling stakes in affiliates BGC Group and Newmark Group.  

## BGC Group, Inc.

(formerly BGC Partners)

- Global brokerage platform for wholesale financial and real estate markets, spun off from Cantor Fitzgerald; provides inter dealer brokerage, trade execution, clearing, and post trade services; publicly traded, with Cantor holding majority control post Lutnick transactions.  

## Newmark Group, Inc.

(formerly Newmark Grubb Knight Frank)

- Commercial real estate services firm offering leasing, investment sales, debt placement, valuation, and advisory; third largest U.S. investment broker by volume; acquired via BGC in 2011 from Grubb & Ellis assets; publicly traded with Cantor controlling interest.  

## eSpeed

- Electronic trading platform for U.S. Treasuries and fixed income launched by Cantor in late 1990s; pioneered fintech disruption in bond markets under Lutnick leadership.  

## Cantor Fitzgerald Relief Fund

- Philanthropic entity established post 9/11 by Howard Lutnick, raising over 180 million dollars for victims' families; tied to Cantor corporate social responsibility.  

## CCRE

(Cantor Commercial Real Estate)

- Cantor division handling commercial real estate finance, launched mid 2000s as part of diversification strategy.  

## 26North

- Alternative asset management firm led by Josh Harris, minority investor in Cantor Fitzgerald ownership transition announced May 2025.  

## Oak Hill Advisors

(via Glenn August)

- Private investment firm participating as minority stakeholder in Cantor Fitzgerald alongside 26North during Lutnick family ownership shift.  

## Berkeley Point Financial

- Multi family lending provider acquired by BGC from Cantor in 2017 for 875 million dollars, expanding Newmark's capital markets services.  

## Regency Capital Partners

- Real estate capital advisory firm acquired by Newmark in 2017, specializing in debt, equity, and joint ventures for office and multifamily projects.  

## Frederick Ross

- Denver based real estate brokerage and advisory firm acquired by BGC to bolster Newmark's regional presence.  

## Smith Mack

- Philadelphia based real estate services firm acquired by BGC, enhancing Newmark operations in Pennsylvania, Delaware, and New Jersey.

# Kushner Family Business Ties

## Mid 2010s

- Newmark Grubb Knight Frank (later Newmark Group) emerges as brokerage provider for Kushner Companies Manhattan office leasing, capitalizing on shared New York commercial real estate market dominance.

- Newmark brokers multiple Kushner property transactions, positioning as key advisor amid Kushner Companies expansion under Charles Kushner leadership.

## 2010s

- Indirect professional overlap intensifies as Newmark handles leasing for 666 Fifth Avenue vicinity properties while Kushner Companies restructures debt on the flagship tower.

- Newmark's commercial leasing expertise aligns with Kushner needs during post financial crisis recovery, fostering repeated engagements without formal equity partnerships.

## Early 2020s

- Newmark Group continues servicing Kushner Companies portfolios, including advisory on multifamily and office repositioning amid remote work shifts.

- Brokerage deals solidify Newmark as preferred partner for Kushner asset management, leveraging Howard Lutnick's oversight of Cantor affiliates.

## 2024

- Howard Lutnick co-chairs Trump transition team alongside Linda McMahon, consulting informally with Jared Kushner on staffing amid shared political networks.

- Transition coordination highlights longstanding New York real estate adjacency between Lutnick firms and Kushner Companies principals.

## 2025

- Cantor Fitzgerald records peak profitability under Lutnick sons' interim leadership as Howard assumes Commerce Secretary role, coinciding with Kushner affiliated deals in opportunity zones.

- No direct Kushner Companies involvement documented, though market speculation links broader real estate upswing to Trump administration policy tailwinds benefiting both families' portfolios.
