# The Zelnickova Family

(Trump)

## 1920s

### 1926

- Birth of Marie Francova (later Marie Zelníčková), Ivana’s mother, in Czechoslovakia.

### 1927

- Birth of Miloš Zelníček, Ivana’s father, in Czechoslovakia.

## 1940s

### 1949-02-20

- Birth of Ivana Marie Zelníčková in Gottwaldov, Czechoslovak Republic, later renamed Zlín in the Czech Republic.

### Late 1940s

- Ivana is raised in a Catholic household by her parents Miloš, an electrical engineer, and Marie, a telephone operator.

## 1950s

### 1950s

- Ivana begins skiing at about age four, encouraged and coached by her father Miloš.

### 1950s

- The Zelníček family lives under the communist regime in Czechoslovakia, with sharply limited opportunities to travel to the West.

## 1960s

### 1960s

- Ivana develops into a competitive skier and joins the junior national ski team of Czechoslovakia, which grants her rare permission to travel abroad.

### 1960s

- Ivana appears on Czechoslovak television and is linked to roles that culminate in an appearance around 1970 in the children’s series Pan Tau, reflecting early media exposure.

## 1970s

### 1970

- Ivana appears in the children’s television series Pan Tau on Czechoslovak Television.

### 1972

- Ivana earns a master’s degree in physical education from Charles University in Prague.

### Early 1970s

- Ivana marries Austrian skier Alfred Winklmayr, a marriage widely described as one of convenience to obtain a Western passport and facilitate her exit from communist Czechoslovakia.

### Early to mid 1970s

- After obtaining an Austrian passport, she relocates to Canada, living in Montreal, working as a ski instructor, and then moving into fashion modeling.

### 1977

- Ivana marries American real estate developer Donald Trump in New York City, entering the Trump family and American high society.

### Late 1970s

- Ivana becomes actively involved in The Trump Organization, working alongside Donald on real estate and hospitality ventures.

## 1980s

### 1981

- Birth of Ivanka Marie Trump, Ivana and Donald Trump’s first daughter, in New York.

### 1983

- Birth of Eric Trump, Ivana and Donald’s younger son.

### 1984

- Birth of Tiffany Trump to Donald Trump and Marla Maples, who becomes the half sister of Ivana’s three children though not related to Ivana by blood.

### 1980s

- Ivana serves as vice president of interior design at The Trump Organization, taking charge of interior design for major projects including Trump Tower.

### 1980s

- Ivana serves as CEO and president of Trump’s Castle casino resort in Atlantic City, becoming one of the most visible female executives in the casino and hotel industry.

### Late 1980s

- Ivana is installed as president of the Plaza Hotel in New York after Donald Trump acquires the property, overseeing renovations and daily operations.

### Late 1980s

- The Trump family, including Ivana and their children Donald Jr., Ivanka, and Eric, become tabloid and society page fixtures due to their wealth, properties, and social life.

## 1990s

### 1990-10

- Death of Miloš Zelníček, Ivana’s father, from a heart attack at age 63, with Donald Trump reported to have attended the funeral in Zlín.

### 1990

- Post communist era access to Czechoslovak secret police records reveals that Miloš Zelníček had cooperated with the Státní bezpečnost, or StB, as an informer.

### 1990

- Reports indicate that Miloš supplied the StB with information derived from Ivana’s conversations about Western society and politics, including a reported accurate prediction that George H. W. Bush would win the 1988 United States presidential election.

### 1990s

- Journalistic and archival investigations raise suspicions that Ivana’s family, particularly through Miloš, had ties to Czechoslovak intelligence; Ivana herself is portrayed primarily as an unwitting information source rather than a trained operative.

### 1991

- Ivana and Donald Trump finalize their highly publicized divorce, and Ivana receives a substantial financial settlement that establishes her independent wealth.

### 1990s

- Ivana launches her own branded businesses, including fashion, jewelry, and beauty lines, as well as home products, capitalizing on her name recognition.

### 1990s

- Ivana writes or coauthors several books, including self help and lifestyle works and a novel, drawing on her experiences as a businesswoman and social figure.

## 2000s

### 2000

- Ivana continues to grow her lifestyle and fashion ventures, making appearances on television shopping channels and licensing her name internationally.

### 2000

- Death of Mary Anne MacLeod Trump, Donald Trump’s mother, marking the loss of a central Trump family matriarch to whom Ivana’s children were closely connected through their father.

### 2000s

- Ivana’s personal life includes further marriages and divorces, notably to Italian businessman Riccardo Mazzucchelli and later to Italian actor and model Rossano Rubicondi, keeping her in the public eye as a socialite.

### 2000s

- Ivana remains active in New York and European society circles while her children increasingly assume executive roles in The Trump Organization, bridging her Central European origins and the family’s expanding American prominence.

## Other Events

- Mid twentieth century: Members of the extended Zelníček and Francova families largely remain in Czechoslovakia, with limited public detail about cousins or more distant relatives beyond their connection to Miloš and Ivana.

- Cold War period: The StB and other Eastern Bloc intelligence agencies routinely targeted citizens who could travel abroad, including athletes like Ivana, making families such as the Zelníčeks potential intelligence assets through travel based access to Western contacts.

- 1980s - 1990s: Ivana’s children Donald Trump Jr., Ivanka Trump, and Eric Trump attend elite schools in the United States, later joining The Trump Organization and extending the Trump family’s blend of business, media, and eventually political influence into a new generation.

# Czechoslovak Leadership

## Klement Gottwald

- Senior communist leader whose position as party chairman and later prime minister after the 1948 coup placed him at the top of the political hierarchy that defined the powers and tasks of the StB toward internal class enemies and Western states. His rule created the repressive environment into which Ivana’s parents, Miloš and Marie, were born and in which their early adult lives and family formation took place.

## Antonín Novotný

- Party first secretary and later president who oversaw a rigid Stalinist system in the 1950s and early 1960s, relying heavily on the StB for surveillance and ideological control. Under his leadership, families like the Zelníčeks lived with restricted travel, rationed consumer goods, and frequent monitoring of any contact with the West that might later become relevant to Miloš’s dealings with security services.

## Alexander Dubček

- Reformist first secretary whose Prague Spring reforms in 1968 briefly relaxed censorship, travel restrictions, and StB pressure, allowing a short period of hope for professional families such as Ivana’s that life might liberalize. The subsequent Soviet led invasion and crackdown reversed many of these gains, reinforcing for Ivana’s generation the risks and unpredictability of political change.

## Gustáv Husák

- General secretary and later president who led the normalization period after 1968, restoring strict party discipline and expanding the StB’s role in monitoring dissent and foreign ties. During Ivana’s adolescence and early adulthood, Husák’s leadership meant that athletes traveling abroad and their relatives, including families like the Zelníčeks, were carefully watched and often drawn into intelligence reporting networks.

## Miloš Jakeš

- Late communist era party chief whose tenure in the 1980s maintained a stagnant but still repressive system heavily dependent on the StB’s reports about contacts with Westerners. As Ivana rose to international prominence through marriage and business, Jakeš’s leadership presided over the apparatus that evaluated information about high profile émigrés and their relatives remaining in Czechoslovakia, including her father Miloš.

## Vasil Biľak

- Hard line party ideologue closely associated with the decision to invite Warsaw Pact intervention in 1968, reinforcing the dominance of pro Moscow security thinking. His influence sustained a doctrine that treated émigré communities and successful Western based Czechs, like Ivana and others from similar backgrounds, as potential threats or tools to be exploited through relatives at home.

## Oldřich Černík

- Prime minister during the Prague Spring whose attempt to balance reform with party control occurred while security structures remained central to governance. For families such as the Zelníčeks, his tenure marked a fleeting moment when outward openness seemed possible, even as the underlying system that would later monitor Ivana’s travels and calls stayed intact.

## Jozef Lenárt

- High ranking official and prime minister who helped coordinate party decisions throughout the state bureaucracy, including the ministries that oversaw travel and foreign trade. Under leaders like Lenárt, procedures were established that made international movement by athletes, professionals, and their kin contingent on political reliability, something directly relevant to Ivana’s ability to travel as a skier.

## Antonín Zápotocký

- President in the early 1950s whose period followed mass purges and show trials that demonstrated the lethal potential of the security apparatus. His era shaped the formative climate of fear and caution in which Ivana’s parents learned to navigate state expectations, a mentality that later informed how Miloš handled approaches from intelligence officers.

## Central Committee members

- The collective leadership core that approved strategic lines for domestic control and foreign policy, including the classification of Western powers, émigrés, and travelers as priority intelligence targets. Their decisions created the structural conditions that framed Ivana’s skiing trips abroad and her later international marriage as matters of interest to security agencies, thereby affecting how her father was treated and used as an informant.

# Elites And Athletes

## National team athletes

- High level competitors whose participation in international events gave them rare legal access to Western countries, hotels, and social gatherings. Ivana’s own path as a competitive skier on the junior national team placed her in this category, making her travels, conversations, and observations a valuable source of information that her father could later pass on to the StB.

## Winter Sports Competitors

- Specialists in alpine, Nordic, and related disciplines who frequently attended training camps and races abroad, often under close political vetting. Ivana’s skiing talent, encouraged by her parents from childhood, brought her into this milieu and indirectly linked her family to the security state through debriefings, monitored contacts, and interest in her foreign experiences.

## World Championship Delegations

- Mixed groups of athletes, coaches, doctors, and officials who traveled together under strict supervision and often included designated informants. Although Ivana’s primary fame came later, the model applied to her generation meant that any Czechoslovak athlete with foreign exposure, including those from regions like Zlín, could become a node through which relatives such as Miloš were asked to report.

## Trade officials

- Representatives of state enterprises and foreign trade ministries who negotiated contracts, observed Western technologies, and built person to person networks overseas. For families like Ivana’s, connections to such officials, whether through local contacts or relatives, could open opportunities but also attract attention from the StB, which sought to integrate economic and political intelligence.

## Scientific and technical specialists

- Engineers and researchers dispatched to fairs, conferences, or training programs in the West and expected to share detailed reports upon return. Ivana’s father, as an electrical engineer, belonged to a professional category that the state regarded as both economically important and politically sensitive, increasing the likelihood that his foreign related knowledge, including about his daughter abroad, would be tapped by intelligence officers.

## Cultural delegations

- Writers, musicians, actors, and artists whose tours projected an official image of socialist culture while quietly gathering impressions of émigré communities and Western audiences. While not directly in Ivana’s immediate family, such delegations sometimes overlapped socially with athletic circles and returning émigrés, widening the net of people whose contacts and stories could feed into files on prominent expatriates, including Ivana.

## Civil Transport Personnel

- Airline crews, ship staff, and long distance railway workers whose frequent travel made them repeat sources of informal observations and gossip about life in the West. In regions like Moravia, where Zlín is located, acquaintances or kin in transport jobs could transmit information about émigré successes abroad, forming part of the background knowledge that made Ivana’s later marriage and lifestyle especially interesting to officials speaking with Miloš.

## Diplomatic staff

- Embassy personnel and consular workers whose duties combined formal representation with unofficial intelligence gathering and assessment of expatriate communities. They paid attention to high profile Czechoslovak born figures overseas, and their reports, cross checked with informants such as relatives back home, contributed to the long term StB interest in Ivana’s movements and her marriage to a rising American businessman.

## Student exchange participants

- Carefully selected students and trainees sent to Western universities or firms, often from technically skilled or politically trusted families. Though Ivana herself left through marriage rather than a state program, her university education at Charles University and her later life abroad echoed patterns seen among this group, encouraging security services to treat her as someone whose views and forecasts, relayed through Miloš, might hold analytical value.

## Tour organizers and guides

- Individuals responsible for managing group travel whose role in shaping itineraries and mediating contact with foreigners made them convenient observers and recorders of behavior. For Ivana’s ancestral region, local party or travel functionaries could combine gossip about families with foreign connected daughters or sons, like the Zelníčeks, with formal reporting, reinforcing the state’s picture of émigré success stories and potential political ambitions.
