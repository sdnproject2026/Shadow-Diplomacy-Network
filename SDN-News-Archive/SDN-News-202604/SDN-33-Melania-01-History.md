# New York Modeling Agencies

How New York Modeling Agencies Structured Relationships With Independent Scouts 

(Mid-1990s)

## Core Structure of the System

### Agency-Controlled Gatekeeping Model

- Major agencies like Elite, Next, and IMG operated as centralized gatekeepers.

- Scouts did not typically sign models directly.

- Scouts funneled talent into agencies in exchange for referral fees or commission splits.

### Paolo Zampolli Style Scouts

- Independent scouts operated as freelance talent finders.

- Worked on commission-based referral systems, often receiving:

- Percentage of a model’s first contract

- Flat fees per signed model

- Ongoing residual commissions in some cases

### International Recruitment Pipeline

- Scouts identified talent in Europe, Eastern Europe, and Latin America.

- Agencies provided:

- Visa sponsorship (often via modeling contracts)

- Travel coordination to New York

- Housing arrangements or shared model apartments

### Agency Intake Process

- Step 1: Scout identifies model internationally or locally

- Step 2: Model is introduced to agency casting directors

- Step 3: Test shoots and portfolio creation in New York

- Step 4: Agency signs model under exclusive contract

- Step 5: Scout receives commission based on contract value or bookings

### Financial Incentive Layer

- Agencies benefited from low-cost acquisition of international talent.

- Scouts benefited from volume-based recruitment incentives.

- Models were often advanced expenses (flights, housing) recouped through future earnings.

### Informal Social Layer

Important Context

- The modeling industry in 1990s New York had a heavy informal component:

- Private dinners and social introductions

- Photographer-driven referrals

- Club and event-based scouting

- This informal layer is where many Epstein-era network overlaps are discussed in later investigations, though not always directly proven at agency level.

### Structural Weak Points

- Lack of centralized regulation of scouts

- Weak age verification enforcement in some international contexts

- Heavy reliance on personal trust networks

- Cross-border recruitment with minimal oversight at entry point
