# Bondi's "nothing left"

The origins of the **Epstein Files Transparency Act**, as recounted by Representative Thomas Massie, hinges on his interactions with Pam Bondi at a dinner event.

The narrative details a shift in rhetoric from Bondi concerning the existence of unreleased documents related to Jeffrey Epstein, which Massie cites as the catalyst for his legislative push.

## February 2025

Attorney General Pam Bondi claims in a media interview that the Epstein client list is "sitting on her desk right now" for review.

## May 2025

Representative Thomas Massie attends a "cordial dinner" where each attendee was granted one "question" to ask, he asked Pam Bondi about the status of the remaining Epstein files.

During this dinner, Pam Bondi claims there are no files left to release, contradicting her previous statements regarding the "tons of stuff" found on her desk.

## July 7, 2025

The Department of Justice (DOJ) and the FBI announce that no further files from the Epstein investigation will be released, with Bondi stating no incriminating "client list" was uncovered.

## July 15, 2025

Following the realization that files would not be released voluntarily, Massie and Representative Ro Khanna introduce the **Epstein Files Transparency Act** (H.R. 4405).

## November 19, 2025

The **Epstein Files Transparency Act** is signed into law, mandating the public disclosure of all investigative records related to Jeffrey Epstein within 30 days.

## February 11, 2026

Pam Bondi testifies before the House Judiciary Committee, where she and Massie engage in a heated exchange regarding the DOJ's failure to meet disclosure deadlines and the improper redaction of files.

# Sitting On My Desk

AG Pam Bondi first made her widely reported "sitting on my desk" comment during a televised interview in February 2025, shortly after taking office.

This statement was a primary catalyst for the legislative friction that followed between her and Representative Thomas Massie.

## February 21, 2025

- During an interview on Fox News, Bondi stated that the Jeffrey Epstein "client list" was "sitting on my desk right now to review," along with files related to JFK and Martin Luther King Jr.

- Bondi released "Phase 1" of the declassified Epstein files at a White House event.

- However, the release was criticized for containing mostly previously public information, leading Bondi to claim she had been "misled" by the FBI.

- Following the "Phase 1" release, Bondi sent a formal letter to FBI Director Kash Patel demanding a "truckload of evidence" and "tens of thousands of pages" that she claimed the FBI had previously withheld from her desk.

## May 7, 2025

- Bondi publicly claimed that the investigative materials included "tens of thousands of videos of Epstein with ch...en," a claim that was later walked-back by other administration officials.

## July 8, 2025

- During a Cabinet meeting, Bondi clarified her original "sitting on my desk" remark, stating she meant the "entire file" was on her desk for review, not necessarily a specific "client list," and confirmed the DOJ's finding that no such list existed.

## October 7, 2025

- During a Senate Judiciary Committee hearing, Bondi defended her earlier statements to Senator Dick Durbin, reiterating that her "desk" comment referred to a massive volume of unreviewed documents rather than a finalized list of names.

## February 11, 2026

- In a combative House Judiciary Committee hearing, Bondi clashed with Rep. Thomas Massie over the "nothing left" comment he alleged she made during their private dinner in May 2025.

- Massie argued her current redactions were a cover-up for the "tons of stuff" she originally promised was on her desk.
