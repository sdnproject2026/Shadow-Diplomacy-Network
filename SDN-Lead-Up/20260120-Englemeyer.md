Dear Judge Engelmayer:

We write on behalf of our clients Congressman Thomas Massie and Congressman Ro Khanna (the "Congressmen" or the "Amici") in further support of the Congressmen's request for leave from the Court to file an amici curiae brief as ordered by the Court in its January 13, 2026 Order. (Dkt. 833.)

We have urgent and grave concerns about DOJ’s failure to comply with the Act as well as the Department’s violations of this Court’s order.

On December 19, 2025, the Department of Justice released only a portion of responsive materials.

That release, however, did not comply with the statute as written.

Specifically, the Department failed to meet the Act’s requirements in multiple respects, including:

1. Missing the statutory deadline: The Act required full disclosure by December 19, 2025. 2. Asserting common-law privileges: The DOJ has invoked privileges that the Act does not permit for these specific records.

3. Extensive and improper redactions: The DOJ is applying redactions that appear inconsistent with the Act’s prohibition on withholding records to protect "politically exposed persons."

4. Failure to report: The Act requires a report to the House and Senate Judiciary Committees summarizing redactions; no such report has been provided.

Put simply, the DOJ cannot be trusted with making mandatory disclosures under the Act.

To date, the Department has publicly released only roughly 12,000 documents out of a total the DOJ now claims exceeds 2 million (and potentially 5 million according to recent internal estimates).

This represents less than 1% of the responsive material.

Furthermore, the DOJ has engaged in the "wholesale removal" of records after they were initially posted online, without explanation or court authorization.

This conduct is a flagrant violation of the mandatory disclosure obligations under the Epstein Files Transparency Act and has caused serious trauma to survivors who are seeking finality and justice.

Thus, in our capacity as amici curiae, we suggest that pursuant to its inherent authority and Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 53, this Court appoint a Special Master and/or Independent Monitor for the purpose of ensuring all the documents and electronically stored information are immediately made public in accordance with the Act.

We also suggest the Independent Monitor be given authority to notify and prepare reports to this Court about the true nature and extent of the document production and whether improper redactions are taking place.

Respectfully submitted,

# Update on the Court's Decision

On January 21, 2026, Judge Engelmayer denied the request to appoint a Special Master.

While he noted that the Congressmen raised "legitimate concerns," he ruled that a criminal case against Ghislaine Maxwell was not the correct legal venue to enforce a civil records disclosure statute (the EFTA). 

He suggested the lawmakers would need to file a separate civil lawsuit to compel the DOJ to act.

# Legal Arguments

The following summary details the legal arguments and events following Judge Engelmayer’s January 21, 2026 ruling. 

This strategy focuses on the "Gatekeeper" theory - that the Department of Justice (DOJ) already surrendered its jurisdictional objections through its own prior actions.

## Voluntary Submission of Authority

In December 2025, the DOJ filed a motion (Dkt. 810) requesting the Court’s permission to unseal files for the Epstein Files Transparency Act (EFTA).

## Concession of authority

The DOJ cannot treat the Judge as a master when seeking to unseal files, then treat him as a stranger when he is asked to supervise the quality of that release.

## "Gatekeeper" Argument

- The records in question are currently governed by a Protective Order issued by Judge Engelmayer in the Maxwell case.

- The Amici argue that only the Judge has the legal power to modify or enforce the terms of his own order.

- Because the DOJ is using this Order as the legal basis for its redactions, the lawmakers argue the Judge has a duty to oversee those redactions regardless of external "standing."

## Statutory Attachment & Mandatory Duty

- The EFTA specifically identifies the Maxwell case materials as responsive records.

- Lawmakers posit that the statute "annexed" itself to the criminal docket, creating a mandatory duty for the Court to ensure the law is followed, rendering the personal "standing" of the Congressmen secondary to the Court's internal administration.

## The Doctrine of Inherent Authority

The Congressmen assert that federal courts possess "inherent supervisory authority" to prevent a party from acting in bad faith.

They argue that because the DOJ "wholesale removed" documents from the public website after initially posting them, the Court must intervene to protect the integrity of the judicial process.

## January 22, 2026

### Survivor Joinder 

Attorneys for several Epstein survivors, including those for Maria Farmer and Haley Robson, filed letters supporting the Congressmen's position.

These filings argue that the DOJ is "paying lip service" to victims while failing to provide the specific transparency required by law.

### The "Double Standard" Filing 

Counsel for Khanna and Massie submitted a supplemental communication highlighting the irony of the DOJ’s position the Department uses the Court's authority to shield documents from the public but denies that same authority when asked to justify why 99% of the files remain hidden.

### Legislative Escalation

Congressman Khanna publicly threatened Attorney General Pam Bondi with "Inherent Contempt" of Congress.

This proposal includes a daily fine of $5,000 for every day the DOJ fails to provide the mandatory redaction report required by Section 2(a) of the Act.

### Parallel Civil Litigation

Taking the Judge’s "hint" from his ruling, the lawmakers announced they are preparing a separate civil lawsuit in the D.C. District Court.

This new suit aims to bypass the Maxwell criminal docket entirely and seek a direct injunction against the DOJ for statutory non-compliance.
