# "Rule of Seven" and "Rule of Five"

The "Rule of Seven" and "Rule of Five" refer to a rare legal maneuver based on a 1928 statute (5 USC § 2954) that allows a small group of lawmakers to bypass committee chairs and the House/Senate majority to legally compel the executive branch to produce information.

Lawmakers like Ro Khanna and Thomas Massie can utilize this specific statute to establish "standing" in a civil lawsuit against the Department of Justice.

Traditionally, individual members of Congress are barred from suing the executive branch because they lack "personal injury," but Section 2954 creates a unique statutory right for a minority group of members to demand and receive agency records.

# Section 3 Reporting 

The Section 3 reporting requirements of the Epstein Files Transparency Act (EFTA) serve as the "audit trail" for the entire disclosure process.

By failing to submit this report, the Department of Justice (DOJ) has denied survivors the transparency needed to ensure the law is being followed.

Survivors argue that this failure is a direct violation of the Crime Victims' Rights Act (CVRA), which mandates that victims be treated with fairness and dignity.

### The Section 3 Mandate 

DOJ's Failures

According to the statutory text of H.R. 4405, Section 3 requires the Attorney General to provide a detailed accounting of the release process to the House and Senate Judiciary Committees.

## December 19, 2025

The 30-day statutory deadline for the full release of all unclassified records passed.

Under the Act, this date triggered the countdown for the Section 3 report.

## January 3, 2026

The deadline for the Section 3 report expired (15 days after the initial release deadline).

As of late January 2026, the DOJ has failed to submit this report, providing no formal list of withheld categories or redaction justifications.

### The Missing "Vaughn-Style" Accounting

Section 3(2) requires a "summary of redactions made, including legal basis." Without this, survivors argue they cannot challenge improper withholdings, such as the use of "common-law privileges" which the Act does not explicitly permit.

### The Non-Existent PEP List

Section 3(3) mandates a list of all Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) named in the materials with no redactions permitted under the "embarrassment" clause.

Survivors contend the failure to produce this list is a deliberate attempt to shield high-profile enablers.

### Evidence of CVRA "Procedural Harm"

In her January 19 letter, survivor Haley Robson argued that the DOJ’s silence regarding Section 3 constitutes "continued victimization." Under the CVRA, victims have a right to "timely proceedings"; survivors argue that blowing past a transparency deadline is a procedural delay that denies them the "solicitude" they are legally owed.

# Writ of Mandamus

A Writ of Mandamus is an "extraordinary remedy" used to compel a government official to perform a mandatory, non-discretionary duty.

In the DC Circuit, which is the likely venue for a civil challenge against the Department of Justice (DOJ), the legal standard is exceptionally high, requiring plaintiffs to prove there is "no other adequate means" to attain the desired relief.

For survivors like Haley Robson and lawmakers like Ro Khanna, a mandamus petition would argue that the Epstein Files Transparency Act (EFTA) created a "ministerial duty" - a task so plainly prescribed that the Attorney General has no choice but to perform it, specifically regarding the missing Section 3 report.

## Overriding Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 

6(e) Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 6(e) codifies the traditional "veil of secrecy" over grand jury proceedings.

The Epstein Transparency Act overrides this rule through a specific legal mechanism:

### Legislative Intent as a "Special Circumstance"

Typically, a party seeking grand jury records under Rule 6(e) must show a "particularized need" that outweighs the interest in secrecy.

The Act flips this burden

The law presumes disclosure is the default, and the government must prove that a record falls into a narrow category of "Permitted Withholdings" (such as national security or victim privacy) to keep it sealed.
