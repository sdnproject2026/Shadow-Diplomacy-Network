# Events

## January 30, 2026

- The Justice Department releases over 3 million pages of records, marking the final major tranche in compliance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act.

- Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche holds a news conference confirming the release includes more than 2,000 videos and 180,000 images, many of which were seized from Epstein’s personal devices.

- The DOJ implements an age-verification barrier on its website due to the presence of commercial adult and graphic content within the investigative files.

- Blanche confirms that while notable figures and politicians were not redacted, every woman depicted in images and videos - with the exception of Ghislaine Maxwell - has been redacted to protect survivors.

- The DOJ establishes a dedicated email inbox for victims to report any concerns regarding redaction errors.

## December 19, 2025

- The original statutory deadline for the total release of files expires; the DOJ misses this deadline, citing the need to review more than 6 million pages to ensure victim protection.

- The agency releases an initial batch of approximately 100,000 pages, which includes photographs of various public figures, including former President Bill Clinton.

## November 19, 2025

- President Trump signs the Epstein Files Transparency Act (H.R. 1) into law, requiring the public disclosure of all unclassified records related to Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell.

# Search Function

1. The Official DOJ Search Tool

The DOJ has launched a dedicated search page

- https://www.justice.gov/epstein/search

However, using it is often a frustrating experience for a few reasons:

## Technical Limitations

- The DOJ has explicitly noted that because many files are handwritten notes, poor-quality scans, or images, they may not be "electronically searchable" or may yield unreliable results.

## High Traffic & Queues

- Due to the massive interest in today's 3-million-page dump, the site frequently uses a "waiting room" queue system, limiting your time on the site (often to 10-minute windows).

## Formatting Issues

- The documents are often uploaded as massive, generic PDFs (e.g., "Data Set 9") rather than being indexed by name or specific topic, making it hard to find a "needle in a haystack" without knowing exactly which file to open.

2. What is Actually Searchable?

## Text-Based Documents

- Official FBI memos, court transcripts, and DOJ emails that were born-digital are generally searchable if you download them and use a PDF reader's `Ctrl+F` function.

## Non-Searchable Media

- The latest release includes 180,000 images and 2,000 videos.

- These are not searchable by content.

- You have to scroll through them manually.

## Redactions

- A significant portion of the text is "blacked out" to protect victim identities.

- These redacted sections are, by definition, not searchable.

# Alternatives 

## CBS News & Major Outlets

- Many news organizations are maintaining their own indexed databases where they have run OCR (Optical Character Recognition) on the files to make them more searchable than the DOJ’s raw files.

## Independent Repositories

- Platforms like Courier Newsroom and various Internet Archive contributors have consolidated the files into searchable PDFs, organized them by date, and removed the "waiting room" barriers.

## Pro-Tip for Searching

If you are looking for specific names, do not rely solely on the DOJ's web search.

It is much more effective to download the specific data sets you're interested in and use a professional PDF tool (like Adobe Acrobat or even a browser) to search the text locally.

> Note on Accessibility

- If the official DOJ site places you in a "waiting room" queue due to traffic, the Courier Newsroom or Internet Archive mirrors are generally the fastest ways to view the 3.5 million pages without a delay.

# Third-parties

## COURIER Newsroom

- One of the most comprehensive independent repositories.

- Includes a specialized search tool that allows users to filter by names, dates, and keywords.

- Notably provides independent transcriptions of audio files (e.g., conversations between Todd Blanche and Ghislaine Maxwell) to bypass DOJ redactions or poor audio quality.

## CBS News

- Maintains a dedicated interactive database of the tranches released since December 2025. - Focuses on linking photos to documents, making it easier to identify the context of the 180,000+ images released in the latest dump.

## Zeteo

- Features a filtered search system that categorizes documents by People, Organizations, and Locations.

- Highly effective for mapping out Epstein's "vast web of influential figures" in politics and business.

- The Internet Archive (Wayback Machine / Community Collections):

- Archivists have been "mirroring" the DOJ site to prevent data loss (after reports of files disappearing from the official site).

- While less "glossy" than news sites, it provides raw, OCR-processed (Optical Character Recognition) versions of the 3 million pages that can be searched via the site’s internal text search.

## House Oversight Committee Disclosures

- While technically a government source, the Committee's repository often includes documents derived from the Epstein Estate that are not yet fully integrated into the DOJ's public library.

- Commonly used to cross-reference the "Birthday Book" and other personal contact logs.

## Tips for Searching These Databases

### OCR Search

Look for "OCR" or "Text-Searchable" labels.

This means the database has converted images of text into actual searchable data.

# Download and Local Search

For the most reliable results, many researchers recommend downloading the Data Set ZIP files from these mirrors and using a local tool like Adobe Acrobat or Foxit to run a "Full Search" across multiple PDFs at once.
