# Epstein Files Transparency Act

The text in Section 2(a)(4) and the reporting requirements in Section 3 are the most relevant to your request.

## Statutory Text

"...submit to the House and Senate Committees on the Judiciary a report listing:"

- All categories of records released and withheld

- A summary of redactions made, including the legal basis

- A list of all government officials and politically exposed persons (PEPs) named or referenced in the released materials

## Compliance Records

The DOJ hosts these compliance reports and the corresponding data sets.

- https://www.justice.gov/epstein/doj-disclosures

## Status

While the law requires a "list," the DOJ has fulfilled this by releasing the raw documents in 12 "Data Sets" totaling over 3.5 million pages.

### January 30, 2026

Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche stated that the formal summary report containing the specific list of government officials and PEPs would be submitted to Congress within 15 days (by mid-February 2026).

### Searchability

Because the "list" is often embedded within millions of PDFs, transparency groups use the RECAP archive to make the names searchable:

- https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/4355835/giuffre-v-maxwell

## Redactions

Per Section 2 of the Act, the DOJ is permitted to redact information that would "identify victims" or "constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy" for non-public individuals. Consequently, there is no single "master list" of every person mentioned—only the specific list of government officials and PEPs is strictly mandated as a separate summary.
