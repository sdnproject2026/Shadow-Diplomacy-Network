# Special Arizona election

Here is a detailed list of events following the special Arizona election and the failure to swear in the elected official, including the alleged connection of these events to the pending vote to release all Jeffrey Epstein investigative files.

### September–October 2025

### Special Arizona Election Victory of Adelita Grijalva

Adelita Grijalva, a Democrat, won a special election in Arizona, becoming the first Latina elected to Congress from the state.

Despite her victory, she has not been sworn in due to opposition from Republican leadership in the House.

Significant Individuals: Adelita Grijalva, Mike Johnson (House Speaker), Ruben Gallego (Arizona Congressman)

### Early October 2025

### Refusal to Swear in Adelita Grijalva

Republican House Speaker Mike Johnson refused to swear in Adelita Grijalva, citing procedural and political reasons amid a government shutdown.

This refusal has sparked controversy and accusations of political obstruction.

Significant Individuals: Mike Johnson, Ruben Gallego, Raúl Grijalva (Arizona Congressman)

### October 2025

### Allegations of Epstein Cover-Up Linked to Swearing-In Block

Prominent Democrats and media outlets alleged that the refusal to swear in Grijalva is connected to a broader effort to delay or block the release of all Jeffrey Epstein investigative files.

The timing of the refusal coincides with a pending House vote regarding the full disclosure of Epstein-related documents.

Critics argue that blocking Grijalva’s swearing-in is a tactic to maintain Republican control over the vote and suppress potentially damaging information.

Significant Individuals: Mike Johnson, Ruben Gallego, Raúl Grijalva

### October 2025

### Heated Political Confrontations in Congress

Democratic senators and representatives confronted Speaker Johnson over the refusal, framing it as part of a shutdown and obstruction strategy linked to Epstein file disclosures.

The dispute has intensified partisan tensions in Congress, with calls for transparency and accountability.

Significant Individuals: Mike Johnson, Ruben Gallego, Senator Ed Markey
