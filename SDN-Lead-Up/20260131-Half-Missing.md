The statement you provided references a significant legal and political dispute regarding the implementation of the **Epstein Files Transparency Act** (H.R. 4405). This act, signed into law on November 19, 2025, mandated the release of all Department of Justice (DOJ) records concerning Jeffrey Epstein.

The controversy stems from the DOJ’s admission that while it identified over **6 million potentially responsive pages**, it only released approximately **3.5 million pages** to the public by late January 2026. Critics, including Representative Ro Khanna and Representative Thomas Massie, argue that withholding the remaining 2.5 million pages (roughly 41% to 50% of the total cache) constitutes a violation of the law's intent. The DOJ maintains that the unreleased portions consist of duplicate files, child sexual abuse material (CSAM), or documents that would compromise victim privacy or ongoing investigations.

### Timeline of Key Events

* November 19, 2025
President Trump signs the Epstein Files Transparency Act, giving the DOJ 30 days to release all unclassified records.
* December 19, 2025
The original legal deadline for the document release passes with only a small fraction of the files made public.
* January 5, 2026
The DOJ acknowledges in a letter to Congress that it has published only 125,575 pages to date, citing the "sheer volume" of material as the reason for the delay.
* January 21, 2026
House Republicans begin a process to hold various officials in contempt for the slow rollout of the files.
* January 30, 2026
The DOJ releases a massive "Data Set" of 3 million additional pages, bringing the total to 3.5 million, and declares it has met its legal obligations.
* January 31, 2026
Lawmakers and survivors' groups release statements condemning the "missing" millions of pages and the "extreme" redactions found in the released materials.
