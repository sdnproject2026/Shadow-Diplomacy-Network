#  One Million More Documents

The "stunning 11th-hour development" was revealed via a social media post on X, where the DOJ claimed federal prosecutors in Manhattan and the FBI had only recently uncovered the massive cache of records.

## December 24, 2025

###  One Million More Documents

The Justice Department stated that the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of New York (SDNY) and the FBI uncovered over a million more documents potentially related to the Epstein case.

### Release Extended

Officials stated that lawyers are "working around the clock" to review and redact the files, but the sheer volume means the release process may take "a few more weeks," effectively bypassing the original December 19 deadline.

###  Contempt Threats

Representatives Ro Khanna (D-CA) and Thomas Massie (R-KY) threatened to bring inherent contempt charges against Attorney General Pam Bondi, arguing that the department had months to prepare and is now obstructing the law.

###  "Coverup"

Senator Chuck Schumer issued a statement calling the Christmas Eve announcement a "news dump" intended to hide information, questioning why these files were only "suddenly" discovered now.

## December 23, 2025

### 30,000 pages of files

The DOJ released a tranche of approximately 11,000 files (totaling nearly 30,000 pages) including photos, court records, and internal emails.

### Trump’s Flights

A 2020 email from a federal prosecutor revealed that Donald Trump flew on Epstein’s private jet "many more times" than previously reported, citing at least eight flights between 1993 and 1996.

### Untrue And Sensationalist

In an unusual move, the DOJ accompanied the document release with a statement asserting that some files contained false allegations against President Trump submitted to the FBI shortly before the 2020 election.

### "Fake" Epstein Letter

The DOJ and FBI concluded that a 2019 letter purportedly written by Epstein to Larry Nassar, which referenced Trump, was a forgery due to inconsistencies with postmarks and return addresses.

## December 19–21, 2025

### Transparency Act Deadline

The legal deadline for the full release of unclassified Epstein materials passed on Friday, Dec. 19, with the DOJ only releasing a "fraction" of the expected files initially.

### "Disappearing" Files

News outlets reported that at least 15–16 files were briefly uploaded to the DOJ portal and then removed without explanation, including a photo that showed a picture of Donald Trump in a drawer at Epstein's home.

### High-profile Photos

Early tranches included images of Epstein with prominent figures such as Bill Clinton, Michael Jackson, Mick Jagger, and Prince Andrew.
