# Testify Under Oath

In late September 2025, Democrats on the House Oversight Committee compelled Alex Acosta, the former U.S. Attorney who negotiated Jeffrey Epstein’s controversial 2008 plea deal, to testify under oath about his role in the case. The session lasted approximately six hours and was held behind closed doors, with committee members pressing Acosta on his decision-making and the handling of the investigation.

Democrats highlighted significant inconsistencies in Acosta’s testimony, noting that he refused to admit Epstein received a “sweetheart deal” and declined to take responsibility for the impact on Epstein’s victims. Acosta defended his actions, stating that his primary goal was to ensure Epstein was put behind bars, registered as a sexual offender, and that victims could seek restitution. He argued that pursuing a federal trial would have been a “crapshoot” due to challenges with evidence, victim testimony, and jurisdictional issues, and that the negotiated plea was the best available option at the time.

Committee members, including Ranking Member Robert Garcia and other Democrats, expressed skepticism about Acosta’s credibility and lack of remorse. They pointed out that Acosta admitted he had not personally reviewed victim statements and appeared to lack knowledge of key details about the investigation in his own office. Democrats also criticized Acosta for not acknowledging the broader consequences of the plea deal, which allowed Epstein to avoid federal prosecution and reportedly enabled further abuse.

The testimony was part of a broader congressional probe into the Trump administration’s handling of the Epstein case and ongoing efforts to demand transparency. Acosta’s appearance marked a significant moment in the investigation, as lawmakers sought to clarify the extent of the administration’s involvement and accountability in the matter.

Primary-source documents and court filings related to Donald Trump’s indictments - including the classified documents case and the New York business records case - are publicly available from official court and government sources. - All court filings, including indictments, statements of facts, and sentencing orders, are accessible through official court websites such as NYCourts.gov and the U.S. District Court’s public access portal.
