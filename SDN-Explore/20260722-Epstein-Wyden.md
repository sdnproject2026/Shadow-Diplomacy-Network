# Senator Ron Wyden

As of 2026, Senator Ron Wyden has expanded his multi-year investigation beyond suspicious banking transactions into broader questions involving Treasury oversight, IRS enforcement, DEA investigations, money laundering, and the financial relationships between Jeffrey Epstein and billionaire Leon Black. The inquiry has become one of the most extensive congressional financial investigations related to Epstein.

## Timeline

### 2008

Jeffrey Epstein pleaded guilty in Florida.

Financial relationships with numerous wealthy clients continued following his conviction.

### 2012 - 2017

Leon Black paid approximately $158 million to Jeffrey Epstein for tax and financial services.

Wyden later questioned whether these transactions received adequate IRS scrutiny.

### 2019 - 07

Jeffrey Epstein arrested on federal sex trafficking charges.

Banks later submitted Suspicious Activity Reports cited by Senator Wyden.

### 2025 - 07

Ron Wyden disclosed Treasury documents describing:

- nearly 4,725 wire transfers

- approximately $1.1 billion moving through Epstein-controlled accounts

- approximately $1.5 billion in suspicious transactions reported by four financial institutions after Epstein's arrest.

Viral social-media posts falsely claimed the wire transfers implicated Donald Trump directly; fact-checkers concluded Wyden had not made that allegation.

### 2025 - 11

Congress passed the Epstein Files Transparency Act requiring broader public release of DOJ Epstein records.

### 2025 - 12

DOJ released its first tranche of Epstein records.

Members of both parties criticized heavy redactions and missing files.

Digital redaction errors allowed portions of concealed material to be recovered by the public.

### 2026 - 01 - 30

DOJ released more than three million pages of Epstein-related material.

DOJ stated it had substantially complied with the Transparency Act.

Several lawmakers disputed whether all responsive records had been produced.

### 2026 - 02 - 06

Congress received access to review unredacted Epstein files in secure DOJ reading rooms.

Congressional oversight of DOJ implementation expanded.

### 2026 - 02 - 26

Wyden disclosed evidence of a previously undisclosed DEA investigation involving Epstein.

A heavily redacted memorandum suggested investigators examined possible money laundering and drug-trafficking issues.

Wyden requested production of the unredacted investigative records and an explanation of why the investigation ended.

### 2026 - 03 - 23

Wyden continued investigating Leon Black.

New Senate findings referenced:

- alleged hush-money style payments

- surveillance of women

- additional financial records requiring explanation.

Wyden requested detailed responses from Black regarding these findings.

### 2026 - 06 - 04

Wyden formally urged the House Oversight Committee to question Leon Black.

The referral emphasized:

- Black's payments to Epstein

- potential tax implications

- Epstein's apparent role as an intermediary for payments.

CBS News reported the request as an expansion of Wyden's multi-year investigation.

### 2026 - 07

House investigators subpoenaed Leon Black after an unsatisfactory interview.

AP reported Wyden had referred findings from nearly four years of investigation to House investigators.

DOJ leadership continued facing questions regarding the completeness of Epstein document releases.

Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche met Epstein victims amid continuing congressional scrutiny of DOJ handling of the files.
