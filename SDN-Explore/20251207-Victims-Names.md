# Revealed Epstein Victims

Today’s news regarding the release of Epstein-related documents highlights concerns that the Department of Justice (DOJ) revealed the names of dozens of Epstein victims in files sent to Congress and made public, while Trump’s name was redacted and protected.

Many victims believe this was intentional, arguing that the DOJ prioritized shielding Trump over protecting survivors’ privacy.
- [1][2][3]

## July

The DOJ announced it would cease releasing Epstein-related materials after Attorney General Bondi informed Trump that his name appeared in the files, sparking speculation about political interference and selective redaction.
- [8][1]

## November 19

 President Donald Trump signed a bill mandating the DOJ to release all Epstein-related files within 30 days, with provisions allowing for redaction of victims’ information and withholding of sensitive or ongoing investigation materials.
- [2][7]

## Late November

Survivors and advocacy groups criticized the DOJ for revealing their names in released documents, while Trump’s name was consistently redacted, fueling allegations of bias and inadequate victim protection.
- [5][1][2]

## December 3

 Lawmakers from both parties requested a status update from Attorney General Pam Bondi, emphasizing the need for full transparency and privacy protections for survivors as required by the new law.

## December 5

 A judge granted the DOJ’s request to unseal grand jury records related to Epstein, aligning with the new transparency law and increasing pressure for full disclosure.
