## Special Master

### Status 

A request was made for the appointment of a Special Master to provide independent oversight of the Department of Justice’s handling of Jeffrey Epstein-related records. The stated purposes were to ensure DOJ compliance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act, protect victims’ privacy, oversee redactions, and review any disputed withholding of records.

### July 2026

No confirmed public appointment of a Special Master has occurred. DOJ continues to maintain its Epstein-related document release system, while oversight continues through congressional review, litigation efforts, and the Inspector General audit.

### Timeline

July 15, 2025

The Epstein Files Transparency Act was introduced in Congress. The proposed legislation sought public disclosure of unclassified Epstein-related records while requiring protections for victims and sensitive information.

November 19, 2025

The Act became law, establishing DOJ disclosure obligations and procedures for releasing covered Epstein-related materials while protecting legally protected information.

December 19, 2025

The statutory disclosure deadline passed. Questions and disputes emerged regarding the completeness, timing, and scope of DOJ compliance.

Early January 2026

Members of Congress, including Representatives Ro Khanna and Thomas Massie, called for appointment of a Special Master. The request argued that an independent officer was necessary to verify DOJ compliance, evaluate redactions, and ensure victims’ identities were protected.

January 2026

The Department of Justice opposed the Special Master request, arguing that the court lacked authority to impose such oversight in the requested manner and challenging the legal basis for the appointment.

January 30, 2026

DOJ announced a large-scale release of Epstein-related records pursuant to the Transparency Act. DOJ stated that materials had been reviewed and redacted to protect victims and other sensitive information.

2026

The DOJ Office of Inspector General initiated an audit examining DOJ compliance, including document collection, review procedures, redactions, and decisions regarding withheld material.

## Current Status Summary

Special Master Appointment:

- Requested by lawmakers and advocates for independent review.

- Opposed by DOJ.

- No publicly confirmed appointment has been made.

Victim Protection:

- DOJ states that victim-identifying information has been protected through redactions and review procedures.

- Critics continue to argue that independent verification is needed to ensure adequate protection.

DOJ Compliance Review:

- DOJ has released records under the Transparency Act framework.

- The DOJ Inspector General audit remains the primary independent review process identified.

## Accountability Outlook

The central unresolved issue is whether DOJ’s internal review and redaction process is sufficient or whether an independent Special Master should oversee compliance. 

At present, oversight remains divided among DOJ disclosures, congressional scrutiny, and Inspector General review.
