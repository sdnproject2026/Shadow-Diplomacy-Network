Recent news regarding the "missing minute" of video footage from Jeffrey Epstein's jail cell before his death has been clarified by the release of new documents and video clips. 

The key details, including dates and descriptions, are listed below.

## July 2025

the FBI and Department of Justice released nearly 11 hours of surveillance video from the Manhattan Metropolitan Correctional Center, which included footage from the night of Epstein's death on August 9-10, 2019.

## September 3, 2025

The House Oversight Committee released additional documents and video, including the previously missing minute, which revealed guards moving around Epstein's cell during that time.

# Discovered Footage

The newly discovered footage of Epstein's jail cell was released by the US House Oversight Committee on September 2, 2025, as part of a broader release of documents and videos related to Epstein's death.
- [1][2][4]

The public and media reacted with intense scrutiny, as the missing minute had fueled conspiracy theories about foul play in Epstein's death. 

Many news outlets highlighted how the release contradicted earlier claims by Attorney General Pam Bondi, who said the minute was missing due to a routine reset of the prison’s CCTV system.
- [2][5][1]

The newly released footage shows the camera switching over at midnight, with guards moving around Epstein's cell during that time. 

There is nothing unusual visible in the footage, and it does not show any evidence of foul play. 

It also includes Epstein being escorted by guards to make a phone call earlier that evening, which had not been seen before.
- [5][1][2]

The release of the Epstein documents and the missing minute footage has dampened many conspiracy theories, as the video does not reveal any suspicious activity during the previously missing period. 

However, some questions remain about the quality and authenticity of the footage, as experts noted the video may be a screen recording rather than raw footage, and metadata is missing.
- [5]

Attorney General Pam Bondi commented on the 'missing minute' in July 2025, stating it was due to a nightly reset of the prison’s CCTV system. 

The House Oversight Committee’s release on September 2, 2025, directly contradicted her explanation and prompted further public and media discussion.
- [1][2][5]
