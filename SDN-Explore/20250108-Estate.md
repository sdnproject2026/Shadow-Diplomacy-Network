# Jeffrey Epstein Estate 2026

As of January 2026, the Jeffrey Epstein estate is facing significant legal and legislative pressure focused on the release of vast amounts of internal data and the testimony of its executors.

The following are the key actions taken against the estate and its representatives in the current year:

##  Subpoenas of Estate Executors

On January 7, 2026, the House Oversight Committee secured subpoenas for the two co-executors of the Epstein estate, Darren Indyke and Richard Kahn.

Lawmakers are seeking testimony regarding their roles as Epstein's long-time lawyer and accountant, specifically focused on the "movement of funds" and potential enabling of his crimes.

##  Release of Estate Documents

The House Oversight Committee continues to process and release thousands of pages of internal estate records, including cash ledgers, message logs, calendars, and flight logs. 

In late 2025 and early 2026, over 50,000 pages were made public.

##  Epstein Files Transparency Act Compliance

Under this federal law, the Department of Justice (DOJ) was required to release all Epstein-related files by December 19, 2025. 

In January 2026, lawmakers criticized the DOJ for releasing less than 1% of the documents (approximately 12,000 out of over 2 million), leading to calls for a special master to oversee the release and prevent "improper redactions" by the estate or government.

##  Scrutiny of Executor Payments

Democratic and Republican senators have questioned why the DOJ has never interviewed Indyke and Kahn, particularly regarding reports that they moved $6.5 million into a trust for their own benefit months after Epstein's death.

##  Virgin Islands Settlement Follow-up

While the estate reached a $105 million settlement with the U.S. Virgin Islands in 2022, current litigation and hacks of redacted files in early 2026 have brought renewed attention to the details of that settlement, including how the estate facilitated the sale of "Little St. James."

##  Audit Demands

Bipartisan groups of senators have requested that the DOJ’s Acting Inspector General audit the department’s compliance with document releases, specifically looking for records the estate may still be shielding.
